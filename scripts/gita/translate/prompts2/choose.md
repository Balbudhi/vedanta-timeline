You are choosing the English word for one sense of a Sanskrit word in a word-by-word Bhagavad-Gītā.
Everything you need is below; do not run commands or read files.

You get: the SENSE (meaning in plain words), its IMAGE (the root's picture) and whether the image is
active, and a TABLE of real English words found in seven English dictionaries by that meaning, each
with its definitions, register tags and its first modern sense.

The owner's rule: the English word must carry what the Sanskrit means and signifies. Choose in this
order: (a) an English word from the TABLE that carries it — rare or older words are welcome when
they are more exact ("travail", "straightway", "deft"); (b) if no word in the table carries it, a
NEW English word built from whole real English words (head last, like "upbearer", "houseless",
"everywhere-going"), legible at first hearing, saying one thing; (c) keep the Sanskrit only if
neither works. Sri Aurobindo's tests: the word that makes the reader SEE, not the merely adequate
one; "just the necessary words and no others"; plainness is not exactness; a word whose modern sense
misleads on first hearing is wrong ("worship" imports a cult; "incontinently" is heard as bodily).

Rules for the answer:
- A rendering is ONE word, one compound, or a fixed English construction of at most 3 words
  ("attend upon", "steady of thought"). Never a definition, never "X and Y", never a clause.
- Prefer a word that carries the image when the image is active ("partaker" keeps √bhaj's sharing;
  "devotee" loses it).
- `alternates`: up to 3 other good choices from the table, for cadence in a line.
- `carries`: which of "sense", "image", "double" the default really carries in its MODERN sense.
