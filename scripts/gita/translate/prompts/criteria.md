# Criteria (translator, lexicon and reviewer all use these ids)

Sources: the owner's standard (lossless in the line; his word-choice procedure) and Sri
Aurobindo, CWSA: FP = The Future Poetry (vol. 26), LPA = Letters on Poetry and Art (27),
HMF = Hymns to the Mystic Fire (16), ISHA (17), KENA (18), VPS (14). Line numbers are the
plain-text files' (verified 2026-11-09). Where his practice as a free literary translator
differs from this product, the product rule governs and says so.

## M — Meaning (from the Sanskrit)
- **M1 Sanskrit first.** The sense at this occurrence is established from Sanskrit sources: the
  root's Dhātupāṭha artha and Dhātuvṛtti, the kośas, Nirukta/Uṇādi, and the commentators'
  glosses here. A Sanskrit–English dictionary may locate a sense, never decide it (owner).
- **M2 Signification.** The root image and the word's further value are part of its meaning
  ("the Sanscrit word for wolf means radically 'tearing'", VPS 21011). An image is ACTIVE when a
  commentator glosses by it, the root repeats in the passage, or the verse's figure uses it.
  Active signification must be carried in the line (the owner's standard).
- **M3 Double sense.** Where the original combines two meanings in one word, render both
  (HMF 806–808): one English word holding both (preferred); else a properly formed compound;
  else, for two grammatical construals, a second full line. Never a note.
- **M4 Inside the hedge of grammar.** A deeper reading must stay within the word's "clear
  grammatical connotation" (ISHA 11405–11407).
- **M5 The image must live in the English word as used now.** "Derivations are depravations …
  what matters is what the word means" (LPA 23731). An English word whose etymology carries the
  image but whose current sense does not, does not carry it.

## W — Word choice
- **W1 Found by meaning.** Search English by the decided meaning (`english "<meaning>"`, then
  the root image), `define` every candidate. A remembered word is labelled remembered and still
  `define`d (owner: the word is found from the dictionaries, not recalled).
- **W2 The word that makes us see.** Grades: adequate < effective < illuminative < inspired <
  inevitable (FP 10399–10400); "the essential power of the poetic word is to make us see"
  (FP 951). Plainness is not exactness: "Simplicity is not the test" (LPA 5804); an inadequate
  rendering "falsifies" (FP 1057, of the Gītā's equality). Ornament is not exactness either:
  "just the necessary words and no others … the precise colour and nothing more" (LPA 6207).
  Neither plain nor rare is a default; the criterion is: carries meaning and signification whole
  > reads at first hearing > keeps the image > sounds best > fits scripture read aloud.
- **W3 Order of means (owner).** (a) an existing English word — rare, older or archaic words
  are fair game when exact ("if it is in the big dictionary, that is sufficient … worth
  reviving", LPA 23593–23595); (b) two words, a phrase, or an English construction for the
  exact and complete meaning (HMF 795): adjective + "of" + noun for a bahuvrīhi, the absolute
  construction, the Saxon genitive, -less/un-; (c) a new word, "properly formed and not ugly or
  unintelligible" (LPA 23944), of whole real English words, head last, saying one thing where a
  phrase says two (LPA 8588, "halo-moon"); no budget — used whenever it carries more than (a)
  or (b); (d) keep the Sanskrit only when nothing in English carries it.
- **W4 The dictionary is not a licence.** "The dictionary admits many words which poetry refuses
  to admit" (LPA 23453): no pedantic connectives ("wherethrough", LPA 23451), no scholastic Latin
  participles as adjectives (LPA 23668), nothing that is "not English" (LPA 23641).
- **W5 Connotation, separately.** Read the word's modern senses and hear it in the line; reject
  a word whose current association takes over on first hearing ("incontinently", "passing dear",
  "worship" for upāsanā, "devotee" for bhakta where √bhaj's sharing is active).
- **W6 Decide once.** The same sense takes the same English throughout ("It is misleading to
  translate Prâna sometimes by life, sometimes by breath", KENA 5744); register variants of the
  same word are allowed; a different sense is a different row.
- **W7 Not Joyce's liberty.** Invention without these tests "would end in a language like
  Joyce's which is not desirable" (LPA 23595–23597); prose is held stricter than verse (LPA
  23624–23634).

## L — The line
- **L0 Add nothing.** A bare and scrupulously exact rendering is the basis (HMF 761–763). Every
  English content word renders one Sanskrit word of the sentence with that word's own meaning —
  not a commentator's explanation, not an etymology set beside it, not a doctrine. Product rule:
  Aurobindo's own freedom to dilute and add (HMF 772–774) is closed to us; his freedoms of word,
  order, turn and rhythm are open (LPA 6859).
- **L1 Lossless.** The line carries every word's meaning and active signification. A
  loss is a defect unless shown unavoidable after W3 (a)–(d).
- **L2 Grammar.** Form kept: case relation, agent noun as agent noun, participle, voice, mood,
  tense, negation scope (a universal under negation is "any"), bahuvrīhi as a description of its
  holder; the construal's relations and referents.
- **L3 Figures kept.** "The figures are there in the original and one cannot suppress them"
  (LPA 23742): anaphora, chiasmus, litany, a repeated root (same English root, LPA 10068).
- **L4 Rhythm carries the thought.** Rhythm is "the premier necessity … the sound-movement which
  carries on its wave the thought-movement" (FP 902–905); the medium is cadenced prose, which
  reproduces "the exact thought and spiritual intention" without recasting it (FP 5824–5832).
  Each colon ends on a strong content word; no "of"-chains of three; no function word burying
  the key word (LPA 1980); sounds dispersed — no sibilant clusters, no colliding consonants
  (LPA 1984, 23689); sense rules sound (LPA 3917).
- **L5 English.** Parses on first hearing; one register per line; reads as English, as close to
  the original as possible (LPA 6817–6819); the right word in the right place (LPA 717).

## Severity
- **S1 blocks:** an addition (L0); a meaning or active signification lost without a complete
  showing (L1, M2); a grammar error (L2, M4); a sense not supported by the Sanskrit (M1); a word
  whose connotation misleads (W5); a word that is not English (W4); a doctrine or word-swap as a
  second line (M3).
- **S2 must fix:** a flatter or merely adequate word where the evidence shows a more exact or
  more seeing word (W2); a figure dropped (L3); a rhythm or end-weight failure (L4); an
  inconsistency with the lexicon (W6); a candidate not found by meaning (W1).
- **S3 polish:** anything else that would make the line better.
