# Ramakrishna, Śaṅkara, and *Vijñāna Vedānta*: a claim audit

## Question

This note tests a specific contrast in Ayon Maharaj (Swami Medhananda): Śaṅkara allegedly treats the personal God, Śakti, and world as ontologically unreal, whereas Ramakrishna's *vijñāna* treats Brahman and Śakti as equally real and mutually ontologically dependent. The question is not whether Ramakrishna used distinctive language or whether Medhananda's synthesis is spiritually valuable. It is whether the cited Bengali and Sanskrit establish that contrast.

This is an audit of wording and inference. It makes no allegation of mistranslation, and it does not classify either position as “modalism.”

## 1. Ramakrishna's Bengali: what is actually said

In the 22 September 1883 *Kathāmṛta* entry, Ramakrishna says:

> **যিনিই ব্রহ্ম তিনিই শক্তি। … যখন সৃষ্টি, স্থিতি, প্রলয় করেন তখন তাঁকে শক্তি বলে। দুটা আলাদা জিনিস নয়। একই জিনিস।**

> “The very one who is Brahman is Śakti. … When [that one] creates, sustains, and dissolves, [that one] is called Śakti. They are not two separate things. [They are] the same thing.”

The construction **যিনিই … তিনিই** (*jini-i … tini-i*) is a relative–correlative: “the very one who … is that very one who.” The focus particle **-ই** strengthens co-reference. **অভেদ** (*abheda*, Sanskrit *a-* + *bheda*, “non-difference”) and **একই জিনিস** (“the same thing”) likewise deny separateness. The wording supports an identity/non-separation reading. The change in designation—“when [the one] creates … [the one] is called Śakti”—is keyed to activity.

The same corpus gives the *vijñāna* formulation in the roof-and-stairs image: the one reached through *neti neti* is recognized as the one that “has become” living beings and world; the *vijñānī* sees the same material in roof and stairs. This is strong evidence that Ramakrishna presents realization as more than a blank negation of experience. It is not, by itself, a technical determination of the final ontological standing of differentiated forms.

**What these passages establish:** Brahman and Śakti are not two separate things; their distinction is tied to a difference of designation and activity; *vijñāna* includes a positive recognition of allness.

**What they do not state:** that two numerically distinct entities mutually produce, sustain, or give being to one another.

## 2. Medhananda's claim

Medhananda is explicit about the additional step. Having cited Ramakrishna's fire/burning-power, water/wetness, and gem/radiance analogies, he argues that their bidirectionality gives Brahman and Śakti equal ontological status; he then concludes that Ramakrishna teaches “mutual ontological dependence.” He contrasts this with Advaita, where Śakti is dependent on *nirguṇa* Brahman and ultimately unreal.

That is a philosophical proposal, not a translation claim. His rendering “That which is Brahman is also Śakti” is looser than the emphatic Bengali but not false. Nor is “inseparable” an implausible rendering of *abheda*.

The inferential issue is narrower:

| Step | Textual status |
| --- | --- |
| Brahman and Śakti are non-separate / inseparable | Directly supported. |
| The two terms are conceptually reciprocal in Ramakrishna’s analogies | Directly supported. |
| They are equally real *as two aspects of one reality* | A possible metaphysical model; not the Bengali’s wording. |
| They are mutually ontologically dependent | A further philosophical conclusion; not stated and not entailed by non-difference. |

If Brahman and Śakti are genuinely one referent, “mutual dependence” cannot mean the ordinary relation between two distinct beings. It might instead mean that a complete conception of the one Reality cannot abstract from its power. That is conceptual or essential co-implication. Calling it *ontological dependence* requires a further account of what the relata are and what “dependence” means.

Source: [Maharaj, *Infinite Paths to Infinite Reality*, pp. 36–38](/Users/eeshan/Dev/parishishta/materials/medhananda/maharaj_2018_infinite_paths_to_infinite_reality.txt:1785).

## 3. Śaṅkara: the positive and negative controls

### A. Worship and knowledge — *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya* 1.1.12

Śaṅkara says that Brahman is apprehended as **dvirūpam**: “in two forms/modes of teaching,” qualified by name, form, modification, difference, and limiting adjuncts; and as the contrary, free from every adjunct. He then says that, in the condition of ignorance (**avidyāvasthāyām**), all transaction involving Brahman as worshipped and worshipper occurs.

This establishes a hierarchy: the worshipper–worshipped relation is not the final nondual account. It does not say that the worshipped Brahman is a metaphysical nonentity. The passage repeatedly speaks of one supreme Self, lordship, powers, and forms within the teaching of worship. “Ontologically unreal” is therefore an interpretive compression of this hierarchy, not the passage’s literal conclusion.

Source: [*Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya* 1.1.12](/Users/eeshan/Dev/vedanta-timeline/data/sources/sanskrit/vedanta/shankara_brahma_sutra_bhasya.txt:337).

### B. Name and form — *Chāndogya-bhāṣya* 6.3.2

Śaṅkara’s crucial formulation is:

> *sarvaṃ ca nāmarūpādi sadātmanaiva satyaṃ; vikārajātaṃ svatas tv anṛtam eva.*

> “All name-and-form and so on is true only with Sat as its Self; the class of modifications is untrue in its own right.”

The grammar determines the asymmetry.

- **sadātmanā** is instrumental singular, *sat-ātmanā*: “with/by virtue of Sat as its Self.” Name and form have truth only in that qualification.
- **svatas** means “of itself, in its own right.”
- Śaṅkara restates the conclusion as **sato ’nyatvena anṛtatvam**: falsity “in the condition of being other than Sat.”

This directly blocks the crude thesis that Śaṅkara treats phenomenal content as simply nothing. But it also blocks the opposite thesis that form, as form, is ultimately real on its own terms. The text gives non-otherness, not a second positive *vastu*.

Source: [*Chāndogya Upaniṣad Bhāṣya* 6.3.2](/Users/eeshan/Dev/vedanta-timeline/data/sources/sanskrit/vedic/chandogya_upanisad_gretil.txt:6901).

### C. The hard limit — *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya* 2.1.14

On *tad-ananyatvam*, Śaṅkara defines the effect’s **ananyatva** (“non-otherness”) from its cause as its absence apart from that cause:

> *kāraṇāt paramārthato ’nanyatvaṃ vyatirekeṇābhāvaḥ kāryasya.*

In the same discussion he says that no modification exists “in the manner of a thing” (*na tu vastuvṛttena vikāro nāma kaścid asti*), and rejects the opponent who treats unity and plurality as co-true like ocean and waves. His conclusion is: **ekatvam evaikaṃ pāramārthikam**—unity alone is ultimate; plurality is an expansion of false cognition.

This is not a temporary instruction to beginners. It prevents attributing to Śaṅkara a final ontology in which differentiated manifestation is a co-ultimate feature of Brahman. It does not require a physical disappearance of appearances; it denies their independent or coordinate ultimate reality.

Source: [*Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya* 2.1.14](/Users/eeshan/Dev/vedanta-timeline/data/sources/sanskrit/vedanta/shankara_brahma_sutra_bhasya.txt:2424).

### D. *Vijñāna* is not merely conceptual

At *Gītā* 7.2, Śaṅkara glosses **sa-vijñānam** as knowledge joined to one’s own direct experience (**svānubhava-yuktam**). At 9.1 he again calls it knowledge joined with experience and identifies its content with *Vāsudevaḥ sarvam*, *ātmaivedaṃ sarvam*, and *ekam evādvitīyam*. Thus it is inaccurate to represent Śaṅkarian complete knowledge as an empty state that knows only an abstract self-nature.

This does not prove that Śaṅkara gives Ramakrishna’s particular roof-and-stairs pedagogy or a positive Śakti ontology. It establishes the more limited point: Śaṅkara’s *jñāna/vijñāna* is direct realization of allness under the condition that allness is not a second reality alongside Brahman.

Source: [*Bhagavad-gītā-bhāṣya* 7.2 and 9.1](/Users/eeshan/Dev/vedanta-timeline/data/sources/sanskrit/vedanta/shankara_gita_bhasya.txt:4945).

## Finding

The primary evidence does not support a simple opposition between Śaṅkara’s empty or world-negating realization and Ramakrishna’s world-inclusive *vijñāna*. Śaṅkara explicitly grants name and form truth as Sat-selfhood and describes completed knowledge as direct experiential allness. Ramakrishna’s positive recognition of divinity in the world can therefore be read as a devotional and pedagogical expression compatible with that non-otherness.

The evidence also does not establish that Śaṅkara teaches Ramakrishna’s distinct language of Śakti, or that plurality and unity are equally ultimate. Śaṅkara’s secure commentaries deny precisely that. Hence the justified conclusion is narrower than either polemic: Medhananda’s sharp contrast over-compresses Śaṅkara, while a full historical identification of Śaṅkara with a positive Śakti-manifestation doctrine goes beyond the cited Sanskrit.

The remaining constructive proposal—Brahman and Śakti as one non-objectifiable Reality named according to stillness and manifestation—is a coherent modern interpretation. It should be presented as such, not as something decided by a translation or simply asserted by Śaṅkara.
