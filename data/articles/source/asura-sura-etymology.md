# *Asura* and *Sura*: The Traditional Derivations, and What Underwrites the "Harmony" Gloss

*An investigation into Swami Chinmayananda's gloss that* sura *is connected with* sur *("harmony, being in tune") and that* asura *therefore means "the inharmonious" — checked against Yāska's* Nirukta, *the Dhātupāṭha, the Aṣṭādhyāyī and the Uṇādi-sūtras, the Sanskrit lexicographical tradition (Śabdakalpadruma, Vācaspatyam, Apte, Monier-Williams), Śaṅkara's bhāṣyas, and the Ṛgvedic record.*

---

## The short answer

The tradition does **not** derive *sura* from *svara* ("tone, accent"). But nearly every other component of the gloss turns out to have a real traditional basis, and the picture is considerably richer than a simple "folk etymology" verdict allows:

1. **The grammarians derive *sura* regularly, from a root meaning "to shine / to be lordly."** There is a genuine Dhātupāṭha root **सुर्** (*ṣura*, *aiśvarya-dīptyoḥ*), and Śabdakalpadruma derives *sura* from it by an ordinary Pāṇinian kṛt-sūtra, **A 3.1.135**. *Sura* = "the shining one."
2. **They derive *asura* the same way.** Śabdakalpadruma and Vācaspatyam both derive *asura* from √*as* **in the sense of *dīpti*, "to shine"** — and both give **"the sun"** as a meaning of *asura*. In the grammatical tradition the two words are not antonyms; they are near-synonyms by derivation.
3. **Yāska already pairs them — but not as a privative.** *Nirukta* 3.8 derives both together from the ablatives *soḥ* / *asoḥ*: same *-ra*, contrasted stems *su* / *asu*. The pairing is ancient; the reading of *a-* as a negation is a *different*, later analysis, listed second.
4. **The *svara* entanglement is real and documented three separate ways** in the lexica — including Śabdakalpadruma listing *svaraḥ* outright as a sense of *sura*.
5. **The strongest support is not etymological at all.** It is Chāndogya adhyāya 1 and Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3, where the devas defeat the asuras **by singing**, and where the śruti *names prāṇa "svara"* and says the devas **became immortal by entering svara**. Read against that, "the asura is the one out of tune" is a compression of the *udgītha-brāhmaṇa*, not a pun.

What is *not* traditional is the specific morphological claim — that Sanskrit *sura* comes from *svara* "tone." That one does not survive. Everything else does better than expected.

## I. What the grammarians actually derive

### I.1 *Nirukta* 3.8 — Yāska's four analyses, and the paired derivation

The oldest etymological authority in the tradition treats *asura* at **Nirukta 3.8**, commenting on RV 10.53.4 (*tad adya vācaḥ prathamaṃ manisīya | yena asurān abhi devā asāma*). Yāska gives **four** analyses in succession:

> **असुरा असुरताः स्थानेषु, अस्ता स्थानेभ्य इति वा ।**
> *asurā(ḥ) a-suratā(ḥ) sthāneṣu, astā(ḥ) sthānebhya iti vā*
> — "*asuras*: **not well-settled** (*a-su-ratāḥ*) in their stations; **or**: **hurled** (*astāḥ*) from their stations."

> **अपि वा असुर् इति प्राणनाम, अस्तः शरीरे भवति तेन तद्वन्तः ।**
> *api vā asur iti prāṇa-nāma, astaḥ śarīre bhavati tena tadvantaḥ*
> — "**Or**: *asu* is a **name for *prāṇa***; it is deposited (*astaḥ*) in the body; they are possessed of it."

> **सोर् देवान् असृजत तत् सुराणां सुरत्वम्, असोर् असुरान् असृजत तद् असुराणाम् असुरत्वम् इति विज्ञायते ।**
> *soḥ devān asṛjata tat surāṇāṃ suratvam, asor asurān asṛjata tad asurāṇām asuratvam iti vijñāyate*
> — "**From *su*** [the *praśasta*, the excellent part of himself] **he emitted the devas: that is the *sura*-hood of the *sura*s. From *asu* he emitted the *asura*s: that is the *asura*-hood of the *asura*s.** So it is taught."
>
> — Yāska, *Nirukta* 3.8 (`data/sources/sanskrit/grammar/yaska_nirukta_gretil.txt`, §`3,8`)

Four things follow, and they matter.

**(a) These are Yāska's, not a lexicographer's report.** The previous version of this article quoted analyses 1, 2, 3 and 4 from Apte with the note that the *Nirukta* was not on disk and the citations unlocated. All four are now verified in the primary text at 3.8, in Yāska's own order. Apte was transmitting Yāska accurately, and the fourth analysis — which Apte gave without any attribution at all, and which looked like a stray Brāhmaṇa etiology — is Yāska's too.

**(b) Analysis 1 is the closest classical relative of the Chinmayananda gloss.** *A-su-ratāḥ sthāneṣu* splits the word as *a-* + *su-* + *ratāḥ*: "not **well**-settled." The *su-* here is the ordinary prefix "well, good," not the noun *sura*. Translate the sense Yāska is after — unsettled, not properly seated, off its proper station — and "out of true" is a short step. This is a *nirvacana* of *asura* as **being-not-well-ordered**, from the tradition's founding etymologist.

**(c) Analysis 4 pairs the two words, and it is not a privative.** Yāska derives *sura* and *asura* **together**, from the ablatives *soḥ* and *asoḥ* — the same *-ra* formant on two contrasted stems, *su* ("the excellent") and *asu*. This is important against the standard Indological account (§VI): whatever the historical facts, **the tradition has treated *sura* and *asura* as a derivational pair since Yāska**, and it did so *without* reading the *a-* as *nañ*. The pairing is ancient; the privative reading is a separate, later option.

**(d) Analysis 3 is the source of Śaṅkara's gloss.** *Asu* = *prāṇa*, "they are possessed of it." Śaṅkara's *asuṣu ramaṇāt* (§V) is this analysis turned into a moral psychology.

### I.2 *Nirukta* 10.34 — *asu* as *prajñā*, and *asuratva* of the gods

Yāska returns to the word at **10.34**, glossing the refrain of RV 3.55 (*mahad devānām asuratvam ekam*):

> **महच् च अस्मै देवानाम् असुरत्वम् एकम्, प्रजावत्त्वं वा ऽनवत्त्वं वा । अपि वा असुर् इति प्रज्ञानाम । अस्यत्य् अनर्थान् । अस्ताश् च अस्यां अर्थाः । असुरत्वम् आदिलुप्तम् ।**
>
> — "'Great and one is the *asura*-hood of the gods for him' — [i.e.] having offspring, or … Or: ***asu* is a name for *prajñā* (insight)**. It casts away (*asyati*) what is meaningless; and meanings are deposited (*astāḥ*) in it. *Asuratvam* is [a form] **with its beginning elided**."
> (`yaska_nirukta_gretil.txt`, §`10,34`)

So Yāska's reading of *asura* in the honorific Vedic sense is **"the one possessed of insight."** This confirms the *Naighaṇṭuka* equation *asu* = *prajñā* that Monier-Williams cites and that was previously uncheckable here.

### I.3 There is a real Dhātupāṭha root *sur*, and it means "to shine"

This is the datum that changes the picture. The Dhātupāṭha contains:

> **षुर~ ऐश्वर्यदीप्त्योः** — *ṣura~ aiśvarya-dīptyoḥ*, **6.66** (*tudādi*, parasmaipada, akarmaka, seṭ; forms *surati*)
> — "**in the senses of *aiśvarya* (lordship) and *dīpti* (radiance).**"
> (vidyut Dhātupāṭha `06.0066`; cross-checked against the Kṛṣṇācārya Dhātupāṭha in `sanskrit_parser`:
> `{"DAtuH":"sur","mUlaDAtuH":"zura","DAtvarTaH":"ESvarya-dIptyoH","gaRaH":"tudAdiH","rUpam":"surati"}`)

Both dictionaries carry it:

> `sur … cl. 6 P. surati, to rule, possess supreme or superhuman power, Dhātup. xxviii, 50; to shine, ib.` — Monier-Williams, s.v. `sur`
> `sur I. 6 P. (surati) 1 To rule, govern. 2 To shine.` — Apte, s.v. `sur`

And Durgādāsa, commenting on Vopadeva's *Kavikalpadruma*, glosses it identically — quoted in Śabdakalpadruma directly above its *sura* entry:

> **सुर, ष भैष्ययोः । इति कविकल्पद्रुमः ॥ (तुदा०-पर०-अक०-सेट्।) दन्त्यादिः । … भा, दीप्तिः । ऐश्यम् ऐश्वर्य्यम् । इति दुर्गादासः ॥**
> — "…*bhā* = *dīptiḥ* (radiance); *aiśyam* = *aiśvaryam* (lordship). Thus Durgādāsa."
> (Śabdakalpadruma, s.v. `sura`)

### I.4 *Sura* is a regular Pāṇinian *kṛdanta*: √*sur* + *ka* by A 3.1.135

Śabdakalpadruma gives three derivations of *suraḥ*, and **the second is the one that matters**:

> **सुरः, पुं, (सुष्ठु राति ददात्यभीष्टमिति । रा + कः । यद्वा, सुरति शोभते इति । सुर + "इगुपधे"ति कः । यद्वा, सुनोतीति । षुञ् अभिषवे + "सुसूधाञ्गृधिभ्यः क्रन्" उणा० २।२४ । क्रन्।) देवः । इत्यमरः । १।१।७ ॥**
>
> 1. *suṣṭhu rāti dadāty abhīṣṭam iti | rā + kaḥ* — "he who **gives well** what is desired": *su* + √*rā* + *ka*.
> 2. ***yadvā, surati śobhate iti | sura + "igupadhe"ti kaḥ*** — "**or: *surati*, i.e. HE SHINES** — from [the root] *sur* with ***ka*** by [the sūtra] ***igupadha…***"
> 3. *yadvā, sunotīti | ṣuñ abhiṣave + "susūdhāñ-gṛdhibhyaḥ kran" uṇā 2.24* — "or: *sunoti*, from √*ṣuñ* 'to press [soma]', Uṇādi 2.24, *kran*."
>
> "…**a god**. So Amara [*Amarakośa* 1.1.7]."
> (Śabdakalpadruma, s.v. `suraH`)

The sūtra invoked in derivation 2 is real, and mainstream:

> **इगुपधज्ञाप्रीकिरः कः**
> *igupadha-jñā-prī-kiraḥ kaḥ* — **Aṣṭādhyāyī 3.1.135**
> (verified: `data/sources/sanskrit/grammar/panini_astadhyayi_gretil.txt`, `PS_3,1.135`)

"[The kṛt affix] ***ka*** [comes] after a root having an *ik* vowel as its penultimate (*igupadha*), and after √*jñā*, √*prī*, √*kṝ*." The root **सुर्** has *u* — an *ik* vowel — as its *upadhā*. It therefore takes *ka*, giving **सुर**.

This is worth being clear about, because it inverts the previous verdict. *Sura* is **not** confined to the Uṇādi-sūtras, the tradition's holding pen for irregular forms. It has a **regular derivation by a mainstream kṛt-sūtra of the Aṣṭādhyāyī**, from a root the Dhātupāṭha glosses as *aiśvarya* and *dīpti*. On this analysis:

> ***sura* = "the shining one / the lordly one"** — the exact semantic twin of *deva* from √*div*, *dyotana*.

The Cologne *Śabda-sāgara* records the same: "or *ṣur* to be radiant, *ka* affix."

### I.5 *Asura*: which √*as*? The lexica answer *dīptau*, "to shine"

The previous version of this article left open the one point where MW (√*as* 2, "to throw") and Apte (*asu*, "breath") diverged on Uṇādi 1.42 *aser uran*. The two great Sanskrit-to-Sanskrit lexica settle it, and they agree with each other:

> **असुर, पुं, स्त्री, (अस्यति देवान् क्षिपति इति । असं + उरन् । यद्वा न सुरः विरोधे नञ्तत्पुरुषः । यद्वा नास्ति सुरा यस्य सः । सूर्य्यपक्षे असति दीप्यते इति उरन् ।)**
>
> 1. *asyati devān kṣipati iti | asaṃ + uran* — "he **hurls** the gods" (√*as*, Uṇādi 1.42)
> 2. *yadvā na suraḥ | virodhe nañ-tatpuruṣaḥ* — "or: **not-*sura***, a *nañ-tatpuruṣa* in the sense of opposition"
> 3. *yadvā nāsti surā yasya saḥ* — "or: he who has no *surā* (liquor)"
> 4. ***sūrya-pakṣe: asati dīpyate iti uran*** — "**in the sense of *the sun*: *asati*, i.e. HE SHINES**, with *uran*"
>
> — Śabdakalpadruma, s.v. `asuraH`. It then adds: **सूर्य्यः । इति मेदिनी** — "**the sun**. So the *Medinī*[-kośa]."

Vācaspatyam gives the same, and puts the shining sense **first**:

> **असुर पु० अस—दीप्तौ उर० । १ सूर्य्ये । अस्यति क्षिपति देवान् उर विरोधे नञ् त० वा । सुरविरोधिनि २ दैत्ये ।**
> — "*asura*, masc.: from **√*as* in the sense of *dīpti* (shining)** + *ura* — (1) **the SUN**. [Or] *asyati*, 'hurls', the gods — *ura* — or as a *nañ-tatpuruṣa* in the sense of opposition — (2) the daitya, opposed to the *sura*s."
> — Vācaspatyam, s.v. `asura`

### I.6 The upshot: in the grammatical tradition they are not opposites

Set the two entries side by side:

| | root | sense of the root | affix | authority | a meaning given |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ***sura*** | √**सुर्** *ṣura* | *aiśvarya-**dīptyoḥ*** — lordship, **radiance** | *ka* | **A 3.1.135** (regular kṛt) | *deva*, **the sun**, sage |
| ***asura*** | √**अस्** *as* | ***dīptau*** — **shining** | *uran* | Uṇādi 1.42 | **the sun**, spirit, demon |

**Both words are derived from roots meaning "to shine," and both are given "the sun" as a lexical meaning.** The privative reading (*na suraḥ, virodhe nañ-tatpuruṣaḥ*) is listed by Śabdakalpadruma as derivation **2 of 4** — real, recognised, but neither primary nor the only one on offer.

This is the substantive correction to the earlier draft, which presented the tradition as deriving the two words from unrelated roots and treating the opposition as a late artefact. The tradition does something more interesting: it derives them from *convergent* roots and treats the opposition as **one construal among several**.

## II. Pāṇini's own evidence

A scan of all 3,951 sūtras of the Aṣṭādhyāyī returns exactly two hits on the string *sura*:

- **A 4.4.123 — असुरस्य स्वम्** (*asurasya svam*) — "[the affix *yat*] after *asura* in the sense 'belonging to the asura'." The Kāśikā's example is Vedic and accented: *asuryàṃ vā etat pātraṃ yat kulālakṛtam cakravṛttam* (Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā 1.8.3).
- **A 5.4.135 — गन्धस्येदुत्पूतिसुसुरभिभ्यः** — here *surabhi*, not *sura*.

Plus **A 4.4.124 मायायामण्** (*māyāyām aṇ*), immediately following, giving *āsurī* specifically in the sense of *māyā*: Kāśikā cites *āsurī māyā svadhayā kṛtāsi* (Vājasaneyi-Saṃhitā 11.69).

So Pāṇini composed **a dedicated sūtra pair for *asura*** and **never mentions *sura*** as a stem. Two further data:

**(a) *Asura* is a warrior-clan name.** At **A 5.3.117 पर्श्वादियौधेयादिभ्यामणञौ**, the Kāśikā's *parśvādi* gaṇa — *āyudhajīvi-saṅgha*, "confederations that live by arms" — reads: *parśu. **asura**. rakṣas. bāhlīka. vayas. marut. daśārha. piśāca. viśāla. aśani. kārṣāpaṇa. satvat. vasu.* MW records the same: `a/sura m. pl. N. of a warrior-tribe (g. parśv-ādi)`. In the grammarians' world *asura* names **a people**, listed beside *marut* and *satvat*.

**(b) The word itself undergoes r → l.** The vārttika on **A 8.2.18 कृपो रो लः**: *vāla-mūla-laghv-**asur**-ālam-aṅgulīnāṃ vā ro lam āpadyate* — "optionally *r* becomes *l* in *vāla*, *mūla*, *laghu*, ***asura***, *alam*, *aṅguli*." Hence **असुरः / असुलः**. This returns in §III.3.

## III. The *svara* entanglement — documented three ways

No traditional source derives *sura* from *svara* "tone." But the two words are tangled in the transmitted texts at three distinct levels, and the tangle is recorded by the tradition's own lexicographers.

### III.1 The lexica list *svara* as a **sense** of *sura*

Monier-Williams ends his *sura* entry with the flat note **`w.r. for svara`** — *sura* occurs as a **wrong reading for *svara*** in the Mahābhārata. Śabdakalpadruma goes further and lists it as a meaning outright, with the locus:

> **(स्वरः । यथा, महाभारते । १३ । ८५ । ९१ । "लक्षणानि सुरास्तोमा निरुक्तं सुरपङ्क्तयः ॥")**
> — "**[*sura* =] *svara*.** As in the Mahābhārata 13.85.91: '*lakṣaṇāni surāstomā niruktaṃ surapaṅktayaḥ*.'"
> (Śabdakalpadruma, s.v. `suraH`)

Look at what the verse is *about*: *lakṣaṇāni* … *niruktam* … *paṅktayaḥ* — a list of the Vedāṅga disciplines. The expected reading is *svarāstomāḥ … svarapaṅktayaḥ*, "the **accent**-stomas … the **accent**-series." The manuscript tradition wobbles between *sura* and *svara* **precisely in a verse about Vedic accent and recitation**. (Flagged as *reported*: the Mahābhārata is not on disk here and this reading is Śabdakalpadruma's.)

### III.2 The **roots** are variants of each other in the Dhātupāṭha

> `sur … cl. 10 P. surayati, to find fault (**v.l. for svar**), [Dhātup.] xxxv, 11` — MW, s.v. `sur`
> `sur … **II. 10 P. = svar** q. v.` — Apte, s.v. `sur`

In the tenth class the Dhātupāṭha manuscripts themselves cannot keep √*sur* and √*svar* apart, and Apte simply equates them. (Precision matters here: this class-10 √*svar* is *ākṣepe*, "to blame," not the √*svṛ* *śabdopatāpayoḥ* — Dhātupāṭha 1.1081 — that yields *svara* "sound." The variance is documented; it is not by itself a sound-etymology.)

### III.3 The grammarians' own trope: catastrophe by being out of tune

Patañjali's *Paspaśāhnika*, the introduction to the *Mahābhāṣya*, lists the reasons one must study grammar. Two consecutive items:

> **[Pas_4.1]** *te **asurāḥ** helayaḥ helayaḥ iti kurvantaḥ parā babhūvuḥ | tasmāt brāhmaṇena na mlecchitavai na apabhāṣitavai …*
>
> "Those **asuras**, saying '*he 'layaḥ, he 'layaḥ*' [for *he 'rayaḥ*, 'O foes'], **were defeated**. Therefore a brahmin must not speak barbarously…"

> **[Pas_4.2]** *duṣṭaḥ śabdaḥ **svarataḥ** varṇato vā mithyā prayukto na tam artham āha | sa vāg-vajro yajamānaṃ hinasti yathā **indraśatruḥ svarato 'parādhāt**.*
>
> "A corrupt word, wrongly employed **with respect to pitch-accent (*svara*)** or with respect to phoneme, does not convey its meaning. That **word-thunderbolt** destroys the sacrificer — as *indraśatruḥ* did, **through an error of *svara***."
> (`data/sources/sanskrit/grammar/patanjali_mahabhasya_gretil.txt`, `[Pas_4.1]`, `[Pas_4.2]`)

The asuras are destroyed **by defective speech**; the very next item says defective speech is defective **in *svara***, the paradigm case being a mispronunciation that destroyed its speaker. And the beneficiary of the *indraśatru* error was Vṛtra — whom **Yāska identifies as an asura**:

> *tat ko vṛtraḥ? megha iti nairuktāḥ, **tvāṣṭro 'sura** ity aitihāsikāḥ* — "Who is Vṛtra? A cloud, say the etymologists; **the asura son of Tvaṣṭṛ**, say the historians."
> (*Nirukta* 2.16)

So the archetypal asura is, in the grammarians' most-read passage, *the being produced by an error of svara*.

A final loop: the asuras' specific error was substituting **l for r** — and the vārttika on A 8.2.18 (§II.b) lists **असुर** itself among the words in which *r* optionally becomes *l*. The word for the people undone by saying *l* for *r* is itself a word that says *l* for *r*.

None of this is an etymology. But **"the asura is the one whose speech is out of true, who errs in *svara*"** is a thing the vyākaraṇa tradition genuinely says.

## IV. The strongest support: the *udgītha-brāhmaṇa*

This is where the gloss is on its firmest ground, and it is not lexical at all. In **Chāndogya adhyāya 1** and **Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3**, the deva/asura conflict is decided **by singing**, and the devas are defined as **those who entered *svara***.

### IV.1 The war is a singing contest

> *devāsurā ha vai yatra saṃyetire | ubhaye prājāpatyās tad dha devā **udgītham** ājahrur anenainān abhibhaviṣyāma iti*
> — "When the devas and asuras contended — both being descendants of Prajāpati — the devas **took up the udgītha [the sung portion of the Sāman]**, thinking: 'by this we shall overcome them.'"
> — Chāndogya 1.2.1

> *te ha devā ūcur hantāsurān yajña **udgīthenātyayāma** iti*
> — "The devas said: 'Come, let us overcome the asuras in the sacrifice **by means of the udgītha**.'"
> — Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3.1

The asuras pierce each sense-*prāṇa* with *pāpman* (evil) in turn; but when they attack the breath in the mouth — the one that sings —

> *taṃ hāsurā ṛtvā vidadhvaṃsur yathāśmānam ākhaṇam ṛtvā vidhvaṃsetaivam*
> — "the asuras, striking against it, were shattered — as a clod is shattered striking against a rock."
> — Chāndogya 1.2.7; cf. BṛhU 1.3.7, *tato devā abhavan, parāsurā*, "then they became devas, and the asuras were defeated"

Śaṅkara's comment on the opening line gives both etymologies at once:

> *devā **dīvyater dyotanārthasya** śāstrodbhāsitā indriya-vṛttayaḥ | asurās tad-viparītāḥ **sveṣv evāsuṣu** viṣvag-viṣayāsu prāṇana-kriyāsu **ramaṇāt** svābhāvikyas tama-ātmikā indriya-vṛttaya eva*
>
> — "The **devas** are the sense-functions **illumined by scripture** — from √*div* in the sense of **shining**. The **asuras** are their opposite: the natural, darkness-natured sense-functions themselves, **from revelling (*ramaṇa*) in their own *asu*s**, i.e. in the outward-turned, object-directed breathing-activities."
> — Śaṅkara ad Chāndogya 1.2.1
> (`chandogya_upanisad_gretil.txt`; the GRETIL file reads *aśirāstadvirītāḥ*, an OCR corruption of *asurās tad-viparītāḥ*)

### IV.2 The śruti *names* prāṇa "svara"

Two sections later, the Chāndogya gives the breath a name:

> ***svara** itīmam ācakṣate, **svara** iti **pratyāsvara** ity amum*
> — "**This one** [the breath] they call ***svara***; and **that one** [the sun] ***pratyāsvara***."
> — Chāndogya 1.3.2
>
> Śaṅkara: *yasmāt prāṇaḥ **svaraty** eva na punar mṛtaḥ pratyāgacchati* — "because the breath **goes forth / sounds** (*svarati*) and does not return once departed; the sun, having set, returns day after day — hence *pratyāsvara*."

### IV.3 The devas become immortal **by entering svara**

> *te nu vittvordhvā ṛcaḥ sāmno yajuṣaḥ **svaram eva prāviśan***
> — "Having understood [death's intent], the devas rose up out of the Ṛc, the Sāman and the Yajus, and **entered *svara* itself**."
> — Chāndogya 1.4.3
>
> Śaṅkara: *tad apāsyāmṛtābhaya-guṇam akṣaraṃ **svaraṃ svara-śabditam** prāviśann eva* — "abandoning that, they entered the imperishable, **called *svara***, whose qualities are immortality and fearlessness."

> *eṣa u **svaro** yad etad akṣaram etad **amṛtam abhayam** | **tat praviśya devā amṛtā abhavan***
> — "This is the ***svara***: this imperishable syllable [Om], **immortal, fearless**. **Entering it, the devas became immortal.**"
> — Chāndogya 1.4.4; and 1.4.5, *etad evākṣaraṃ **svaram amṛtam abhayaṃ** praviśati*

> *om iti hy eṣa **svarann** eti* — "for this one goes **sounding** 'Om'." Śaṅkara: *svarann uccārayan* (√*svṛ*, "to utter").
> — Chāndogya 1.5.1

And when the udgītha is traced to its root in the Śilaka/Dālbhya dialogue:

> *svara iti hovāca | svarasya kā gatir iti | prāṇa iti hovāca | prāṇa-niṣpādyo hi **svaras** tasmāt svarasya prāṇo gatiḥ*
> — "'***Svara***,' he said. — 'And what is the goal of *svara*?' — '***Prāṇa***,' he said. For *svara* is produced by *prāṇa*; therefore *prāṇa* is the goal of *svara*."
> — Chāndogya 1.8–9 with Śaṅkara

### IV.4 *Svara* is the wealth and the gold of the Sāman

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka, in the same chapter as the deva/asura war, ends its analysis of the Sāman like this:

> *tasya vai **svara eva svam** | tasmād ārtvijyaṃ kariṣyan vāci **svaram iccheta** | tayā vācā **svarasampannayā** rtvijyaṃ kuryāt | tasmād yajñe **svaravantaṃ** didṛkṣanta eva*
>
> — "Of that [Sāman], ***svara* alone is its own wealth**. Therefore one who is about to perform priestly office **should seek *svara* in his speech**. With that speech **endowed with *svara*** he should perform the office. Therefore at the sacrifice people desire to see **the one who has *svara***."
> — Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3.25
>
> Śaṅkara: ***svara** iti kaṇṭha-gataṃ **mādhuryam** tad evāsya svaṃ vibhūṣaṇam* — "***Svara*** means the **sweetness residing in the throat**; that alone is its own ornament."

> *tasya vai **svara eva suvarṇam*** — "Of that, ***svara* alone is its gold**."
> — Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3.26
>
> Śaṅkara notes the wordplay explicitly: *suvarṇa-śabda-sāmānyāt **svara-suvarṇayoḥ*** — "from the commonality of the word *suvarṇa* [su-varṇa, 'good phoneme' / 'gold'] between *svara* and gold."

And immediately before this (1.3.24) stands Brahmadatta Caikitāneya's oath: **let my head fall off** if the udgātṛ sang by any deity other than speech-and-breath. Singing wrongly is, again, fatal — the *Mahābhāṣya*'s *vāg-vajra* in śruti form.

### IV.5 What this adds up to

*Prāṇa* is the hinge, and both words hang from it:

- *Prāṇa* rightly deployed **is** *svara*, **is** the Sāman, **is** the udgītha — the devas' weapon against the asuras (ChU 1.2.1, BṛhU 1.3.1), their refuge from death (ChU 1.4.3–5), the wealth and gold of the chant (BṛhU 1.3.25–26).
- *Prāṇa* turned to its own outward gratification is precisely Śaṅkara's ***asura***: *sveṣv evāsuṣu … ramaṇāt*.

So **"being in svara" versus "revelling in one's own asus" is the deva/asura distinction**, stated in śruti and in Śaṅkara, with no pun required. Chinmayananda taught the Chāndogya and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka; the *udgītha-brāhmaṇa* is standard Vedānta curriculum. His gloss reads as a **homiletic compression of this material** — and as such it is well-founded. It is only when it is restated as a *morphological* claim about *sura* < *svara* that it fails.

## V. Śaṅkara: the psychological reading, which *is* traditional

### V.1 Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.3.1 — Śaṅkara's nirvacana

On *dvayā ha prājāpatyā devāś cāsurāś ca*, Śaṅkara asks *kathaṃ punas teṣāṃ devāsuratvam?* and answers:

> *śāstra-janita-jñāna-karma-bhāvitā **dyodanāt** devā bhavanti | ta eva svābhāvika-pratyakṣānumāna-janita-dṛṣṭa-prayojana-karma-jñāna-bhāvitā **asurāḥ** | **sveṣv evāsuṣu ramaṇāt**, **surebhyo vā devebhyo 'nyatvāt**.*
>
> "Suffused with the knowledge and action born of scripture, they are *devas*, **from shining**. Those very same, suffused with natural, perception- and inference-born knowledge and action aimed at visible ends, are the *asuras* — **because they revel in their own *asu*s**, **or because they are other than the *sura*s, the devas**."

His **primary** gloss is *asuṣu ramaṇāt* — Yāska's third analysis (§I.1) turned into moral psychology. His **secondary** gloss, *surebhyo vā devebhyo 'nyatvāt*, does record the *a-sura* reading. It is in the tradition, as an option, in the most authoritative Advaitic commentary there is. Śaṅkara reuses the idea at Gītā 9.3, calling such people *asu-tṛpaḥ*, "gratifiers of the vital breath."

The referents are *the prāṇas — the sense-powers themselves*. The war is between two modes of one person's own faculties.

### V.2 The war is in every body, beginninglessly

> *anyonyābhibhavodbhava-rūpaḥ saṃgrāma iva sarva-prāṇiṣu **pratideham** devāsura-saṅgrāmo **'nādi-kāla-pravṛtta** ity abhiprāyaḥ.*
> — "The purport is that this deva-asura war, in the form of the mutual overpowering and rising of each, goes on **in every body**, in all living beings, having commenced **from beginningless time**."
> — Śaṅkara ad Chāndogya 1.2.1

### V.3 Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.2.3 — the strongest statement

> *athavā **na devā asurā vānye kecana vidyante manuṣyebhyaḥ** | manuṣyāṇām evādāntā ye 'nyair uttamair guṇaiḥ saṃpannās te devāḥ, lobha-pradhānā manuṣyāḥ, tathā hiṃsā-parāḥ krūrā asurāḥ | ta eva manuṣyā … itarāṃś ca guṇān **sattva-rajas-tamāṃsy** apekṣya.*
>
> "Or rather: **there are no devas or asuras whatever apart from human beings.** Among humans, those endowed with excellent qualities are the devas; those in whom greed predominates are the humans; those bent on violence, the cruel, are the asuras — … by reference to **sattva, rajas, and tamas**."

This is the whole modern pedagogical reading — *devas and asuras are guṇa-indexed dispositions within the human being* — stated by Śaṅkara. **Chinmayananda's substantive teaching is orthodox and citable verbatim.**

### V.4 Two more loci

- **Īśa Upaniṣad 3**, *asuryā nāma te lokāḥ*, with Śaṅkara: *paramātma-bhāvam advayam apekṣya **devādayo 'py asurāḥ*** — "with reference to the non-dual state of the supreme Self, **even the gods are asuras**." The category is relative.
- **Chāndogya 8.8.5**, *asurāṇāṃ hy eṣopaniṣat* — "this is the *upaniṣad* of the asuras": that the body is the self. Śaṅkara cites it at Gītā 9.3. The *āsura* position is defined **doctrinally** — materialism — not by temperament alone.

## VI. The historical layer, and how it sits with the tradition

The Ṛgvedic record is unchanged by any of the above, and it is worth keeping separate from the traditional analysis rather than played against it.

**(a) *Asura* is frequent, ancient, and overwhelmingly honorific.**

> *kṣáyann asmábhyam **ásura** prácetā rā́jann énāṁsi śiśrathaḥ kṛtā́ni* — RV 1.24.14 (Varuṇa)
> *hiraṇyahasto **ásuraḥ** sunīthaḥ sumṛḷīkaḥ svávām̐ yātv arvā́ṅ* — RV 1.35.10 (Savitṛ)
> *arvā́ṅ eténa stanayitnúnehy apó niṣiñcánn **ásuraḥ** pitā́ naḥ* — RV 5.83.6 (Parjanya)

The pejorative sense exists but is later and explicitly marked: *anāyudhā́so **ásurā** **ádevāś*** (RV 8.96.9), "weaponless asuras, **godless**." **The Ṛgvedic opposition is *asura* vs. *deva*, never *asura* vs. *sura*.**

**(b) *Asuratva* is a property of the devas.** The refrain of **RV 3.55**, in all twenty-two verses (verified: 22 occurrences):

> ***mahád devā́nām ásuratvam ékam*** — "Great and one is the **asura-hood of the gods**."

Beside it *asurya* in the same positive sense: *ná vā́ u yoṣad rudrā́d **asuryàm*** (RV 2.33.9), "nor does **asura-lordship** depart from Rudra." Yāska glosses this very refrain at *Nirukta* 10.34 (§I.2), and takes *asuratva* as a **virtue**.

**(c) *Sura* does not occur in the Ṛgveda.** A full tokenization of all *sur-* forms yields **zero** occurrences of *sura* meaning "god." Every *sur-* token is a *su-* compound (*su-ratha*, *su-rādhas*, *su-rūpa*, *su-raṇa*) or **surā́**, the liquor (RV 1.116.7). The word for god is *deva*.

**(d) The Indological conclusion, which MW states in the dictionary:**

> `sura m. (prob. fr. asura as if fr. a-sura, and as sita fr. a-sita; thought by some to be connected with 2. svar) a god, divinity`
> `a/sura [In later Sanskṛt sura has been formed from asura, as sita from asita, q.v.]`

*Sura* is a **back-formation**: later speakers re-analysed the *a-* of *asura* as privative *nañ* and extracted a positive counterpart that had never existed — as *sita* "white" was manufactured from *asita* "black."

**How this sits with §I.** These two accounts are not in conflict; they answer different questions. Historically, *asura* is primary and *sura* is secondary. Traditionally, the grammarians — who were not doing historical linguistics — derived *sura* independently and regularly from √*sur* "to shine" (A 3.1.135), and Yāska paired the two words from *su*/*asu* without any privative at all. **The popular gloss goes wrong not by pairing them — Yāska pairs them — but by making *sura* the base and *asura* its negation.** On the historical account that is backwards; on Yāska's account the shared element is *-ra* and the contrast is *su*/*asu*, not *sura*/*a-sura*; and on the Pāṇinian account of §I.4–I.6 they are two independently derived words that both mean "the shining one."

**(e) The tradition's own account of how *sura* arose.** The churning-of-the-ocean etiology, quoted in full by Śabdakalpadruma with attribution:

> ***surāḥ** pratigrahād devāḥ **surā** ity abhiviśrutāḥ | apratigrahaṇāt tasya daiteyāś ca **asurāḥ** smṛtāḥ*
> — "From accepting *surā* (liquor), the gods became renowned as ***sura*s**; and from not accepting it, the Daiteyas are remembered as ***asura*s**." — *iti rāmāyaṇe*
> (Śabdakalpadruma, s.v. `asuraH`; Apte cites the same verse s.v. `suraH`, reading *surā-pratigrahād*)

A folk etymology, but a revealing one: it is the tradition reaching for an explanation of why there is a word *sura* at all, and reaching for one that derives **both** from *surā*, the liquor — rather than treating *sura* as basic and *asura* as its negation.

## VII. Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the tradition derive *sura* from *svara* ("tone, harmony")? | **No.** No grammarian, etymologist or lexicographer does this. *Svara* is from √*svṛ* *śabdopatāpayoḥ* (Dhātup. 1.1081); *sura* is from √*sur* *aiśvarya-dīptyoḥ* (Dhātup. 6.66) or *su*+√*rā*. |
| Then is *sura* just an Uṇādi oddity with no real derivation? | **No — and this is the correction.** Śabdakalpadruma derives it by a **regular Pāṇinian kṛt-sūtra, A 3.1.135 *igupadhajñāprīkiraḥ kaḥ***, from √*sur* "to shine, to be lordly": ***sura* = "the shining one."** Semantic twin of *deva* < *dyotana*. |
| What is the traditional derivation of *asura*? | Four are on record. Śabdakalpadruma and Vācaspatyam both give √*as* ***dīptau*** ("to shine") + *uran*, with **"the sun"** as the first meaning; also *asyati devān* ("hurls the gods"); also *na suraḥ* (*nañ-tatpuruṣa*); also "he who has no *surā*." Yāska adds *asu* = *prāṇa* and *asu* = *prajñā*. |
| So are they opposites in the grammatical tradition? | **No.** Both are derived from roots meaning "to shine," and both are given "the sun" as a meaning. The privative reading is one construal among four. |
| Does the tradition ever pair the two words? | **Yes, from Yāska on** — but as *su*/*asu* + *-ra* (*Nirukta* 3.8), **not** as *sura*/*a-sura*. |
| Does the tradition read *asura* as "not in a good state"? | **Yes** — *Nirukta* 3.8: *a-su-ratāḥ sthāneṣu*, "not **well**-settled in their stations." The closest classical relative of the gloss. |
| Is there a traditional link between asura-hood and being out of tune? | **Yes, and it is strong — but narrative, not etymological.** ChU 1.2 / BṛhU 1.3: the devas defeat the asuras **by the udgītha**. ChU 1.3.2: the śruti **names prāṇa "svara."** ChU 1.4.4: **"entering *svara*, the devas became immortal."** BṛhU 1.3.25–26: ***svara* is the wealth and the gold of the Sāman.** *Mahābhāṣya Paspaśā*: the asuras are destroyed by corrupt speech, and a *svara*-error is a thunderbolt that kills. |
| Is the *sura*/*svara* confusion itself attested? | **Yes, twice in the lexica.** MW: *sura* is a "w.r. for *svara*"; Śabdakalpadruma lists *svaraḥ* as a **sense** of *sura*, citing MBh 13.85.91 — a verse **about Vedic accent**. And MW/Apte record √*sur* (cl. 10) as a **variant of √*svar***. |
| What is the historically correct account? | ***Asura* is primary** — an honorific of Varuṇa, Savitṛ, Parjanya, with *asuratva* predicated of the devas at RV 3.55 twenty-two times. ***Sura* is a back-formation** from it (MW), *sura* being absent from the Ṛgveda entirely. |

**The honest summary.** Chinmayananda's *substance* — that *deva* and *asura* name two orientations of one person's own faculties, guṇa-indexed, at war in every body beginninglessly — is squarely traditional and quotable verbatim from Śaṅkara. His *framing* — that the deva is the one in tune and the asura the one out of tune — is a defensible compression of the **udgītha-brāhmaṇa**, where the devas win by song, are named for entering *svara*, and where *svara* is the Sāman's wealth and gold; and it has a real classical relative in Yāska's *a-su-ratāḥ*, "not well-settled." What does **not** hold is the narrow morphological claim that Sanskrit *sura* derives from *svara* "tone." The grammarians derive it from √*sur*, "to shine" — which yields, not "the one in tune," but something a Vedāntin should like at least as well: **"the radiant one."**
