A dependent individual can discover a Self that is not bound by the conditions of individuality. Forms can be real without being separate from what manifests them. Realization can remove an error about identity while leaving a world, an embodied life and devotion. These are substantive philosophical proposals. A mathematical treatment should explain their structure before judging whether different traditions accept the same proposal.

This article develops a programme for doing that. It assumes familiarity with Indian philosophy and ordinary mathematical reasoning, but no category theory. The mathematics begins with simple examples and builds toward comparing theories. The school studies then distinguish what their texts argue, what has already been mathematically reconstructed, and which connections remain to be established. The integrated Vedānta proposed here is a hypothesis being developed through those comparisons.

## What is being identified

Consider the inscriptions “5”, “V”, and “five.” They differ as inscriptions, while in the appropriate languages they refer to the same number. An objection that counts three inscriptions has not answered a claim about their common referent. Equally, writing a function that sends all three inscriptions to one target does not establish that the target is the number five. Their meaning supplies that warrant.

Something similar is at stake in self-language, with an additional difficulty: the Self is not merely another object of reference. What makes a presentation mine, what manifests experience, and what I correctly recognize myself to be have to be investigated. A causal diagram in which a person and a stone both depend on Brahman leaves those questions unanswered.

The relevant distinctions are therefore between an embodied individual, a mental episode, the first-person character of that episode, and the Self recognized through instruction or realization. Whether the last is one universal Self, a distinct irreducible knower, or an indwelling Self inseparable from dependent knowers is the subject of the comparison. It should not be decided by the notation before the arguments have been read.

Vijñānabhikṣu makes an especially strong move: a teaching about the supreme Self can discriminate even from the jīva and establish the supreme alone as the final Self. He nevertheless preserves real individuals. The correction concerns what has ultimate selfhood; it does not simply erase everything previously discovered about individual existence. Rāmānuja and Jīva Gosvāmin preserve first-person individuality differently. Shared descriptions of conscious nature do not, for them, exhaust what individuates a knower. Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika supplies an explicit theory of ultimate differentiators. These are rival explanations of individuation, not rival ways of counting an already agreed collection. [Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/vijnanamrta-bhasya/category-bhiksu-self) [Source](cite://ramanuja/vedartha-samgraha/category-ramanuja) [Source](cite://jiva/sat-sandarbha/category-jiva-love) [Source](cite://sridhara/nyaya-kandali/category-nyaya-self)

A useful preliminary notation is SelfOf(c, x, j): “in the warranted context c, x is recognized as the Self of the presented individual j.” This relation does not decide the philosophy. Each theory must explain what it means and what it entails. In particular, SelfOf(c, B, j) must not automatically license substituting B for j in every predicate concerning j's changing body and mind. An Advaitic identity judgment requires corrected meanings; a body–indweller account preserves a different structure; Bhikṣu's final self-ascription preserves another. The mathematics must retain these distinctions long enough to test them.

## Levels are several different distinctions

“Different levels” can name at least four things. An apparent object can be corrected by a more authoritative cognition. A bodily self-description can be corrected by discrimination of the conscious subject. A theory can speak about dependent manifestations without treating them as independent realities. A practitioner can pass from mental quiet to understanding and then to an embodied expression of that understanding.

These distinctions concern validity, reference, dependence and practice respectively. They need not form one simple ladder. A quiet mind is not automatically a more adequate metaphysics. A dependent being need not be bound. A correctly perceived pot does not guarantee correct self-understanding. The richer models below keep those facts separate.

This makes the realism question more precise. “Real” can assert that something is independent, that it has a dependent reality, that a judgment about it is valid in ordinary transactions, or that its difference remains ultimately established. These claims have different consequences. Śaṅkara's affirmation of the truth of transactions and modifications as Being is therefore important: their truth is not exhausted by the alternative between independent substances and sheer nothing. His rejection of ultimately real dual form also matters. An integrated account must preserve both passages rather than discard whichever obstructs an attractive slogan. [Source](cite://shankara/brhadaranyaka-bhashya/category-fullness-commentary)

> sadātmanā sarvavyavahārāṇāṃ sarvavikārāṇāṃ ca satyatvaṃ sato ’nyatve ca anṛtatvam iti

Truth of all transactions and of all modifications as the Self that is Being; and untruth in being other than Being—thus. [Source](cite://shankara/chandogya-bhashya/category-6.3.2-sadatmana)

The institutional witness uses a locative construction for “in being other than Being”; the held GRETIL transcription uses an instrumental. These readings have not been silently combined.


Calling every Advaita “realist” therefore leaves an argument unfinished until the relevant meaning of reality is specified. Conversely, calling appearance “nonexistent” loses the distinction between an experienced, correctable appearance and something incapable of such presentation. Madhusūdana's analysis of falsity supplies especially exact tests of that distinction.

## Fullness without depletion

> oṃ pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idaṃ pūrṇāt pūrṇam udacyate
> pūrṇasya pūrṇam ādāya pūrṇam evāvaśiṣyate
> oṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ

Oṃ. That is full; this is full. From the full, the full is brought forth. Having taken the full of the full, the full alone is left. Oṃ. Peace, peace, peace. [Source](cite://upanishads/isha/category-purnam-invocation)

Pūrṇāt is “from the full”; pūrṇasya is “of the full”; ādāya is “having taken.” The grammar does not by itself prescribe arithmetic subtraction. This invocation is transmitted with the Īśā Upaniṣad; it is not one of its eighteen numbered verses.


The invocation places the philosophical burden clearly: this too is full, and manifestation does not leave the source diminished. A finite heap loses something when a part is removed. Fullness cannot simply be modeled on that operation. Infinite cardinal arithmetic can illustrate a different possibility, but an infinite set equinumerous with a proper subset does not thereby explain consciousness, manifestation or realization.

There are consequently three separate questions. In what respect is each manifestation the whole? Which differentiations remain meaningful? What changes when its fullness is recognized? The article's later closure construction gives one precise answer to the first question while keeping determinate roles distinct. It does not settle the other questions by itself.

Śaṅkara's commentary locates fullness in the manifestation's nature as the supreme Self, rather than in its limiting particularity as such. Abhinavagupta supplies a different, more articulated argument about constitutive inclusion and complete apprehension. Aurobindo makes undiminished being and its power of determinate manifestation central to his account. Their shared attraction to fullness is a reason to compare the arguments closely; it is not yet a proof that their explanatory commitments are identical. [Source](cite://shankara/brhadaranyaka-bhashya/category-fullness-commentary) [Source](cite://abhinavagupta/paratrimsika-vivarana/category-trika-fullness) [Source](cite://aurobindo/life-divine/category-aurobindo)

## Śiva and Śakti as inseparable reality and power

The fire analogy is useful because burning power is not a second independently constituted thing subsequently attached to fire. In the selected Vijñānabhairava passage, power and possessor are nonseparate, while their explanatory roles remain distinguishable: power also provides the route by which the possessor is known. [Source](cite://vijnana-bhairava/vijnana-bhairava/category-shakti-context)

> na vahner dāhikā śaktir vyatiriktā vibhāvyate

The burning power is not conceived as separate from fire. [Source](cite://vijnana-bhairava/vijnana-bhairava/19ab)


Utpaladeva adds a stronger argument. Manifestation without reflexive awareness would resemble an inert crystal: something might appear without the conscious self-apprehension needed to explain experience. His account connects that intrinsic reflexivity with freedom and agency. Limited mental reflection and complete reflexivity must consequently be distinguished. An ordinary episode is conditioned by its medium and restricted access; complete reflexivity is not a second finite mind placed opposite a first. [Source](cite://utpaladeva/isvara-pratyabhijna-karika/category-trika-reflexivity) [Source](cite://ksemaraja/pratyabhijna-hrdaya/category-ksemaraja)

This supports the proposal that identity and manifestation are distinguishable aspects of nonseparate reality. It does not establish a universal linguistic equation in which Sanskrit masculine words mean identity and feminine words mean dependent modality. Kṣemarāja calls feminine citi free and the cause of the universe. The philosophical work must come from the author's account of consciousness and power, including the deliberate force of its language, rather than grammatical gender alone. Nor can classical Sāṅkhya's Puruṣa–Prakṛti duality simply be renamed Śiva–Śakti: the relation between conscious subject and nature is precisely what would need a defended translation. [Source](cite://utpaladeva/isvara-pratyabhijna-karika/category-trika-reflexivity) [Source](cite://ksemaraja/pratyabhijna-hrdaya/category-ksemaraja)

Ramakrishna's Brahman–Śakti language likewise supports one reality described in stillness and activity. Swami Medhananda explicitly recognizes aspects of one divine reality; his expression “mutual ontological dependence” should not be treated as if he asserted two independent substances. The substantive question is whether dependence captures the relation adequately, or whether an account of intrinsically possessed power explains it better. Aurobindo's treatment of Brahman–Māyā, Puruṣa–Prakṛti and Īśvara–Śakti gives a developed precedent for that comparison. [Source](cite://ramakrishna/kathamrita/category-ramakrishna) [Source](cite://medhananda/infinite-paths-to-infinite-reality/category-medhananda) [Source](cite://aurobindo/life-divine/category-aurobindo)

## Praxis changes the interpretation of the theory

> tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam

Then: the seer’s abiding in its own nature. [Source](cite://patanjali/yoga-sutra/1.3)


The Yoga sequence links cessation of mental activity with the seer's abiding in its own nature, and contrasts this with conformity to mental activities. The conscious seer is not produced by the cessation. But the mind is on the seen side of this account: “the mind recognizes itself” can obscure the very distinction the teaching seeks to establish. Sleep, temporary quiet and discriminative realization must also remain distinct. [Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/yoga-varttika/category-yoga-reflection)

> evaṃ tattvābhyāsān nāsmi na me nāham ity apariśeṣam
> aviparyayād viśuddhaṃ kevalam utpadyate jñānam

Thus, from practice of the principles, remainderless, pure through non-error, unmixed knowledge arises: “I am not; not mine; not I”. [Source](cite://isvarakrsna/sankhya-karika/64)


Māṭhara makes the scope of Sāṅkhya Kārikā64’s three elliptical negations explicit: they reject identification with the principles, ownership of them, and belonging to them. These are three differently directed relations. They do not announce the annihilation of the conscious subject. The same work says that puruṣa is not intrinsically bound while preserving a meaningful account of realization and the bodily apparatus continuing after knowledge until its final cessation. This is close to the proposed distinction between conditioned life and unconditioned selfhood. Its separate argument for many puruṣas must nevertheless be retained and examined. [Source](cite://mathara/mathara-vrtti/category-mathara-negations)

Taking realization seriously does not make testimony and reasoning dispensable. For Śaṅkara, instruction corrects self-misidentification without presenting the Self as a newly encountered external object; immediacy of self-knowledge is therefore distinguished from ordinary sensory perception of an object. Maṇḍana and Sureśvara investigate how instruction, contemplation and immediate knowledge are related; their disagreement cannot be reduced to experience versus words. Reasoning tests what a teaching licenses, while disciplined practice can address obstacles to its realization. Nor is every resulting state evidence of the same attainment: Yoga's distinction between the seer and mental modifications, and Gauḍapāda's distinction between sleep and knowledge, prevent temporary quiet from certifying a metaphysics. These are author-specific relations among teaching, argument and realization, not one evidential rule for every darśana. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection) [Source](cite://mandana/brahma-siddhi/category-mandana) [Source](cite://suresvara/brhadaranyaka-bhashya-varttika/category-suresvara) [Source](cite://gaudapada/mandukya-karika/category-gaudapada) [Source](cite://patanjali/yoga-sutra/1.3)

A lexical distinction also matters: the inspected Dhātupāṭha assigns *vid* to knowing, *dṛś* to seeing and *śru* to hearing. It does not warrant the literal equation “Veda means seen.” Seeing/hearing vocabulary alone cannot establish a common ontology or identical realization across schools. This is a limited lexical distinction, not a full derivation of the nouns. [Source](cite://panini/dhatupatha/category-knowing-seeing-hearing)

A mathematical starting point is to distinguish current contents from retained misidentifications. Removing the first need not remove the second. A realization path therefore needs both a description of experience and a warrant for correction. Nyāya adds contemplation, inquiry and discussion to its own path; it cannot fairly be excluded as merely abstract logic. What remains distinctive is its account of cognition, the self's qualities and final liberation. [Source](cite://sridhara/nyaya-kandali/category-nyaya-self)

## Devotion as fulfilled expression

> satyapi bhedāpagame nātha tavāhaṃ na māmakīnas tvam
> sāmudro hi taraṅgaḥ kvacana samudro na tāraṅgaḥ

Even with difference gone, Lord, I am yours; you are not mine. For the wave is the ocean’s; the ocean is not the wave’s anywhere. [Source](cite://shankara/vishnu-shatpadi/category-3)

Traditionally attributed to Śaṅkara; its reception in Advaita is distinguished from secure historical authorship.


The verse grants disappearance of difference while retaining asymmetric belonging. Its precise claim is “I am yours,” not an explicit verb “I serve.” Historical attribution to Śaṅkara and its place in received Advaita devotion are different questions; the latter remains philosophically relevant.

Madhusūdana supplies a connected developmental account: the Bhaktirasāyana places nondual knowledge within devotional maturation and describes increased love thereafter. He also distinguishes knowledge and devotion in their constitutive features. That requires at least four distinctions: an occurrence of knowledge, its retained corrective result, a devotional disposition, and a particular act of devotional awareness. A subsequent devotional act can retain the correction without being the same kind of occurrence as the original knowledge. [Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti)

Ramaṇa's highest devotion as abidance, Ramakrishna's retained devotional self-presentation, and Vivekananda's fulfilled worship offer related but distinct accounts. None should be reduced to an unexplained flag saying “devotion continues.” The questions are what the devotion is directed toward, what self-understanding it expresses, and why the activity no longer binds. [Source](cite://ramana/upadesa-saram/category-ramana) [Source](cite://ramana/arunachala-pancharatnam/category-ramana-pancharatnam) [Source](cite://ramakrishna/kathamrita/category-ramakrishna) [Source](cite://vivekananda/complete-works/category-vivekananda)

These distinctions give category theory something definite to preserve. We now begin with its basic operations, then use them to examine the arguments more closely.

## Learning to ask a categorical question

Before asking whether the traditions fit together in a topos, we need to learn what the proposed fit would say. What has been preserved? Which distinctions remain visible? What would count as a counterexample? Category theory helps us ask these questions precisely. It does not decide the answers in advance.

Our mathematical route follows the progression of Eugenia Cheng's *The Joy of Abstraction*: construct a situation we understand, notice the relationships that matter, then find a definition that captures their behavior. We will use original examples drawn from the work of interpreting philosophical texts. These examples teach the mathematics; the source-based reconstructions elsewhere in this article must separately justify its application to a school. Cheng's relevant chapters here are 8 and 14–19. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

There will be three distinct mathematical settings: a tiny research workflow, sets of records and functions between them, and an ordering of selected commitments. Keeping these settings distinct is part of the lesson. An arrow can mean a procedure in one category and an implication in another. It cannot quietly change its meaning halfway through an argument.

### Objects and arrows: what is being related?

Imagine that we track three kinds of research dossier:

- R: a dossier containing a passage and its source reference.
- G: a dossier that also contains a grammatical analysis.
- I: a dossier that also contains an interpretive argument.

For this deliberately simplified workflow, allow one named step g from R to G and one named step i from G to I. These are formal steps in our example, not claims that grammatical or philosophical interpretation has a unique correct procedure.

```
R ──g──→ G ──i──→ I
```

Reading an arrow starts with its endpoints. The notation g: R → G says that R is its source and G its target. We can follow g by i because the first target matches the second source. We call the combined arrow i ∘ g: R → I. Read the expression from right to left: first g, then i.

We cannot follow i by g in this diagram. The output type of i is I, whereas g expects R. Merely writing g ∘ i does not make it meaningful. This is the simplest form of type-checking, and it is already philosophically useful. A claim about a cognition, a claim about its subject, and a claim about the referent of a sentence need not have interchangeable types.

Every object must also have an identity arrow. The identity 1_R: R → R leaves the formal dossier exactly as it is. It is not the claim that a researcher has performed a psychological act of self-recognition. “Identity arrow” is a mathematical term; we must not smuggle a doctrine of the Self into it.

We have now made a category with three objects and six arrows:

```
1_R, 1_G, 1_I, g, i, i ∘ g
```

The identities and the composite are present even when a diagram does not display them. Composition with an identity changes nothing. If we extend the workflow by j: I → J, grouping three successive steps in either way must give the same arrow:

```
j ∘ (i ∘ g) = (j ∘ i) ∘ g.
```

This is associativity. It does not say we may reorder the steps. Parentheses can move; the sequence cannot.

A **category** consists of objects, arrows with specified sources and targets, identity arrows, and a composition operation for every composable pair. Composition must be associative and satisfy the identity laws f ∘ 1_A = f = 1_B ∘ f for f: A → B. The collection of arrows A → B is written C(A,B), or Hom_C(A,B). It can contain no arrows, one arrow, or many.

A category is **small** when its objects and arrows form sets. Our finite examples are small. A category is **locally small** when each individual C(A,B) is a set, even if its entire collection of objects is too large to be a set. The category of all sets is locally small but not small in the usual foundations. We keep this distinction because later constructions use a small category of contexts; calling everything a set would create foundational problems rather than solve them.

Our workflow is the **free category** on the displayed directed graph: its arrows are finite paths, including the empty paths that serve as identities. We impose no extra equations between paths. Concatenating paths is associative, so the construction satisfies the laws.

**Try it.** Add a direct step d: R → I. Must d equal i ∘ g because both start at R and finish at I?

**Answer.** No. They are two parallel arrows. We could impose d = i ∘ g if we had a reason to count those procedures as the same, but that is extra information. A triangle whose direct route equals its two-step route is a **commutative diagram**. Drawing a triangle does not prove commutativity. In a philosophical reconstruction, the equation would need an argument about what the arrows preserve and what their equality means.

This is our first protection against an empty formalization. A diagram becomes informative when it specifies compositions, equations, or their failure. A page of philosophical nouns joined by unexplained arrows does not yet do that work.

### Two more categories, and why the arrows matter

The category **Set** has sets as objects and functions as arrows. A function sends every element of its source to exactly one element of its target. Composition is ordinary function composition. Identities send each element to itself.

Let E = {e₁,e₂,e₃} be three distinct report records. Let M = {quiet,active} be a set of two editorial tags. Define t: E → M by

```
t(e₁) = quiet,  t(e₂) = quiet,  t(e₃) = active.
```

These are tags assigned to records, not a classification of consciousness itself. The function deliberately omits most of each report. This makes it useful for organizing an archive and unsuitable for recovering the complete reports.

An object of Set is the whole set E, not an individual element e₁. We can represent an element categorically by a function from a singleton set 1 = {∗}: the function ∗ ↦ e₁ picks out that element. Later, maps from other objects will let us examine more elaborate families of elements.

Our third example starts with three selected commitments p, q, and r. Consider every subset of {p,q,r}. Put exactly one arrow U → V when U ⊆ V. For example,

```
{p} → {p,q} → {p,q,r}.
```

The composite exists because inclusion is transitive; identities exist because every subset includes itself. There is at most one arrow between any two objects. Such a category is called **thin**. Here the underlying order is a partially ordered set, or **poset**: inclusion is reflexive, transitive, and antisymmetric.

This category records adding selected commitments. It does not decide whether the commitments are true, compatible, or well sourced. If we were ordering actual theories instead, logical consequence could matter: adding an axiom that was already provable would not strengthen the theory. That would require a more considered definition of the ordering.

**Try it.** In this inclusion category, two different paths lead from {p} to {p,q,r}, one through {p,q}, another through {p,r}. Are their composites equal?

**Answer.** Yes. There is only one arrow with those endpoints. Compare the free workflow category, where distinct paths stayed distinct unless we imposed an equation. Choosing a thin category has already discarded distinctions between ways of reaching a result. That can be appropriate when we study implication; it can be inappropriate when we study different arguments or paths of practice.

### Isomorphism: sameness with a reversible comparison

Suppose the three records are copied into a second catalog using new identifiers a, b, and c. The function f sends e₁ to a, e₂ to b, and e₃ to c. There is an inverse g that sends each new identifier back to its corresponding record. Both round trips recover the starting element.

An arrow f: A → B is an **isomorphism** if there is an arrow g: B → A such that

```
g ∘ f = 1_A,     f ∘ g = 1_B.
```

We write A ≅ B when some such isomorphism exists. In Set, the isomorphisms are precisely the bijections: functions that pair every source element with exactly one target element and leave no target element unpaired.

Now try to reverse the tagging function t. We can define s: M → E by s(quiet) = e₁ and s(active) = e₃. Tagging the selected record returns the original tag, so t ∘ s = 1_M. But s ∘ t is not 1_E: it sends e₂ to e₁. The second report has been lost.

This is a particularly important failed attempt. A map can admit a return journey that works on one side while failing on the other. A common description of two records does not establish that the records were identical, or even that the description retained everything relevant about them.

Your example of different occurrences of the number five raises precisely the need to distinguish several questions. Two written inscriptions can be distinct tokens while referring to the same number. Two five-element sets can be isomorphic as sets while having different elements. Those are different mathematical statements. Neither by itself settles how an Advaitin identity statement refers to the Self. That depends on the text's account of reference, limiting conditions, and what is being denied or affirmed. Category theory gives us several kinds of comparison; it does not supply the correct interpretation merely by replacing an equals sign with ≅.

Isomorphism also depends on the category. Take two labels x and y. In one poset they are incomparable; in another x < y. The identity-on-labels function from the first poset to the second preserves order, since the first has no nontrivial order to violate. Its inverse as a set-function does not preserve order. The two structures are bijective as sets but this map is not an isomorphism of posets. Cheng's official errata correct an overgeneralization about this point in the book. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

**Try it.** If an arrow has an inverse, could it have two different inverses g and h?

**Answer.** No. Both inverse laws force

```
g = g ∘ 1_B = g ∘ (f ∘ h)
  = (g ∘ f) ∘ h = 1_A ∘ h = h.
```

The inverse of a specified isomorphism is unique. There can still be many different isomorphisms between the same two objects. Matching three unstructured records to three labels offers six bijections. To select the philosophically appropriate correspondence, we need additional structure and evidence.

### Monomorphisms and epimorphisms: detecting information loss

The tagging map t merges two inputs. The inclusion of the quiet records Q = {e₁,e₂} into E merges none, but misses e₃. These are two different ways a function can fail to be invertible.

A function is **injective** when f(a) = f(a′) implies a = a′. It is **surjective** when every element of its target is the image of some source element. The first condition forbids merging; the second forbids missing an output. To express analogous ideas in any category, including categories whose objects are not presented as sets, we replace elements with arrows.

An arrow f: A → B is **monic**, or a **monomorphism**, when for every object X and every pair u,v: X → A,

```
f ∘ u = f ∘ v  implies  u = v.
```

The map f cannot conceal a distinction between two incoming probes. In Set, let X be a singleton selecting e₁ or e₂. These selections are different, but tagging makes them equal. Thus t is not monic. An injective function is monic because equality after f forces equality of the selected values at every element of every probe domain. Conversely, singleton probes show that every monic function is injective.

An arrow f: A → B is **epic**, or an **epimorphism**, when for every object Y and every pair u,v: B → Y,

```
u ∘ f = v ∘ f  implies  u = v.
```

Here we test outgoing distinctions. If f reaches all of B, two functions agreeing after f must agree everywhere on B. If it misses an element b, we can make two functions to {0,1} that agree on everything f reaches but disagree at b. Consequently, in Set, epic means surjective.

**Try it.** Is the inclusion Q → E epic? Can you exhibit a failed cancellation?

**Answer.** Let u send every record to 0. Let v send e₁ and e₂ to 0 but e₃ to 1. The functions agree after restriction to Q, but u ≠ v. The inclusion is monic and not epic. The tagging map t is epic and not monic.

Every isomorphism is both monic and epic: compose an asserted equality with its inverse to cancel it. But the converse is not true in every category. In the thin category 0 → 1, every arrow is both monic and epic, because any parallel pair is already equal. The arrow 0 → 1 has no inverse. There is no arrow 1 → 0.

This small counterexample is a reason to resist loose translations such as “monic always means no information is lost.” That explanation is reliable for ordinary functions and helpful for the general cancellation law. It does not absolve us from checking which probes the chosen category permits. A category with too few probes can fail to register a distinction we care about philosophically.

### Universal properties: specifying a role precisely

The word “universal” can sound metaphysical. Here it begins as a quantified mathematical condition: a statement concerning every object or every diagram of a specified kind in a specified category.

Consider a report format that records no distinctions whatsoever. Every set of records has exactly one function to a singleton 1 = {∗}. Whatever record enters, the output is ∗. This characterizes a **terminal object**: T is terminal when, for every object X, there is exactly one arrow X → T.

In Set, every singleton is terminal. The empty set is not terminal: no function sends the element of a nonempty singleton into the empty set. Instead, the empty set is **initial**: for every X there is exactly one function ∅ → X, the function with no input assignments to make. In general, an initial object I has exactly one arrow I → X for every X.

These are roles within a category. “Terminal” need not mean largest, most complete, most real, or ultimate. In Set it has just one element. In our inclusion order of commitments, the terminal object is the full set {p,q,r}. The same categorical definition identifies different constructions because the arrows mean different things.

Why insist on exactly one arrow? A category with two parallel arrows A → B fails to make B terminal even if every object has some arrow to B. Mere reachability is insufficient. Conversely, a branching diagram R → G and R → H has no terminal object if there are no further arrows: neither branch can receive an arrow from the other.

**Try it.** Suppose T and T′ are both terminal. Why must they be isomorphic?

**Answer.** Terminality supplies unique arrows f: T → T′ and g: T′ → T. The composite g ∘ f and the identity 1_T are both arrows T → T. Terminality says there is exactly one, so they are equal. Likewise f ∘ g = 1_T′. Moreover, the isomorphism is unique. This is the basic pattern behind uniqueness results for universal constructions.

Initial and terminal are related by **duality**. Given a category C, its opposite Cᵒᵖ keeps the objects but reverses every arrow and reverses the order of composition. A terminal object of C is initial in Cᵒᵖ. A monic arrow in C becomes epic in Cᵒᵖ. This is a transformation of a mathematical description, not a claim that dependence can be physically reversed or that philosophical dualism follows from categorical duality.

We now have enough to understand a universal property as a uniquely determined way of comparing a proposed construction with every competing construction of its specified kind. The comparison maps, their direction, and the equations they satisfy are the content of the claim.

### Products: retaining two answers together

Suppose we give records two tags. Let M = {quiet,active} and V = {checked,pending}. A record gets one tag from each set. What is a single format that records both answers without adding a third choice or losing a combination?

The set M × V has four elements:

```
(quiet,checked)   (quiet,pending)
(active,checked)  (active,pending)
```

There are projections π_M: M × V → M and π_V: M × V → V, which retrieve the first and second entries. Given any set X of records and functions a: X → M and b: X → V, there is a function h: X → M × V defined by h(x) = (a(x),b(x)). It satisfies

```
π_M ∘ h = a,     π_V ∘ h = b.
```

These equations force both coordinates of h(x), so h is unique. We have proved the **product universal property**. In any category, a product of A and B is an object P equipped with projections P → A and P → B such that every pair of arrows X → A and X → B factors uniquely through those projections by an arrow X → P.

Notice what the claim quantifies over. It does not say there is only one arrow from X to P. Different pairs of tags produce different arrows. It says there is exactly one arrow that gives the two answers already specified.

Two plausible alternatives fail in opposite ways. If we omit (active,pending), then a singleton record assigned those two tags has no possible image. Existence fails. If we put two distinguishable copies of (quiet,checked) into the format and let both projections ignore the distinction, a record with those tags has two possible images. Uniqueness fails. The product includes every pair exactly once, in the sense fixed by its projections.

**Try it.** Could a four-element set of numbered codes {10,20,30,40} serve as the same product?

**Answer.** Yes, provided we specify a bijection between the four codes and the four pairs and use it to define the projections. A bare four-element set is insufficient to specify the product structure. Once two product structures are fixed, there is a unique isomorphism between them that preserves both projections. Other bijections between the underlying sets need not preserve the tags.

Products can look quite different elsewhere. In the category of subsets of {p,q,r} ordered by inclusion, the product of U and V is U ∩ V. Why? A projection W → U means W ⊆ U; a projection W → V means W ⊆ V. Every such W is included in U ∩ V. That is exactly the universal comparison W → U ∩ V. The product here is an intersection, not a set of pairs.

The difference matters for our project. A product of report formats records two answers. A product in an implication order can express a common constraint in a different direction, depending on the ordering chosen. Neither automatically means that Brahman is assembled from separate components. Calling something a product must specify the category and the projections before it says anything philosophically definite.

### Coproducts: retaining alternatives without premature identification

Suppose we have records from two commentarial dossiers A and B. We want one archive into which either dossier can be placed, while retaining which dossier each record came from. The appropriate construction in Set is the **disjoint union** A ⊔ B. Its elements are tagged records: (left,a) for a ∈ A and (right,b) for b ∈ B. The source labels left and right are distinct even when A and B happen to be the same set.

The source tag matters even if both dossiers contain a record called “self.” The matching word does not yet establish matching referents, arguments, or doctrines.

The inclusion maps ι_A: A → A ⊔ B and ι_B: B → A ⊔ B place each record in the combined archive. Given any functions f: A → X and g: B → X, there is exactly one function h: A ⊔ B → X such that

```
h ∘ ι_A = f,     h ∘ ι_B = g.
```

Define it by cases: h(left,a) = f(a) and h(right,b) = g(b). Every record belongs to one tagged part, so the definition covers the entire archive without ambiguity.

This is the **coproduct** universal property. It reverses all the arrows in the product property. Products combine maps into their factors; coproducts combine maps out of their summands. In Set, a product value supplies both entries, whereas a coproduct value specifies an entry from one of the two tagged sources.

**Try it.** Why might ordinary untagged union fail to be a coproduct?

**Answer.** If A and B both contain an element labeled “self,” choose f and g that send that element to different outputs. An untagged union has only one “self” element, so no function from it can extend both assignments. The coproduct avoids this forced agreement by preserving provenance.

In the inclusion order of subsets, coproduct is ordinary union. Every pair of inclusions U → W and V → W uniquely factors through U ∪ V → W. Again, the ambient category determines the construction. The union works there because maps are inclusions, not arbitrary assignments to outputs.

For comparing traditions, retaining tagged alternatives is often the responsible beginning. It is not yet integration. The next question is which cross-tradition identifications are warranted, and what follows when we impose them.

### Pullbacks: compatibility over a declared common question

Return to the three report records E and their tag map t: E → M. A second dossier contains F = {f₁,f₂}, with u(f₁) = quiet and u(f₂) = active. We want pairs of records that agree on this selected question.

The ordinary product E × F contains six pairs, including pairs with disagreeing tags. Instead take

```
E ×_M F = {(e,f) | t(e) = u(f)}.
```

There are three elements: (e₁,f₁), (e₂,f₁), and (e₃,f₂). Projections return the E record and the F record. Their tags agree, which means the square commutes:

```
E ×_M F ─────→ F
   │            │ u
   ↓            ↓
   E ────t────→ M
```

Commutativity alone does not make this a **pullback**. We also need the universal property. Suppose X is any set equipped with a: X → E and b: X → F such that t ∘ a = u ∘ b. Then h(x) = (a(x),b(x)) belongs to E ×_M F, because the tags agree. It is the unique function whose two projections recover a and b. Thus this set is universal among compatible pairs of assignments.

In a general category, a pullback of A → C ← B is a commutative square with vertex P such that every other compatible pair X → A and X → B factors uniquely through P. Products are the special case in which C is terminal: the two composites to C agree automatically.

**Try it.** Suppose all E records map to quiet, while all F records map to active. Does the pullback cease to exist?

**Answer.** In Set it still exists: it is empty. There are no compatible pairs. A nonempty X could not supply maps a and b satisfying the required equality, so the universal property does not demand an impossible map from such an X into ∅.

This gives a precise distinction between constructing a space of possible agreements and showing that it has a member. An empty space can correctly report an obstruction. Even a nonempty pullback proves agreement only over the common question M. Our three matching pairs do not establish that the paired reports have the same grammar, doctrine, or experience.

The philosophical work lies partly in choosing M. If it remembers only “speaks about consciousness,” many incompatible accounts will agree there. A richer interface might retain claims about agency, self-manifestation, dependence, and liberation. Enlarging it can remove matches that existed in the weaker interface. We should regard that as improved discrimination, not as a defect to hide.

### Pushouts: joining accounts along specified identifications

A pullback starts with two maps into a common target and finds compatible pairs. A **pushout** starts with two maps out of a common source and joins their targets subject to the prescribed identifications:

```
       K ───b──→ B
       │         │ j_B
     a ↓         ↓
       A ──j_A─→ P
```

The square must commute: j_A ∘ a = j_B ∘ b. Universality says that whenever f: A → X and g: B → X already agree on K, there is a unique h: P → X extending both, so h ∘ j_A = f and h ∘ j_B = g.

In Set, construct P from the disjoint union A ⊔ B and impose the identifications a(k) ∼ b(k) for each k ∈ K. Then take the equivalence relation generated by those identifications. Its equivalence classes are the elements of P.

An equivalence relation is reflexive (x∼x), symmetric (x∼y implies y∼x), and transitive (x∼y and y∼z imply x∼z). The relation generated by the chosen identifications is the smallest one satisfying those laws and containing them. An equivalence class [x] consists of everything related to x. Passing to classes is called taking a quotient: each class becomes one element, so the original distinctions within it are no longer available in that quotient.

Consider A = {a₀,a₁,a₂} and B = {b₀,b₁}, with K containing one item mapped to a₀ and b₀. The pushout has four elements: the joined class [a₀=b₀], plus the separate classes [a₁], [a₂], and [b₁]. This is an archive comparison in which one correspondence has been established and the other distinctions remain.

Why is it universal? Given agreeing functions f and g, define h([a]) = f(a) and h([b]) = g(b). Their agreement on K ensures that identified elements receive the same output. Equality of outputs is itself an equivalence relation, so the agreement extends through every identification generated by symmetry and transitivity. The function is well-defined. Every class has a representative from A or B, so the extension is unique.

Now try a more consequential correspondence. Let K have two items k₀ and k₁. Send them to a₀ and a₁ in A, but send both to b₀ in B. The pushout identifies

```
a₀ ∼ b₀ ∼ a₁.
```

Two previously distinct A records have become one class. This follows from the chosen comparison maps; it is not a theorem that the original distinction was philosophically illusory.

**Try it.** If an A-side interpretation assigns different values to a₀ and a₁, can it extend through this pushout while retaining that difference?

**Answer.** No. Any extension would have to assign the single class [a₀=b₀=a₁] both values. Such an interpretation does not agree with the stipulated interface. The universal property applies only to maps that do agree.

This is why “we can take a pushout” is not enough to establish a faithful harmonization. We must examine its maps from the original accounts. Do those maps preserve the distinctions, inference rules, and forms of agency the texts require? If a comparison identifies two roles that one school insists must remain distinguishable, the mathematics has exposed the cost of the proposed integration. We then need a better interface, a weaker claim, or an explicit philosophical argument for revising that school.

### Limits, colimits, and a useful final check

Products and pullbacks belong to one larger family. A **cone** over a diagram is an object with arrows to the objects in that diagram, compatible with its arrows. A **limit** is a universal cone: every other cone factors uniquely through it. Terminal objects are limits of empty diagrams; binary products are limits of diagrams containing two objects and no nonidentity arrows; pullbacks are limits of diagrams A → C ← B.

Reverse the arrows and we obtain cocones and **colimits**. Initial objects, coproducts, and pushouts are the corresponding examples. “Finite limits” means limits of all finite diagrams, including the empty diagram. Having binary products alone does not supply that empty limit, so we must include a terminal object when claiming all finite products.

One additional limit is an **equalizer**. For two parallel functions f,g: A → B, it is the inclusion of the subset {a ∈ A | f(a) = g(a)} into A. Every function X → A on which f and g agree factors uniquely through this subset. If f and g are two tag assignments to the same archive, the equalizer isolates exactly the records on which they agree. It records agreement without pretending the assignments were identical everywhere.

Before moving to functors and more ambitious constructions, try this diagnostic. A map sends every theory in a comparison to the label “nondual.” Have we proved that they are the same theory?

No. We have described a map that forgets distinctions. To know what it establishes, we need its domain, its target, its action on the relevant relations or arguments, and the properties it preserves or reflects. The preceding constructions give us concrete ways to ask those questions. The next stage is to learn how entire categories, rather than only individual objects, can be compared. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)


## Translating a theory: functors, naturality and the meaning of sameness

Suppose two teachings both distinguish misidentification, discrimination and freedom. Their diagrams might look alike. What would make that likeness mathematically informative? We need to specify what each diagram contains, translate its arrows as well as its labels, and determine which distinctions survive. We can then ask a stronger question: can we recover what we started with?

This lesson develops the required mathematics through three small examples. They are deliberately invented teaching examples, not reduced versions of whole darśanas. A small example lets us calculate every arrow; the philosophical application then has an explicit obligation to justify its objects, arrows and equations. Cheng’s Chapters 20–23 motivate the progression from maps of categories to maps between those maps and finally to representable functors. The examples and exposition here are original. In formulas, α[x] means the component at x, and 1[x] is the identity at x; brackets keep indices readable without specialized typesetting. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

## Start with an actual category

Imagine a curriculum with three stages:

- U: an unsettled self-description;
- D: a distinction has been articulated;
- R: the proposed recognition has been reached.

There are three basic procedures: a:U → D, b:D → R, and h:U → R. The first two describe a staged procedure; the third is a separately recorded direct procedure. We stipulate no equation identifying the two routes. This is a mathematical account of recorded procedures, not a claim that every practitioner must pass through these stages.

Form the free category Proc on this graph. It contains three identities, the three basic arrows, and one additional arrow b ∘ a:U → R. There are seven arrows altogether. There are no other nonidentity composites, because no nonidentity arrow leaves R. Its homsets are:

| From / to | U | D | R |
|---|---|---|---|
| U | {1[U]} | {a} | {h,b ∘ a} |
| D | ∅ | {1[D]} | {b} |
| R | ∅ | ∅ | {1[R]} |

The notation Proc(U,R) names the set in the upper right. It remembers two routes even though they have identical endpoints. Composition means concatenating procedures, with the rightmost procedure performed first: b ∘ a means first a, then b.

Now form a second category S with three statuses 0<1<2. It has exactly one arrow from i to j when i ≤ j, and no arrow otherwise. This category records available progress, but no distinction between procedures achieving the same progress. Both categories have three objects. Their difference lies in their arrows.

Before assigning philosophical significance, notice what this construction has **not** assumed. A return route has not been added. Reaching the same endpoint has not made two paths equal. An arrow has not been called a causal law, a proof or an identity of selves. Those interpretations require further arguments.

**Exercise 1.** How many arrows does S have? Which homset differs in size from the corresponding homset of Proc?

**Answer.** Six: three identities and arrows 0 → 1, 1 → 2, 0 → 2. The U-to-R homset has two arrows in Proc, while the 0-to-2 homset has one.

## Derive the definition of functor

A translation F:C → D must assign an object F(x) to every object x, and an arrow

```
F(f):F(x) → F(y)
```

to every arrow f:x → y. The endpoints are part of the requirement. An assignment of attractive labels is not yet such a translation.

What must it do to identities and composites? If the source says that a procedure does nothing, its translation should do nothing. If two procedures are composed before translation, the result should agree with translating them separately and then composing. Thus:

```
F(1[x])=1[F(x)], F(g ∘ f)=F(g) ∘ F(f).
```

These are the functor laws. A functor is precisely an assignment satisfying these requirements. They concern mathematical structure, not the truth of a philosophical interpretation assigned to that structure. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

Define Q:Proc → S by Q(U)=0, Q(D)=1, Q(R)=2. Send a,b,h to the unique arrows with the corresponding endpoints. The image of b ∘ a must be the unique arrow 0 → 2. Hence

```
Q(b ∘ a)=Q(b) ∘ Q(a)=Q(h).
```

This verifies the only composition of two nonidentity arrows. Identity laws handle all remaining composites. Therefore Q is a functor. The calculation also reveals exactly what it forgets: h ≠ b ∘ a, although their images agree.

Functoriality gives a practical failure test. Suppose we enrich the target so it has distinct arrows t,s:0 → 2, with the composite of the 0 → 1 → 2 arrows equal to t. An assignment sending b ∘ a to s cannot be a functor if it sends a,b to those two arrows. Its proposed translation contradicts a composition already fixed in the source.

A chain of translations is another functor. If F:C → D and G:D → E, their composite acts by GF(x)=G(F(x)) and GF(f)=G(F(f)). For example,

```
GF(g ∘ f)=G(F(g) ∘ F(f))=GF(g) ∘ GF(f).
```

This lets us build comparisons in stages. It does not make successive philosophical revisions automatically valid translations: retracting a source theorem may prevent a proof-preserving interpretation altogether.

## Preservation is not reflection

A functor **preserves** a property when the property survives translation. It **reflects** a property when finding that property after translation warrants finding it before translation. These are opposite implications.

Every functor preserves isomorphisms. If f:x → y has inverse g:y → x, then

```
F(g) ∘ F(f)=F(g ∘ f)=F(1[x])=1[F(x)],
```

and the analogous calculation gives F(f) ∘ F(g)=1[F(y)]. Thus F(g) is an inverse for F(f). This proof uses only the functor laws.

Every functor also preserves equations between parallel arrows. If k=g ∘ f, applying F gives F(k)=F(g) ∘ F(f). Consequently a commutative diagram remains commutative after translation.

Neither conclusion has an unrestricted converse. Our Q makes two different routes equal. Another example sends the entire progression S to a single-object category with only its identity arrow. Every arrow then becomes invertible, although 0 → 1 had no inverse in S. An irreversible stage progression can look like no change under a coarse account. That observation does not prove the progression was secretly reversible.

Nor does a general functor preserve every categorical property. A terminal object has a unique incoming arrow **from every object in its category**. Map the one-object category into the discrete two-object category, selecting one of its objects. The source object is terminal, but its image is not: the other target object has no arrow to it. The target has additional tests that the source never supplied.

This matters when interpreting “ultimate,” “complete” or “universal.” A property quantified over all permitted contexts cannot be transported merely because a diagram has matching labels. We must identify the relevant universal property and prove that this functor preserves it.

**Exercise 2.** Does Q(h)=Q(b ∘ a) establish that the direct and staged procedures are interchangeable?

**Answer.** Only in the chosen status account. It establishes no equality in the category that distinguishes procedures. To justify interchangeability there, one must add and defend an equation, or use a translation that reflects the relevant equality.

## Full and faithful: inspect each pair of endpoints

For fixed x,y ∈ C, a functor gives a function

```
F[x,y]:C(x,y) → D(Fx,Fy).
```

It is **faithful** if every such function is injective: distinct arrows with the same endpoints remain distinct. It is **full** if every such function is surjective: every target arrow between these particular image objects comes from a source arrow with those endpoints. Full and faithful means each homset function is a bijection. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

The functor Q is full. Looking at the table, every target arrow has a source preimage in the corresponding homset. It is not faithful because its two U-to-R arrows merge. The translation captures all status transitions but loses procedural distinction.

Faithfulness does not mean injectivity on objects. Let E be a discrete category with distinct objects e_1,e_2, and map both to the sole object of the identity-only category. Each diagonal homset maps a singleton injectively to a singleton. Each off-diagonal homset is empty, whose function into a singleton is injective: there are no two distinct inputs that could collide. The functor is faithful! It is not full: there is no preimage for the target identity in E(e_1,e_2)=∅.

This example is important for identity-talk. A faithful functor can identify two source objects. Faithfulness controls arrows **within a fixed homset**, not global object counting. Fullness supplies the missing requirement in this example.

Together, fullness and faithfulness reflect isomorphisms. Suppose F(f) has inverse v:F(y) → F(x). Fullness supplies g:y → x with F(g)=v. Then

```
F(g ∘ f)=v ∘ F(f)=1[F(x)]=F(1[x]).
```

Faithfulness gives g ∘ f=1[x]; similarly f ∘ g=1[y]. Hence f was already invertible. The two assumptions do different jobs: fullness lifts the candidate inverse, faithfulness verifies its equations.

For theories, this suggests a more discriminating question than “do the diagrams match?” Are different source reasons collapsed? Does the target introduce relationships absent from the source? Yet an ordinary functor between arbitrarily drawn categories does not by itself preserve all logical connectives or quantifiers. When categories are built from formulas and definable maps, the interpretation must also preserve the structure expressing the chosen logic. The theory-to-category construction and its soundness remain part of the task.

## Natural transformations compare whole interpretations

A functor is already a structured assignment. To compare two such assignments F,G:C → D, choose an arrow

```
α[x]:F(x) → G(x)
```

for every object x. These arrows must work together. For every f:x → y, require

```
G(f) ∘ α[x]=α[y] ∘ F(f).
```

This is the naturality condition. Its square is:

```
F(x) ──αx──▶ G(x)
 │             │
F(f)          G(f)
 │             │
 ▼             ▼
F(y) ──αy──▶ G(y)
```

Both routes start at F(x) and end at G(y). Naturality says that changing interpretation and following the source relationship commute. The word “natural” names this exact condition; it does not mean spiritually authentic, intuitively convincing or historically inevitable. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

Consider a category with one nonidentity arrow r:E → S, meaning that a detailed report is shortened. Define a set-valued functor F by

```
F(E)={A,B} × {vivid,dim},
F(S)={A,B}, F(r)(p,q)=p.
```

Here A,B identify speakers; the second coordinate is an annotation. Define G(E)=G(S)={self-report}, with G(r) the identity. Let α[E] and α[S] classify every input as a self-report.

For the input (A,dim), going right then down yields “self-report.” Going down then right first yields A, then “self-report.” The same calculation applies to the other three inputs. Therefore α:F ⇒ G is natural. It is not invertible: its components erase information about speakers and annotations.

The example contains no claim that A and B are the same conscious subject. It shows that a coherent classification can omit their distinction. Natural compatibility alone is weaker than recoverability.

Natural transformations compose componentwise. If β:G ⇒ H, set (βα)[x]=β[x] ∘ α[x]. The verification is instructive:

```
H(f)β[x]α[x]
=β[y]G(f)α[x]
=β[y]α[y]F(f).
```

Each equality uses one naturality condition. Functors with these transformations therefore form a category, written [C,D]. When arrows are juxtaposed in a calculation, composition is meant, with the rightmost arrow applied first: H(f)β[x]α[x] abbreviates H(f) ∘ β[x] ∘ α[x].

A natural transformation is an isomorphism in this category exactly when every component is invertible. The converse needs a check: rearranging naturality gives

```
F(f)α[x]⁻¹=α[y]⁻¹G(f),
```

so the inverses themselves form a natural transformation.

### Optional proof: comparing a comparison after translation

Suppose another functor H:D → E is applied to both interpretations. The components H(α[x]) form a transformation Hα:HF ⇒ HG. Applying H to the naturality equation proves this. If instead K:B → C is applied beforehand, the components α[K(b)] form α K:FK ⇒ GK. These operations are called whiskering: a comparison is transported through a preceding or subsequent interpretation.

With α:F ⇒ G and β:H ⇒ J, there is also a horizontal composite β⋆α:HF ⇒ JG. Its component is

```
(β⋆α)[x]=β[Gx] ∘ H(α[x])
=J(α[x]) ∘ β[Fx].
```

Why are the two expressions equal? Apply naturality of β to the arrow α[x]:Fx → Gx. This is a useful change of level: a component of one natural transformation serves as the input arrow for another naturality condition. Every endpoint is specified, so the equation can be checked without a pictorial metaphor.

Horizontal and componentwise vertical composition obey the interchange law. For α:F ⇒ G, α':G ⇒ L, β:H ⇒ J, β':J ⇒ M, it is

```
(β'⋆α') ∘ (β⋆α)
=(β' ∘ β)⋆(α' ∘ α).
```

For example, expand the left component as

```
β'[Lx]J(α'[x])β[Gx]H(α[x]).
```

Naturality of β changes the middle pair to β[Lx]H(α'[x]). Functoriality of H then combines the last two factors. The result is exactly the right component. These operations allow a system of interpretations and comparisons to compose coherently; their existence still depends on the particular functors and naturality conditions having been established.

## A transformation is not automatically an event in time

Nothing in the definition just given says that G happens later than F. It can compare two descriptions maintained simultaneously. Its components live in the target category and inherit whatever meaning its arrows have. If those arrows are functions between report sets, the components are functions, not experiences of liberation.

A temporal development is more directly represented by a functor L:T → X, where T is a time or stage category and X is an explicitly specified category of states and transitions. An arrow of T then receives an actual transition in X. Two interpretations of that development can be compared by a natural transformation if their transition maps satisfy the squares.

A concrete failure makes the difference clearer. Let the time category have one transition 0→1. At each time an event functor E has the set {0,1}, where1 means an event is present; its transition clears the event, sending both values to0. A status functor K has the same set at both times, but its transition is the identity, preserving whether recognition has been retained. Try components α[0]=α[1]=id, declaring event presence and retained realization interchangeable. At input1 the naturality equation fails:

```
K(t)(α[0](1)) = 1
α[1](E(t)(1)) = 0.
```

We can repair the representation by storing both event and retained status. At both times take M={(0,0),(0,1),(1,1)}; its transition sends (e,k) to (0,k). Projection π(e,k)=k now gives a natural transformation M⇒K, because clearing the event leaves the retained status unchanged. The state(0,1) represents retention without a currently occurring event. This construction does not prove that a particular knowledge is irreversible; the persistence rule is an explicit premise whose warrant must come from the philosophical account. It shows exactly what information the failed comparison omitted.

Similarly, a correction that retracts “the body exhausts selfhood” is not automatically a natural transformation between proof-preserving interpretations of the old theory. If the old theory proves that assertion, a target interpretation preserving its proofs cannot silently reject it. A category of revisions may be appropriate, but it has different arrows. This distinction keeps the mathematical formalism capable of expressing change rather than disguising revision as preservation.

## Three inscriptions, two values: equivalence without strict identity

Return to the number-five intuition. Take three formal expressions e=“5”, f=“2+3”, g=“6”. Construct a category A whose arrows are **certified agreement of value**, with exactly one arrow between expressions of equal value and none otherwise. Thus e and f have inverse arrows between them; g has only its identity. Composition is forced by agreement of value.

This category does not record actual proof histories: different proofs of 2+3=5 have deliberately been identified. Had we retained those histories, we would need to reconsider its homsets and their equations.

Let Vals be the discrete category with objects the values 5,6. Evaluation Eval:A → Vals sends e,f to 5, and g to 6. For each pair of expressions, both source and target homsets are singletons if the values agree and both are empty otherwise. Evaluation is therefore full and faithful. It also reaches every object of Vals.

Choose W(5)=e, W(6)=g. Then Eval ∘ W=1[Vals], but W ∘ Eval ≠ 1[A], since (W ∘ Eval)(f)=e ≠ f. Nevertheless there is a natural isomorphism

```
η:W ∘ Eval ⇒ 1[A].
```

Its components at e,g are identities and its component at f is the certified agreement e → f. Naturality holds because each relevant homset has exactly one arrow. This is an **equivalence of categories**: inverse translation is available up to coherent isomorphism, rather than literal equality.

In general, F:C → D is an equivalence when there is G:D → C with natural isomorphisms GF ≅ 1[C] and FG ≅ 1[D]. It is an isomorphism of categories if those composites equal the identity functors strictly. Our categories are not isomorphic: they have different numbers of objects. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

The useful recognition criterion says that an equivalence is full, faithful and essentially surjective: every target object is isomorphic to an image object. Conversely these three properties produce an equivalence when the needed representative choices are available, as they are in this finite example.

The criterion has constructive content. Choose, for each d ∈ D, an object Gd ∈ C and an isomorphism ε[d]:F(Gd) → d. For an arrow v:d → d', fullness and faithfulness give a unique arrow G(v) whose image is

```
F(G(v))=ε[d']⁻¹ ∘ v ∘ ε[d].
```

The image of a composite is the composite of these expressions: the middle ε and its inverse cancel. Faithfulness then proves the functor law for G. The displayed equation is also precisely what makes ε:FG ⇒ 1[D] natural. For each c, lift ε[Fc]:F(G(Fc)) → Fc through the homset bijection; these lifts give GF ⇒ 1[C]. They are invertible because full and faithful functors reflect isomorphisms, and naturality follows by applying the faithful F. Thus recovering a theory requires recoverable arrows and coherent comparisons, not just a return assignment on its labels.

The philosophical payoff is exact. Distinct expressions can share a referent without becoming one expression. Distinct mathematical presentations can be equivalent without literal equality. Neither result decides whether two jīvas are interchangeable presentations of one self. The example **assumed** arithmetic evaluation and certified equal value; those facts did the work. A selfhood comparison needs its own reference rules and admissible arrows.

**Exercise 3.** If A contained only identity arrows while evaluation still merged e,f, would evaluation remain an equivalence?

**Answer.** No. The empty homset A(e,f) would map to the singleton Vals(5,5), violating fullness. Common denotation alone does not supply the source relationships needed for this equivalence.

## Representables retain a whole pattern of access

A handful of predicates can make two things look alike. Category theory offers a more demanding relational profile. For an object x of a small category C, define

```
yx=C(-,x):Cᵒᵖ → Set.
```

Here yx abbreviates y(x); when the object is named y, yy means y(y), not multiplication. At a probe object a, this gives the entire set C(a,x) of arrows from a to x. At a probe-change u:a' → a, it gives the function

```
yx(u):C(a,x) → C(a',x), p ↦ p ∘ u.
```

The opposite category Cᵒᵖ has the same objects as C and every arrow reversed; its composition is correspondingly reversed. The direction of the profile maps reverses. An available route from a to x can be preceded by u, producing a route from a'. This is why yx is contravariant, equivalently an ordinary functor from the opposite category. Identities act as identities, and composition is respected because composition in C is associative.

A functor naturally isomorphic to one of these profiles is called **representable**. More generally, any functor P:Cᵒᵖ → Set is a presheaf; not every presheaf is representable. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

In our procedure category Proc, the profile of R is completely calculable:

```
yR(U)={h,b ∘ a}, yR(D)={b}, yR(R)={1[R]}.
```

Precomposing 1[R] with h yields h. Precomposing it with b, then with a, yields b ∘ a. The profile retains the distinction that the status functor forgot.

Restricted probes can lose decisive information. In a category with objects p,A,B, arrows p → A, p → B, and only identities otherwise, the profiles of A and B at p are both singleton sets. Yet A and B are not isomorphic. At the additional probe A, their profiles differ: C(A,A) contains an identity while C(A,B) is empty. The full profile includes every object, including the object being profiled, and every compatibility map. It is much more than agreement on a survey of properties.

## Yoneda, calculated and proved

Write Nat(yx,P) for the set of natural transformations from yx to P. The Yoneda lemma says that for a presheaf P,

```
Nat(yx,P) ≅ P(x).
```

The correspondence is natural in both x and P. Let us first construct the two directions, rather than treating the formula as a slogan. [Source](cite://eugenia-cheng/joy-of-abstraction/category-cheng)

Given α:yx ⇒ P, its component at x is a function α[x]:C(x,x) → P(x). The identity arrow supplies an input, so define

```
Φ(α)=α[x](1[x]).
```

Conversely, given z ∈ P(x), define a component at every a by

```
αᶻ[a](p)=P(p)(z), p:a → x.
```

This is well typed: contravariance gives P(p):P(x) → P(a). For u:a' → a,

```
P(u)(αᶻ[a](p))=P(u)P(p)(z)
=P(p ∘ u)(z)=αᶻ[a'](p ∘ u).
```

That is naturality. Applying the first construction gives αᶻ[x](1[x])=P(1[x])(z)=z. In the other direction, naturality of α at p:a → x, evaluated at 1[x], gives

```
α[a](p)=P(p)(α[x](1[x])).
```

Thus the second construction recovers every component of the original transformation. We have proved a bijection.

For a calculation, define P on the procedure category Proc by

```
P(R)={r,n}, P(D)={d[r],d[n]},
P(U)={u[r],u[m],u[n]}.
```

Set P(b)(r)=d[r], P(b)(n)=d[n]; set P(a)(d[r])=u[r], P(a)(d[n])=u[n]. Set P(h)(r)=u[m], P(h)(n)=u[n]. Functoriality forces P(b ∘ a)=P(a)P(b). This defines a legitimate presheaf: the two different routes remain different, so they may have different restriction functions. The symbols can be read as possible retrospective records; they are not stipulated psychological laws.

Choosing z=r gives the transformation

```
α[R](1[R])=r, α[D](b)=d[r],
α[U](b ∘ a)=u[r], α[U](h)=u[m].
```

One selected record at R determines all these values. Choosing n gives the other transformation. There are exactly two natural transformations yR ⇒ P, because P(R) has two elements.

The word “natural” in the theorem adds compatibility to this bijection. If θ:P ⇒ Q, then

```
Φ(θ ∘ α)=θ[x](Φ(α)).
```

If f:x → y and α:yy ⇒ P, then

```
Φ(α ∘ y(f))=P(f)(Φ(α)).
```

The first equation follows by evaluating at 1[x]; the second follows from naturality of α. Thus changing either input respects the correspondence.

Setting P=yy gives

```
Nat(yx,yy) ≅ C(x,y).
```

The map sends f:x → y to postcomposition p ↦ f ∘ p. Consequently y:C → [Cᵒᵖ,Set] is full and faithful: the Yoneda embedding. A natural isomorphism of complete profiles therefore yields an isomorphism of their representing objects. It does not yield their literal equality.

**Exercise 4.** Could we alter the displayed transformation by setting α[U](h)=u[r], while keeping α[R](1[R])=r?

**Answer.** No. Naturality at h forces α[U](h)=P(h)(r)=u[m]. Matching isolated values without respecting the transition maps is insufficient.

## What this permits in the philosophical study

Yoneda does not turn the Self into an empirical object. “Object” in category theory means an item in the specified mathematical structure; it need not mean an intentional object of consciousness. We could model contexts of self-ascription and their licensed transformations. Whether that representation captures a theory’s nonobjectifying self-disclosure is a separate adequacy question. Nor does 1[x] represent consciousness merely because it is self-directed: every object has an identity arrow, including objects used to represent stones or numbers.

The mathematics now imposes concrete obligations on our comparisons:

- **Śaṅkara and later Advaita:** preserve distinctions between awareness, cognitive presentation and corrected attribution. A functor forgetting the positive-ignorance posit can display shared correction structure; it cannot prove the disputed posit equivalent to its denial. Saccidānandendra, Bhāmatī, Vivaraṇa and Madhusūdana need their actual explanatory rules retained in the richer theories. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection) [Source](cite://saccidanandendra/vedantaprakriyapratyabhijna/category-saccidanandendra) [Source](cite://vacaspati/bhamati/category-bhamati) [Source](cite://prakasatman/pancapadika-vivarana/category-vivarana) [Source](cite://madhusudana/siddhanta-bindu/category-bindu)
- **Rāmānuja, Nimbārka and Śrīnivāsa:** common reference can coexist with real individual distinction. The expression/value example explains that possibility, but their body–indweller naming inference must be established from their own semantics; arithmetic evaluation is not a substitute. [Source](cite://ramanuja/vedartha-samgraha/category-ramanuja) [Source](cite://nimbarka/vedanta-parijata-saurabha/category-nimbarka)
- **Bhāskara, Vallabha and Vijñānabhikṣu:** condition-removal, manifestation and domain-relative selfhood are different structures. Bhikṣu’s correction of which self is final may require a revision map alongside a theorem-preserving comparison. Vallabha’s disclosed Kṛṣṇa-nature must not silently be replaced by another account’s individual-nature referent. [Source](cite://bhaskara/brahma-sutra-bhasya/category-bhaskara) [Source](cite://vallabha/tattvartha-dipa-nibandha/category-vallabha) [Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/vijnanamrta-bhasya/category-bhiksu-self)
- **Madhva, Jīva and Baladeva:** dependence, actual first-person recognition, positive agency and fulfilled participants are substantive constraints. A common profile that omits all individuating relations cannot establish merger. An adequate mathematical comparison must first explain which relations it includes. [Source](cite://madhva/anuvyakhyana/category-madhva-self) [Source](cite://jiva/sat-sandarbha/category-jiva-love) [Source](cite://baladeva/govinda-bhasya/category-baladeva-agency)
- **Trika and independent Śākta witnesses:** reflexivity, intrinsic power and constitutive fullness need rules beyond an identity arrow. Devīgītā and Kāmakalāvilāsa/Cidvallī also require separate power/correction interpretations. Representing each through presheaves does not make their accounts equivalent. [Source](cite://utpaladeva/isvara-pratyabhijna-karika/category-trika-reflexivity) [Source](cite://abhinavagupta/paratrimsika-vivarana/category-trika-fullness) [Source](cite://devi/devi-gita/category-sakta) [Source](cite://punyananda/kamakala-vilasa/category-punyananda) [Source](cite://natanananda/cidvalli/category-natanananda)
- **Devotional and modern teachings:** Madhusūdana’s developmental account, Ramaṇa’s specified highest devotion, Ramakrishna’s devotion after knowledge, Vivekananda’s fulfilled worship and Aurobindo’s further manifestation need distinct temporal and semantic maps. Natural transformations can compare those maps once defined; they do not establish the historical or experiential sequence themselves. [Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti) [Source](cite://ramana/upadesa-saram/category-ramana) [Source](cite://ramakrishna/kathamrita/category-ramakrishna) [Source](cite://vivekananda/complete-works/category-vivekananda) [Source](cite://aurobindo/life-divine/category-aurobindo)

The same discipline applies to Sāṅkhya’s plural subjects, Nyāya’s substance/quality distinction, Jain knowledge-modes and Madhyamaka’s resistance to intrinsic-nature reification. Shared labels do not supply missing inverses, arrows or source premises.

The next substantial construction should choose one source argument, present its full local theory, and propose an explicit interpretation. We can then calculate preservation, find what fails to reflect, and attempt recovery. The methods learned here make such a comparison possible while leaving its philosophical conclusions open to proof.


## Śaṅkara: correcting the knower, not producing awareness

Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* 2.3.7 argues that attempts to establish or deny something already presuppose the self. Scripture does not introduce Brahman as a further object opposite this knower; 1.1.4 explains its work through the inward self and the removal of erroneous knower–known divisions. The teaching must therefore correct the understanding of the one already asking the question. It does not supply an otherwise absent awareness. [Source](cite://shankara/brahma-sutra-bhasya/category-shankara-self-correction)

This creates a problem for “that thou art.” Initially, “you” may be understood as a finite, acting, afflicted individual, while “that” may suggest an unlimited reality elsewhere. Simply asserting identity would transfer the affliction to Brahman or promise a real transformation of the individual into something previously absent. In *Upadeśasāhasrī* I18.179–195, the two expressions instead correct one another. “That” excludes afflicted limitation; “you” excludes the interpretation of Brahman as an external nonself. Verse195 says *duḥkhitvāpratyagātmatvaṃ vārayetām ubhāv api*: “Both would exclude being afflicted and not being the inward self.” The correction concerns what the expressions disclose, not the indiscriminate transfer of every predicate initially attached to them. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection)

The tenth-person illustration makes the point concrete. Someone who counts the others but omits himself need not produce an additional person. Instruction corrects the omission. Likewise, Śaṅkara's teaching requires discrimination of what “you” means; bare repetition of a sentence is insufficient. *Bṛhadāraṇyakabhāṣya* 1.4.10 describes self-knowledge through the cessation of ignorance-based superimposition and expressly denies that this makes the self an object: *nātmano viṣayīkaraṇam*, “not making the self an object.” A correcting cognition can arise without producing the awareness it discloses. This is why ordinary numerical equality between two independently specified objects is an inadequate starting point for reconstructing his argument. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection) [Source](cite://shankara/brahma-sutra-bhasya/category-shankara-self-correction)

This does not make the world sheer nothingness. *Chāndogyabhāṣya*6.3.2 assigns truth to transformations as Being and rejects their supposed reality apart from Being. Name and form are not an independently established second material. Conversely, their truth as Being does not automatically establish that differentiation **as differentiation** survives ultimate inquiry. The ocean/waves account rejected in *Bṛhadāraṇyakabhāṣya*5.1.1 expressly makes a dual form ultimately real. Calling every dependent mode “real” without specifying this distinction recreates the dispute rather than resolving it. [Chāndogya6.3.2](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/read/chandogya-bhashya/6/); [Bṛhadāraṇyaka5.1.1](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/read/brihadaranyaka-bhashya/5/).

Reflection enters to explain a further difficulty in ordinary “I know” language. The intellect changes and takes object-forms; intrinsic awareness does not perform that changing operation. Yet an unconscious operation by itself does not explain an experienced cognition. Śaṅkara puts the problem sharply: *na buddher avabodho 'sti nātmano vidyate kriyā*—“Understanding does not belong to intellect [intrinsically]; action does not belong to self” (I18.54). Neither side considered in isolation explains the ordinary knower. Nondiscrimination joins them by assigning intellect's agency to awareness and awareness's consciousness to intellect. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection)

A merely adjacent witness would not solve the problem: it would leave unexplained why instruction addressed to this apparent knower can correct its self-understanding. Reflection describes the connection while denying a literal transfer of consciousness into the mind. As the receiving medium changes, the presentation follows its features; intrinsic awareness does not thereby become agitated, divided or damaged. The analogy is deliberately limited: I18.86 denies that illustration and illustrated case agree in every respect. Correct understanding removes the two intrinsic misattributions while cognitive presentation can continue. A correct pot judgment, a dream chariot and doubt can therefore differ as cognitions without requiring three different kinds of underlying awareness. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection)

The present formal reconstruction distinguishes awareness, mental medium, reflected presentation, content and self-ascription. It represents the two directions of misattribution and selected relations between medium-features and cognitive presentation. It does not yet derive the entire account of knowledge, sleep-return or cosmological manifestation. In particular, adding intrinsic formative agency is a philosophical proposal, not a consequence of the reflection mechanism.

[Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection) [Source](cite://shankara/brhadaranyaka-bhashya/category-fullness-commentary)

## Gauḍapāda, Maṇḍana and Sureśvara: contemplation is not one theory

Gauḍapāda3.32–35 distinguishes awakening to self-truth from the mind's subsidence in sleep;3.44 instructs the practitioner to awaken a subsiding mind and quiet a distracted one. Thus “no mental objects” is insufficient. But2.32 and3.48 also make ultimate nonorigination claims, and3.36 explicitly uses namelessness and formlessness. His praxis cannot be used to delete his ontology. We have reconstructed these selected arguments, not a complete Gauḍapāda theory. The next formal step is a transition system distinguishing sleep, distraction, residual coloring and stable knowledge, while keeping ultimate nonorigination separate from descriptions of practice. [*Āgamaśāstra*2.32;3.31–48](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_gauDapAda-AgamazAstra.xml).

Maṇḍana's *Brahmasiddhi* begins with positive bliss, knowing and unbornness; bliss is not merely absence of pain. Its later discussion permits repeated contemplation directed toward immediate realization without making immortality a manufactured product. Sureśvara's *Vārttika*2.4.167–175,217–234 resists requiring an independent yogic perception of the inward self, yet retains hearing and contemplative means until knowledge manifests. “Maṇḍana favors meditation; Sureśvara rejects it” therefore misses the actual issue: which cognition is preparatory, which is immediate, and what makes teaching sufficient. Neither author yet has a separate complete categorical presentation in this study. The next comparison must align these epistemic stages before testing a common teaching rule. [*Brahmasiddhi*, Kuppuswami Sastri edition, pp.1–3,154–157](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_mandanamizra-brahmasiddhi.xml); [Sureśvara2.4](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/read/brihadaranyakabhashya-vartika/2-4-1/).

[Source](cite://gaudapada/mandukya-karika/category-gaudapada) [Source](cite://mandana/brahma-siddhi/category-mandana) [Source](cite://suresvara/brhadaranyaka-bhashya-varttika/category-suresvara)

## Ignorance: the dispute is about explanation

Why does one person's realization fail to liberate everyone? Vācaspati makes this a test of the explanation. If the same undifferentiated world-seed persists, why does the liberated person not become bound again? If that seed is destroyed, why does anyone else remain bound? His answer in *Bhāmatī* 1.4.3 is that ignorance differs per jīva. Knowledge in one locus need not oppose ignorance in another. This does not establish a collection of independently real self-substances: the appearance of individual limitation is itself explained through beginningless ignorance. [Source](cite://vacaspati/bhamati/category-bhamati)

He consequently distinguishes questions that a single “depends on” arrow would obscure. In whom does ignorance reside, and what does it fail to disclose rightly? Ignorance has the jīva as its locus while depending on Īśvara as causal support and as its referent. Vācaspati explicitly denies that these are all the same receptacle relation. His seed-and-sprout reply to mutual dependence refuses a first moment at which an already separately established pure self chose ignorance. It does not exempt the theory from explaining how its simultaneous commitments fit together. [Source](cite://vacaspati/bhamati/category-bhamati)

Prakāśātman's question is different. The self is luminous, yet I can immediately acknowledge “I do not know.” If illumination by consciousness were already the knowledge opposed to ignorance, that acknowledgment would be impossible. Hence witnessing ignorance and removing it must differ. His positive-ignorance account assigns a condition explanatory work beyond the mere absence of a thought: it accounts for obstructed recognition, misleading presentation and persistence through sleep. The mind cannot be its ultimate independent explanation if the mind itself belongs to the field being explained. “Positive” here does not mean an ultimately real stuff existing beside Brahman; the explanation concerns the apparent field. [Source](cite://prakasatman/pancapadika-vivarana/category-vivarana)

Later Advaitins also assign different roles to reflection and its conditions. In Madhusūdana's report of the Vivaraṇa account, ignorance-conditioned consciousness serves as prototype, while the jīva is a reflection conditioned by the mind and its dispositions. His report of Vācaspati instead distinguishes consciousness as the object of ignorance from consciousness as its locus, with ignorance differentiated per jīva. The *Saṃkṣepaśārīraka* report distributes prototype/reflection roles differently again. These differences affect what the teaching's “that” and “you” denote. They are not interchangeable pictures of an already agreed mechanism. Conversely, Vācaspati's own *Bhāmatī* also uses a mirror analogy: the labels “delimitation,” “reflection” and “appearance” cannot be treated as prohibitions on using another illustration. These are Madhusūdana's reports of alternative theories, not evidence that every Advaitin accepts his preferred one-jīva account. [Source](cite://madhusudana/siddhanta-bindu/category-bindu) [Source](cite://vacaspati/bhamati/category-bhamati)

Madhusūdana's *Advaitasiddhi* then asks how partless, already manifest consciousness can be what ignorance concerns. His answer distinguishes the presence of awareness from recognition of its unrestricted nature. Awareness is not produced at liberation. Nor does every cognition remove the relevant ignorance: seeing a pot may disclose that object while leaving the knower's self-misidentification untouched. In the *ajñānaviṣaya* argument, the operative remover is consciousness associated with the relevant valid cognitive modification, not bare consciousness or a mental event acting independently. Calling Brahman the “object of ignorance” therefore does not turn it into an external object seen by a second self. [Source](cite://madhusudana/advaitasiddhi/category-madhusudana-ignorance)

Saccidānandendra accepts the distinction between manifestation and effective correction. His own words are *adhyāsanirodhakatvaṃ tu pramāṇotpannajñānakṛtyam*: “Blocking superimposition is the work of knowledge produced by a means of knowledge.” Self-manifestation does not make everyone already enlightened. What he disputes is the inference from acknowledged confusion to an additional positive ignorance-power serving as its material cause. [Source](cite://saccidanandendra/mulavidya-nirasa/category-saccidanandendra-mula)

Take the mistaken perception of silver in a shell. On his account, the shell appears fit for silver-directed dealings. Correction reveals that silver was not there; it need not destroy an additional silver-entity produced from ignorance. The earlier defender has a serious reply: neither the illusory effect nor its material explanation was claimed to be ultimately real, and an apparent support may be needed to explain the content of the appearance. Padmapāda already rejects both physical transformation and an independently earlier act of superimposition. Thus simply saying “the silver is not physically real” or “there was no first chronological error” does not defeat the earlier account. The disagreement is whether the positive explanatory support is required or instead redescribes the mispresentation as another entity. This reconstructs an earlier answer to the same philosophical objections; Padmapāda was not historically replying to a twentieth-century author. [Source](cite://saccidanandendra/mulavidya-nirasa/category-saccidanandendra-mula) [Source](cite://padmapada/pancapadika/category-padmapada)

Saccidānandendra also explains why correction need not leave a final cognition standing beside Brahman. The knowledge that removes the mistake recognizes its own nonultimate status. No second remover is needed merely to demote the first remedy. His formula *vidyājanir eva hi tannivṛttiḥ* means “The arising of knowledge itself is its cessation”—the cessation of ignorance. Knowledge and cessation describe the same correction from different sides, rather than a cognition first arising and then striking ignorance as a hammer strikes a pot. Lived understanding changes without the self becoming a different thing. [Source](cite://saccidanandendra/mulavidya-nirasa/category-saccidanandendra-mula)

Our common formal fragment preserves self-manifestation, actual error and nonproductive correction. One extension requires an additional positive material source of error; another denies that very posit in the same explanatory register. Both have models after the disputed vocabulary is forgotten. That proves common ground without dissolving disagreement. Separate full models of Bhāmatī, Vivaraṇa and Madhusūdana's residual-condition dynamics remain to be constructed.

Vidyāraṇya adds another needed distinction: *Jīvanmuktiviveka*2 separates truth-knowledge, attenuation of dispositions and mental stilling. Chapter4's active/absorbed-knower discussion does not simply give all knowledgeable activity and samādhi identical rank. The next model needs these three coordinates and their relapse/persistence conditions. Śrīharṣa's category critiques and Appayya's comparative Advaita remain author-level gaps: located works are not reconstructed arguments. [*Jīvanmuktiviveka*, chapters2,4](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_z_misc_major_works/jIvanmuktivivekaH.html).

[Source](cite://vacaspati/bhamati/category-bhamati) [Source](cite://prakasatman/pancapadika-vivarana/category-vivarana) [Source](cite://madhusudana/siddhanta-bindu/category-bindu) [Source](cite://saccidanandendra/vedantaprakriyapratyabhijna/category-saccidanandendra) [Source](cite://vidyaranya/jivanmukti-viveka/category-vidyaranya)

## Rāmānuja and Nimbārka: a shared inference with different explanations

Rāmānuja's *Vedārthasaṃgraha*65–76 explains how the individual can be a real substantive mode/body of its indweller. Inseparable qualification, not merely the grammatical category “quality,” licenses a word for the mode to designate the qualified bearer. The individual's pain need not become the supreme's pain in the same respect. Sections89–90 preserve the individual's own exercise of divinely sustained capacities; dependence is not the absence of agency. Liberation removes obstruction without manufacturing or abolishing that subject. [Primary text, §§65–76,89–90](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_rAmAnuja-vedArthasaMgraha.xml).

Nimbārka and Śrīnivāsa also use the body/indweller passage. *Kaustubha*1.4.22 explains why a word for the individual self can denote the indwelling supreme. Their account is not exhausted by contrasting “power” with Rāmānuja's “body.” At1.4.21 Śrīnivāsa retains nonseparation already in bondage and distinction even in release; at2.3.42 the part is a natural power, not a severed fragment. At2.3.45 finite defects do not transfer indiscriminately to the supreme. [Nimbārka–Śrīnivāsa,1932 Sanskrit edition, printed139–141,235–237](https://archive.org/details/xiba-brahma-sutra-vedanta-parijata-saurabha-vedanta-kau).

The shared theory preserves dependence, real agency, reference and nontransfer. Its naming extension now derives the selected common-reference result from separately warranted semantic rules and body/indweller premises. The worked construction below retains each expression’s qualifying ground and proves why shared reference does not identify those grounds. Mutual recovery of the schools’ additional explanations remains a further question.

[Source](cite://ramanuja/vedartha-samgraha/category-ramanuja) [Source](cite://nimbarka/vedanta-parijata-saurabha/category-nimbarka)

### Rāmānuja: why shared reference does not transfer pain

Rāmānuja's argument has three steps. The individual is supported, governed and possessed for the indweller, so it is the indweller's body in the relevant sense. It is nevertheless a real knowing subject. A word for such an inseparable substantive mode can designate the bearer qualified by it; this is how two expressions can reach one final referent while contributing different qualifications. Shared reference therefore need not transfer every property. The individual can suffer while the divine bearer does not suffer in that respect.

This is a response to a problem, not an arbitrary exemption. If individual and Lord were simply one suffering subject under two descriptions, pleasure in one hand would not prevent pain in the other from being that person's pain. Renaming the suffering element a “power” would not yet help. But when the opponent grants an intrinsically differentiated individual part, Rāmānuja says that the opponent has come to his own path. The promising comparison is therefore between two explained forms of intrinsic relation, rather than between the isolated words “body” and “power.”

[Source](cite://ramanuja/vedartha-samgraha/category-ramanuja)

### Nimbārka and Śrīnivāsa: difference and nonseparation through release

Śrīnivāsa also rules out an easy reconciliation that assigns difference only to bondage and nondifference only to liberation. He first reports that account under Auḍulomi, then qualifies it: even in bondage the individual is nonseparate because its persistence and activity are dependent; even in release its own nature remains distinct. Otherwise the unfailing nature of both would be lost. The two relations coexist across the transition. Liberation changes manifestation and obstruction, not every relation between the individual and the supreme.

Their causal argument has the same care. Brahman in the causal condition possesses subtle powers; in the effect condition those powers are manifested. Effects can differ in their functions without becoming an independent material beside their source. A translation into Rāmānuja's account must therefore preserve both causal continuity and role-specific transformation. Finding the same final referent alone does not show that power transformation and body/mode transformation explain everything in the same way.

[Source](cite://nimbarka/vedanta-parijata-saurabha/category-nimbarka)

### A word's application and its complete reference

Rāmānuja's account is broader than a rule for exceptional passages. In *Vedārthasaṃgraha*17–21 he argues that ordinary words approach what they denote through particular configured things, while their full reference extends through the individual self to the indwelling supreme. Ordinary understanding stops at a part of what is denoted. Hearing Vedānta completes that understanding; it does not cancel the cow, person or other mode first understood. The expressions still have different grounds of application. [Source](cite://ramanuja/vedartha-samgraha/category-ramanuja)

This changes what a mathematical model should represent. If we write `local(e)=j`, “local” records the individual mode through which this expression applies. It does not assert that the expression's entire actual denotation is j. And if `Ref(e,c,B)` records its ultimate bearer in a context c, this projection does not preserve the whole meaning by itself: we must retain how the expression reaches that bearer. Two expressions can reach B through different modes without becoming synonymous.

Rāmānuja gives a useful test. A staff can qualify a person who possesses it, but the word “staff” does not thereby designate that person in the same way as an intrinsic bodily qualification. The possessive construction does work that the unmodified noun does not. His criterion is not simply that two things are connected, nor that a qualifier must be a quality rather than a substance. It is whether the qualification is intrinsically inseparable in the relevant relation. The word “staff” can nevertheless ultimately refer to Brahman through its own place in the divine body. Failure to name the human possessor does not imply failure of Rāmānuja's wider ultimate-reference claim.

Nimbārka and Śrīnivāsa also bring a broad reference claim into their discussion. Śrīnivāsa invokes the supreme’s being denotable by all words on the basis of universal causality and related grounds. The comparison is therefore not a universal theory opposed to one isolated exception. Their discussion of the Maitreyī passage must explain apparently individual-self language together with the promise of knowing all through one and the teaching of liberating contemplation. The indwelling-controller argument gives a reason to interpret that individual-self expression as reaching the supreme. Śrīnivāsa also preserves the difference between this route, the reported causal route and the reported future-liberation route. His own qualification retains nonseparation in bondage and distinction in release. We have grounds for a shared selected inference and a broader denotational premise on both sides. Rāmānuja additionally provides the detailed account of ordinary partial understanding and its completion. We have not established that these entire semantic explanations are mutually recoverable. [Source](cite://nimbarka/vedanta-parijata-saurabha/category-nimbarka)

### Deriving the reference rather than assuming it

Here is a selected reconstruction. Let E be interpreted nominal expressions, X the represented beings, and C resolved interpretive contexts. These are types in the model, not a declaration that Brahman and an individual are independent substances. Write `g:E → X` for the expression's local application ground. Use a relation `Ref(e,c,x)` for its established ultimate bearer. In a resolved use, require at most one such bearer:

```
Ref(e,c,x) and Ref(e,c,y) imply x=y.
```

This condition gives uniqueness, not existence. We have not assumed that the model has already supplied a completed interpretation for every expression in every context. In particular, an omitted Ref tuple in this selected representation is not a historical assertion that the corresponding word fails to denote Brahman.

Let K(e,c) abbreviate explicit sufficient evidence in the selected argument: the nominal is used through its conventional ground; the inquiry concerns the whole Self; the argument appeals to inner-controller testimony and a liberating conclusion; and this is the author's affirmative interpretation. K contains no reference conclusion. For Rāmānuja, §4 connects the identity and inner-controller teachings with contemplation and its liberating fruit; §§17–21 and65–68 develop his affirmative reference argument. For Śrīnivāsa, the connected Maitreyī discussion supplies the contextual evidence. These are sufficient premises for the comparison, not necessary conditions governing every actual denotation.

The common bridge is:

```
K(e,c), Body(g(e),B), InnerSelf(B,g(e)), Governs(B,g(e))
    imply Ref(e,c,B).
```

Here B is the supreme bearer. The bridge is a substantive semantic rule reconstructed from the texts. Logic alone does not turn bodyhood into word-reference. Rāmānuja explains this step through intrinsic qualification: the body is an inseparable mode, and the appropriately grounded expression denotes its qualified bearer. His semantic rule applies more broadly than the selected contextual premises K. Śrīnivāsa's indwelling-controller argument supplies another route from the relevant individual-expression to the supreme. His broader all-word-denotation premise remains part of the source account; choosing this particular derivation does not make it an exception to a generally finite reference.

Take an individual j and an expression e_j whose ground is j. Suppose the source premises establish Body(j,B), the corresponding inner-self/governance relations, and K(e_j,c). Substitute `g(e_j)=j` into the rule: all its premises hold, so `Ref(e_j,c,B)` follows. A directly divine expression e_B has g(e_B)=B and reaches B through its own direct naming rule. This direct naming rule does not need the assumption Body(B,B). The individual distinction j≠B is a retained source premise. The common reference has now been derived through two routes; it is not a ground equality added to obtain the result.

A dependency test makes the achievement precise. Keep the same body, indweller, local-ground and context facts, but remove the body-reference rule and its school-specific semantic explanations. A structure recording only the directly divine expression's reference can satisfy those remaining premises. It need not record the individual's reference to B. Thus the semantic bridge does real inferential work. This is a test of the background theory with a premise removed, not a counterexample to either author's actual naming argument.

### The categorical result

Fix the selected context c and form the denoting domain

```
D_c = {e in E | there exists x with Ref(e,c,x)}.
scope_c : D_c → X.
```

The reference relation defines scope_c because a bearer exists for each member of D_c and is unique. It would be a mistake to call this a total function on all E: the restricted common theory has not supplied that totality. Both e_j and e_B are proved to belong to D_c by the preceding derivations.

Now retain the qualifying contribution with the product map

```
D_c → X × X
 e  ↦ (g(e), scope_c(e)).
```

Its two marked values are `(j,B)` and `(B,B)`. Projecting to the second coordinate gives equal ultimate bearers while the first coordinate preserves different grounds. The pair does not claim to be a complete semantics for every sentence, but it prevents this comparison from discarding the very distinction its sources use.

Recall the cancellation test for a monomorphism. The two names give maps from the singleton object into D_c. They are distinct: applying g yields j and B, which the selected sources distinguish. After composing with scope_c, however, both maps yield B. Therefore **scope_c is not monic**. The same proof works in the coherent syntactic category introduced later: the names give definable constant maps, and their denoting-domain membership is proved before those maps are used. Equal final reference cannot be cancelled backward into equality of local grounds.

A concrete model of this reference fragment checks compatibility with distinct grounds and nontransfer. Take three represented beings B, j and a staff s, with corresponding expressions e_B, e_j and e_s. Let their local grounds be B, j and s, and let all three expressions' completed reference be B. Give j and s their body relation to B; separately associate the staff with its human possessor j. Let suffering hold of j and not B. The two source-shaped naming rules can hold together in this model. Yet the rule “anything associated with j names j” fails: the word for the staff reaches B, not its contingent possessor. Unrestricted transfer of suffering also fails. All-word reference has not removed either the modes or their differentiated properties.

The comparison is consequently more substantial than two diagrams with similar labels. Each reconstructed school theory proves the common naming rule through its own premises. Translating formulas and their definable maps therefore gives structure-preserving functors from the common syntactic category into the two selected school categories. Interpreting the earlier model's total reference function requires using D_c as its expression domain; silently using the whole E would invalidate that interpretation. This is a proved integration of a selected inference. Mutual recovery of the complete body/mode and natural-power explanations, and a full semantics of predication, remain further questions.


## Bhāskara and Vallabha: positive manifestation has different structures

Bhāskara2.3.40 affirms real conditioned action while denying unconditional doership; suffering and the possibility of release constrain the latter. At4.4.4 natural nondifference remains while conditioned division ends. At4.4.7 consciousness coexists with positive powers and experienced bliss. His later provisional treatment of separately abiding liberated selves must not override his own preceding nondivision/sovereignty account. The current theory models condition-removal together with positive fulfillment. Its next extension should distinguish embodied realization from final release and explain what action remains possible, rather than globally equating every action with bondage. [*Brahmasūtrabhāṣya*, printed139–141,243–249](https://archive.org/details/NnNa_brahmasutra-bhashyam-of-sri-bhaskaracharya-by-vindhyeshwari-prasad-dvivedi-chowk).

Vallabha's *Sarvanirṇaya*84–106 and *Prakāśa* articulate Hari through causes, effects, qualities and activity. Common reality does not eliminate differences of manifestation. The principal cosmic jīva's bliss is “as if concealed”; that special passage cannot be generalized without argument to every ordinary person. The knowledge route's subsequent supreme attainment differs from devotion's own fruition. *Siddhāntamuktāvalī*1–2 defines service through a mind turned toward Kṛṣṇa, while14 has the knower see Kṛṣṇa within himself. [*Sarvanirṇaya/Prakāśa*, printed70–83](https://www.pushtimarg.net/pushti/ebooks/Tatvarthdip%20nibandh%20part%201.pdf); [*Siddhāntamuktāvalī*](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_vishhnu/siddhAntamuktAvalI.html).

Our Vallabha fragment preserves manifestation profiles, distinct routes, service and grace. Its common disclosure map selects Kṛṣṇa's nature disclosed within the self, whereas other maps select an individual's nature. Matching mathematical patterns does not identify these referents. The next map must preserve this difference while connecting intrinsic reality, appropriation-error and fulfilled service through source-supported rules.

[Source](cite://bhaskara/brahma-sutra-bhasya/category-bhaskara) [Source](cite://vallabha/tattvartha-dipa-nibandha/category-vallabha) [Source](cite://vallabha/siddhanta-muktavali/category-vallabha-service)

### Bhāskara: conditions, capacity and exercise

Bhāskara denies unconditional doership because action can cease in sleep and liberation must be possible. He immediately blocks the inference that conditioned agency is unreal: *na caupādhikaṃ kartṛtvam apāramārthikam*—“Nor is conditioned agency unreal.” Heated iron and an equipped carpenter illustrate capacities exercised under conditions. Divine enabling also takes account of the individual's effort. The relevant distinctions are therefore intrinsic nature, conditioned capacity and actual exercise—not simply real action versus illusion.

His final-release account removes the condition responsible for division while retaining consciousness, experienced bliss and powers. A faithful translation cannot replace “conditioned” with “nonexistent,” or replace cessation of division with deletion of all positive determination. Conversely, retaining a mathematical index for a person's history does not prove that an independently divided subject survives final release. The proposed model distinguishes that history-index from the predicate asserting metaphysical division.

[Source](cite://bhaskara/brahma-sutra-bhasya/category-bhaskara)

### Vallabha: manifestation, concealment and service

Vallabha's claim is stronger than saying that the world depends on Hari: Hari is the reality of qualities, effects and activity as well as their cause. Yet the following argument distinguishes their manifested functions. One common reality therefore does not make every manifestation interchangeable. In the principal-jīva discussion, bliss is “as if concealed,” while the commentary insists that the manifestation is in reality constituted of bliss. Concealment and destruction of nature must have different predicates.

Fulfillment also requires more than turning a hidden attribute on. The knowledge route and devotion have different sequences of attainment, and service is positively described as a mind inclined toward Kṛṣṇa. A model that equates fulfillment with the absence of every intentional relation would erase that service. A model that equates all knowledge-route and devotional histories because their ultimate basis is the same would erase the sequence that the source actually explains.

[Source](cite://vallabha/tattvartha-dipa-nibandha/category-vallabha) [Source](cite://vallabha/siddhanta-muktavali/category-vallabha-service)

## Madhva and the Gauḍīyas: conscious participation without unrestricted merger

Madhva and Jayatīrtha distinguish independence from freedom: Śrī is dependent but never bound. A jīva's consciousness, experience and agency are real; liberation unveils its intrinsic nature. Their conscious-unity argument concerns actual first-person recognition, not merely identical properties. Divine forms and qualities also have literal unity: this account has a positive theory of unity as well as of distinction. Yet individual liberated enjoyment does not become Viṣṇu's independent universal agency. [*Nyāyasudhā*, ad *Anuvyākhyāna*2.3.67–77;3.2.122–191;4.1.34–45](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_jayatIrtha-nyAyasudhA.xml).

The current model has distinct Lord, released jīva and never-bound Śrī, with separate ownership, knowing and self-recognition relations. Vyāsatīrtha's *Tarkatāṇḍava*I270–275 additionally blocks treating every occurrence of “I” as an unqualified divine speaker. A fuller map must carry this contextual semantics and the account of *viśeṣa*; they cannot be reconstructed from a bare dependence arrow.

Baladeva's *Govindabhāṣya*2.3.43 distinguishes self-manifestation **to oneself** from a lamp's visibility. At2.3.32,40 he defends real dependent agency, including liberated play: association with guṇas, not agency alone, makes the specified activity painful. Jīva's *Prītisandarbha*1,5 preserves higher selfhood and nonfragmentary participation without abolishing the individual;65–66 explains a special hlādinī operation and reciprocal love. The source is not asserting both a proposition and its negation without qualification under the name *acintya*. [Baladeva's primary text](https://grantha.jiva.org/index.php?e_no=669&show=entry); [Jīva's Sandarbha collection](http://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_jiva-gosvami-satsamdarbha.xml).

The Gauḍīya formal fragment preserves self-disclosure, enabled action and love while reserving universal world-governance to Bhagavān. Its next extension should explain higher-self participation and love's manifestation more fully; a single power marker is not a complete rasa theory. A universal-Self interpretation that simply deletes distinct fulfilled participants fails these retained commitments.

[Source](cite://madhva/anuvyakhyana/category-madhva-self) [Source](cite://madhva/tattvaviveka/category-madhva-dependence) [Source](cite://jiva/sat-sandarbha/category-jiva-love) [Source](cite://baladeva/govinda-bhasya/category-baladeva-agency)

### Madhva and Jayatīrtha: first-person recognition and divine unity

Jayatīrtha asks what would establish that two conscious presentations belong to one self. Mere similarity of conscious properties is insufficient. His proposed evidence is reconnecting awareness as oneself: the conscious subject recognizes the relevant nature as its own at some actual time. Previous-life amnesia is anticipated; the claim does not require continuous present recollection. Nor is God's knowing everyone's pain the same as God's experiencing it as his own.

The argument then grants the opponent's reply that the Lord need not own the individual's suffering and presses a further question: should not an omniscient, unobstructed Lord nevertheless recognize the alleged unity? A universal-Self theory therefore owes an account of the relation between universal selfhood and local first-person access. Distinguishing those levels is a possible answer, but it must explain why this recognition requirement does not apply as Jayatīrtha claims. Writing either `j=B` or `j≠B` has not yet engaged the argument.

Madhva also affirms primary unity of the Lord's forms, acts and qualities with the Lord's own nature. Jayatīrtha's viśeṣa account explains how meaningful articulation and selective disclosure can remain without introducing separate divine substances: knowing one aspect need not make every other aspect equally explicit. It is thus a theory of unity with distinctions, not merely a list of separations. But viśeṣa is admitted where unity is independently warranted; it is not a device for declaring all perceived objects one. A proposed integration must preserve this restriction as well as the positive unity.

[Source](cite://madhva/anuvyakhyana/category-madhva-self) [Source](cite://madhva/tattvaviveka/category-madhva-dependence)

### Baladeva and Jīva: enabled agency and reciprocal love

Baladeva tests the idea that being dependent makes the jīva inert. The jīva contributes willing and effort; God enables and directs that activity. It is consequently an enabled agent, not a lump of wood moved without its own contribution. Statements denying doership can deny independence rather than every capacity to act. Liberated play then provides a decisive control: if agency itself were the cause of suffering, such action would be impossible. The harmful condition and the act must be distinguished.

Jīva adds something a generic agency model does not contain. Fulfilled love involves the divine bliss-power's particular operation and reciprocal enjoyment; the participant is not deleted when the higher Self is realized. A faithful map must therefore preserve who loves, who receives and reciprocates love, and how divine power enables it. It cannot recover that structure merely from one shared “realized” state. Nor may it transfer Bhagavān's universal world-governance to every participant merely because they share consciousness and fulfillment.

[Source](cite://baladeva/govinda-bhasya/category-baladeva-agency) [Source](cite://jiva/sat-sandarbha/category-jiva-love)

## The other darśanas change the comparison

Īśvarakṛṣṇa's *Sāṅkhyakārikā*64 gives three elliptical negations whose scope Māṭhara explains through disidentification, nonownership and nonbelonging in relation to the natural principles. These negations do not deny that puruṣa exists. Kārikās17–21 argue for a subject distinct from prakṛti and for plural subjects;67–68 distinguish knowledge, continuing embodiment and final separation. The model must retain those arguments, not infer one universal subject from similar discrimination across persons.

Patañjali1.2–4 and the received Vyāsa commentary distinguish mental restraint, the seer's own nature and identification with modifications. At1.9 Vyāsa even questions possessive grammar: when puruṣa is consciousness, what is being predicated of what? Yet the path includes accurate cognitive concentration before cessation; Vācaspati on4.22–29 distinguishes reflected self-discrimination from its later relinquishment. Our formal case retains these successive functions. The next map must connect cognition's production, its accuracy and its eventual cessation, not treat silence as a sufficient cause of liberation. Primary anchors: *Sāṅkhyakārikā*17–21,64–68 with Māṭhara/*Yuktidīpikā*; *Yogaśāstra*1.1–9,1.50–51,4.22–34, Āgāśe edition1904.

Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika is not this same consciousness ontology with different words. Praśastapāda and Śrīdhara distinguish enduring self from cognition as a quality; their individuation argument does not identify subjects merely because general properties agree. In the selected final-release account special cognitive qualities cease, so its shared disclosure map records historically achieved knowledge, not an eternally continuing recognition-event. *Nyāyakandalī*,1895 edition, printed279,285–287,323, is the decisive witness here. Annambhaṭṭa's *Dīpikā* on absence and Madhusūdana's second mithyātva definition additionally require the relevant relation and delimiter to remain fixed: dropping them can turn “absent here in this respect” into an incompatible universal denial.

Finally, Kumārila's persistent recognizer, Śālikanātha's own-subject disclosure, Jain enduring conscious substance with newly arising complete-knowledge modes, and Madhyamaka's removal of intrinsic-nature grasping are different theories. Nāgārjuna18 and24 and Candrakīrti's commentary do not warrant an independent universal Subject left behind after correction. These comparisons show why praxis is indispensable but not a single proof of one ontology. Their categorical fragments are comparison controls, not evidence that all darśanas already assert Brahman.

### Sāṅkhya–Yoga: why suspension is not enough

The strongest test is not simply whether objects have disappeared. Vācaspati says consciousness-power is established in its own nature even during cosmic dissolution, yet this is not liberation. The conditions for renewed involvement have not thereby been exhausted. Correct discrimination must arise in the cognitive instrument, remove the relevant misidentification and dispositions, and cease without restoring the causes of return. Cessation is the end of a specified corrective process, not an unexplained switch that manufactures a conscious subject.

The formal distinction is small but important. A mental event e can be generated in an instrument and disclose a subject p without generating p. Later e can cease while p remains. Add separate predicates for latent seeds and nonreturn: otherwise sleep or temporary suspension receives exactly the same description as release. Plural subjects and the world's persistence for others must also survive the translation; a single person's realization does not dissolve every cognitive career.

[Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/yoga-varttika/category-yoga-reflection) [Source](cite://mathara/mathara-vrtti/category-mathara-negations)

### Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika: direct self-vision and what remains

Śrīdhara's Nyāyakandalī gives a strikingly close version of the proposed praxis. He calls the self intrinsically neither agent nor experiencer, explains I-making and mine-making through bodily and sensory conditions, and explicitly invokes Yoga1.12 and Sāṅkhya Kārikā64–65. Merely hearing the true doctrine is insufficient: entrenched bodily identification acts like a distorted taste that verbal assurance alone does not remove. Sustained concentration makes self-knowledge direct. With the appropriative dispositions removed, “the world, although existent, does not touch the self.” Self-vision, joy arising from it and living liberation are positive parts of his account.

The endpoint nevertheless differs from an intrinsically self-luminous consciousness theory. For Śrīdhara, the subject is an enduring substance, and its cognitive events are qualities. The liberating cognition can therefore do its work and finally cease, while the subject remains. The shared comparison records achieved self-knowledge; it must not silently turn that history into an eternally occurring experience. His ultimate differentiators also answer the equal-properties proposal directly: liberated selves may share ordinary qualities without being one subject. The integration must engage both the close praxis agreement and this different account of what finally remains.

[Source](cite://sridhara/nyaya-kandali/category-nyaya-self)

## Vijñānabhikṣu: integration through correction of finality

Bhikṣu's *Vijñānāmṛtabhāṣya*4.1.3 argues that just as jīva is self of an internal organ from which it differs, Brahman is self even of the jīva from which it differs. *Yogavārttika*4.23 says a supreme-self section requires discrimination even from jīva. The earlier self/mind discrimination remains valid; its claim to have reached the final Self is revised. Nondivision does not abolish mutual nonidentity. See *Vijñānāmṛtabhāṣya*2.3.43,4.1.3,4.4.4, inspected Kāśī witness pp.196,305–306,332; *Yogavārttika*4.23,1884 witness p.294.

The formal `SelfRole(domain,bearer,that-of-which-it-is-self)` relation encodes this argument. The learner's body→jīva→highest-self sequence changes endorsed finality while preserving real subjects. This is a concrete integration precedent, but not an unchanged endorsement of every lower theory. Its revision path must remain distinct from a translation preserving every theorem. The next task is to attach the fuller Yoga cognitive mechanism to this hierarchy, rather than mistake role labels for an explanation of recognition.

[Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/vijnanamrta-bhasya/category-bhiksu-self) [Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/yoga-varttika/category-yoga-reflection)

### Vijñānabhikṣu: preserving discrimination and correcting finality

Take a mind m, an individual j and the highest Self B. The discriminative teaching warrants “j is the self of m”; the Vedāntic teaching warrants “B is the self even of j.” Bhikṣu preserves j as real and conscious, its distinction from mind, and its distinction from B. What he revises is the claim that j exhausts ultimate selfhood. Difference as mutual nonidentity and nondifference as nondivision therefore do different work in his argument.

This lets us separate a faithful translation from a revision. A translation can preserve the earlier self/mind discrimination. If the earlier theory additionally claims that no higher selfhood is possible, the successor cannot preserve that finality claim unchanged. The further instruction disputes it. A diagram of successive instruction should show that change openly rather than calling every stage equally final or pretending that the later claim is obtained merely by relabelling the earlier one.

[Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/vijnanamrta-bhasya/category-bhiksu-self)

## Trika, independent Śākta traditions, and Ramakrishna

Utpaladeva1.5.11–15 makes reflexive manifestation central: illumination without *vimarśa* would resemble an inert crystal. Kṣemarāja's *Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya*1–5 explains world and finite mind through free consciousness and contraction, while16–19 allows stable recognition during object-experience and after absorption. Abhinavagupta's *Parātriṃśikāvivaraṇa* adds constitutive fullness: prerequisites and retained complete apprehension together make a determination implicate the whole. [Utpaladeva](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_utpaladeva-IzvarapratyabhijJAkArikA-with-vRtti.xml); [Kṣemarāja](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/sa_kSemarAja-pratyabhijJAhRdaya.xml).

This supports nonseparate power, not two independent entities joined afterward. But a universal rule assigning masculine identity and dependent feminine modality fails even here: Kṣemarāja identifies consciousness, grammatically feminine, as free. Independent Śākta sources add their own arguments. *Devīgītā*2.2–21 makes the Goddess nonobjectifiable consciousness with inherent power;3.1–19 qualifies māyā by ordinary and ultimate vision while grounding the world's reality in her reality. At4.18–24, conflicting direct meanings give way to a common indicated consciousness-referent. Its highest devotion in7.11–33 includes recognition, remembrance and non-hostility toward beings as divine forms. These selected arguments have been reconstructed and reviewed; no independent complete categorical theory of the work is yet supplied. [*Devīgītā*, working primary witness](https://textgridlab.org/1.0/aggregator/text/textgrid:49k64.0).

Puṇyānanda's *Kāmakalāvilāsa*1–4 and Naṭanānanda's *Cidvallī*,1918 edition pp.1–6, separately explain reflexive first-person will, knowledge and action through intrinsic power. Their Śrīvidyā cosmology must not be equated with *Devīgītā*'s knowledge-sublated māyā merely because both are Śākta. [Primary root/commentary transcription](https://muktabodha-digital-library.org/texts/IAST/M00025). The next map should preserve these different power and knowledge roles. *Devīmāhātmya*, *Yoginīhṛdaya*, *Tripurārahasya* and Krama still need separate connected primary reconstructions; none is counted as covered by the two selected witnesses or by Trika.

Ramakrishna's gem and its radiance emphasize inseparability: the radiance is not a second independent thing subsequently attached to its bearer. Still water and waves bring a different distinction into view—the same reality can be described through rest or active manifestation. His comparison with the same Captain in different activities makes this descriptive point especially clear. These analogies motivate distinguishing a reality's power from a particular exercise of that power: still water need not be powerless. They do not by themselves establish a general theory of mutual metaphysical grounding. Medhananda already speaks of aspects of one reality; the substantive question is whether their inseparability is best expressed as mutual dependence, coessential aspects, or one reality's intrinsic power. [Source](cite://ramakrishna/kathamrita/category-ramakrishna) [Source](cite://medhananda/infinite-paths-to-infinite-reality/category-medhananda)

### How reflection actually restricts a presentation

The mirror comparison also contains a mechanism. In *Tantrāloka*3, an ordinary mirror receives visible form without receiving the beloved's touch. Its capacity determines which aspect appears. The mirror as the image's inseparable support is also distinguished from the light, eye and cognition through which that image becomes known. A representation therefore needs separate places for its support, receiving conditions, displayed aspect and cognitive access. Otherwise the model cannot distinguish an image that appears from the original object being bodily present.

Abhinavagupta then tests the analogy's limit. Memory and imagination show presentations without a currently present external original. This does not by itself prove that consciousness produces the entire world. The larger argument also denies an independently established outside of manifestation and invokes consciousness's intrinsic powers. At the limited level, an internal cognitive field presents a differentiated “this.” At complete reflexivity, the subject is not another image produced before an additional observer. The same term “reflection” must not conceal these different operations.

[Source](cite://abhinavagupta/tantraloka/category-trika-receiver)

### Śākta accounts of consciousness and power

In *Devīgītā*, consciousness cannot become an object illuminated by a further cognition: that would leave the further illumination unexplained. Nor is knowledge an accidental quality attached to an otherwise insentient self. The Goddess's power is inherent, yet the work distinguishes ordinary and ultimate vision and rejects ultimate separate difference. Its treatment of “that” and “you” consequently changes the semantic interpretation: directly expressed predicates conflict, while an indicated consciousness-referent is shared. This is a worked reason to distinguish a referent from the descriptions under which it is first approached.

*Cidvallī*, commenting on *Kāmakalāvilāsa*, instead makes first-person will, knowledge and action central to its explanation of luminosity and power. The root's mirror and conjunction language does not establish two independently constituted substances; neither should those words be edited out. The commentary interprets the mirror through the Lord's own first-person power. A shared formal vocabulary can represent both accounts, but a successful integration must explain the different work done by knowledge, power and sublation in them.

## Realized devotion: four distinct contributions

The received Ṣaṭpadī3 concedes disappearance of difference while retaining asymmetric belonging. Devotion is therefore not restricted grammatically to someone who has never considered nonduality, though the verse alone does not date a completed realization. [Primary hymn](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_vishhnu/vishnu6padi.html).

Madhusūdana begins by asking whether devotion can be desired as fulfillment itself rather than only as an instrument leading elsewhere. He distinguishes devotion as means from devotion as fruit: hearing, chanting and service cultivate a God-oriented disposition, while fulfilled love has its own experienced value. Shared preparation does not make bhakti and brahmavidyā identical. He expressly distinguishes their nature, means, fruit and eligible practitioner. In this contrast, devotion involves a melted mind taking God's form in a determinate modification; brahmavidyā concerns the nondual Self in a nondeterminate modification without the melting condition. The latter is not unconsciousness, and the former is not merely failed nondual knowledge. [Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti)

His image of melted lac explains durable affection. What enters it while melted remains as a stable disposition when it hardens, becoming manifest again under suitable conditions. This distinguishes enduring devotion from a passing emotional episode. It also gives the comparison a concrete problem to solve: how can devotional cognition change without a return to binding self-misidentification? The object, affective preparation and mode of enjoyment matter; a generic arrow called “expression” would omit the explanation. [Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti)

In the eleven-stage account, the sprout of love precedes realization of one's nature. That realization discriminates the inward self from gross and subtle bodies and culminates in knowledge of nondifference between the indicated meanings of “you” and “that.” The next stage follows for a reason: such knowledge strengthens dispassion toward separate rewards, and that dispassion intensifies love toward God. His own commentary states this sequence, rather than leaving it to an illustrative saint's story. Later divine practices can occur effortlessly as fruit rather than as an unfinished obligation. This is a particular devotional maturation, not a claim that every Brahman-knower has the same melted-mind condition or must traverse every stage. [Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti)

The liberated devotee's further enjoyment is therefore not evidence that liberation failed. Madhusūdana expressly affirms devotion in those liberated while living. Root2.49's Ganges comparison distinguishes returning to the water for delight from bathing to fulfill an unmet instrumental purpose; the following verse distinguishes present embodied enjoyment from a future-bodied reward. This supports a positive account of fulfilled love. In the formal comparison, knowledge-occurrence, retained realization, devotional disposition and devotional occurrence must therefore remain distinct. A person can retain realized understanding while devotional cognition changes; this does not make a single cognitive modification both determinate and nondeterminate in the senses just distinguished. [Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti)

Ramaṇa's *Upadeśasāram*9 instead identifies the specified highest devotion with abiding in true being beyond thought; *Aruṇācalapañcaratnam*5 joins all-as-divine-form worship with single-hearted love. Ramakrishna's11 March1886 discourse explicitly retains devotion after knowledge for teaching and enjoyment. Vivekananda's *Para-Bhakti*, chapterIV, describes fulfilled devotees living to worship. These are positive theories, not one definition repeated four times. [Ramaṇa](https://archive.arunachala.org/docs/upadesa-saram/); [Ramakrishna](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/kathamrita/pdfhtml.htm); [Vivekananda's works](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/complete_works.htm). Their next mathematical comparison must preserve each account's endpoint and temporal claims rather than globally identify the word “bhakti.”

[Source](cite://madhusudana/bhakti-rasayana/category-madhusudana-bhakti) [Source](cite://ramana/upadesa-saram/category-ramana) [Source](cite://ramana/arunachala-pancharatnam/category-ramana-pancharatnam) [Source](cite://ramakrishna/kathamrita/category-ramakrishna) [Source](cite://vivekananda/complete-works/category-vivekananda)

### Ramaṇa: devotion changes its form as self-understanding deepens

Read Upadeśasāram's sequence rather than isolating its ninth verse. Offered action prepares the mind; service of the world as the Lord gives that action a different orientation; repetition develops toward continuous contemplation. Nondivisive contemplation then culminates in stable true being, called highest devotion. The claim is not that an unconscious blank replaces love. It changes what devotion consists in as the assumed division between worshipper and divine reality is examined.

The later verses connect this account to self-knowledge. The I-thought is investigated as dependent; Lord and individual differ under their conditioning and cognition while their nature as being is not two independently constituted realities. Seeing one's own Self is therefore also seeing the Lord, with seeing understood through abidance rather than an encounter with a further external object. Temporary mental subsidence remains distinct from irreversible correction. A mathematical account needs both the removal of an appropriating self-conception and the positive condition of abidance. It cannot represent this sequence simply by erasing every relation. [Source](cite://ramana/upadesa-saram/category-ramana)

The fifth Aruṇācalapañcaratnam stanza nevertheless affirms worship of everything as the divine form with undivided love. Devotional expression and nonotherness are jointly present. This does not, by itself, assert an eternally separate substantial experiencer. The inference from a second-person address to an independently existing second substance is exactly what the larger self-vision account prevents. [Source](cite://ramana/arunachala-pancharatnam/category-ramana-pancharatnam)

### Ramakrishna: a retained devotional role has positive value

The recorded teaching of 11 March 1886 distinguishes a binding ego from the devotional self-presentation a knower may retain. Teaching and the enjoyment of divine love are expressly different purposes. Devotion after knowledge is consequently more than a performance for others who have not understood. The burnt-rope comparison explains how an appearance of individuality can remain without its earlier binding force.

The qualification matters as much as the affirmation. The 1 March 1885 pot-in-water discussion makes I–you consciousness conditional on the pot remaining; it does not make the pot eternal. Its different accounts of ascent, return and nonreturn cannot be collapsed into a rule that every liberated person necessarily resumes the same devotional life. The proposed integration should represent a nonbinding relational role and its conditions, rather than making either retained relation or its permanent disappearance compulsory. [Source](cite://ramakrishna/kathamrita/category-ramakrishna)

### Vivekananda: recognition alters the meaning of continued appearance

Vivekananda's Philosophy of Ishvara treats the philosophical absolute and the object of devotion within one account, while distinguishing devotional consciousness from realization beyond the subject–object triad. Para-Bhakti IV then describes many fulfilled devotees continuing to live for worship. Chapter X allows consciously adopted relational love while concluding in the unity of love, lover and beloved. Neither mere ignorance of identity nor three independent ultimate substances describes that whole argument.

The Real and the Apparent Man supplies the complementary mechanism. Continued body–mind activity can retain momentum after its binding interpretation has been corrected. Appearance can recur with a changed authority, as a recognized mirage differs from one taken as water. The claimed consequence is greater love toward beings recognized as divine, not withdrawal of every meaningful relation. The mathematical task is to track the persistence of correction through changing appearances and actions, while preserving this ethical and devotional content. A bare realized/unrealized switch loses what the account is trying to explain. [Source](cite://vivekananda/complete-works/category-vivekananda)


## A worked translation from Sanskrit to a formal rule

Madhusūdana's second definition of falsity in the Advaitasiddhi is a good place to see why the Sanskrit must govern the mathematics. An objection points out that an accepted object can be absent under a different relation: a pot is on the ground through contact but not through inherence. Another objection exploits a changed delimitation, as with contact at a tree's top rather than its root. A definition that permits those switches would classify an ordinarily accepted presence as false for the wrong reason.

> tac ca rūpaṃ sambandhaviśeṣo ’vacchedakaviśeṣaś ca /

And that respect is a particular relation and a particular delimiting condition. [Source](cite://madhusudana/advaitasiddhi/category-1-relation-delimiter)


His reply requires the particular relation and delimiting condition to be retained. The surrounding sentence repeats the relative–correlative construction “by whichever … by that very same.” It also preserves the locus. In the same connected discussion he distinguishes presence and absence at different orders of existence from contradictory assertions in the same order. These are argumentative constraints, not decorative precision. [Source](cite://madhusudana/advaitasiddhi/category-navya-madhusudana)

Let x denote an intentional target, t a time, and f a frame containing the locus, relation, delimiter and appraisal grade. Merely having a name for x in the model does not assert its external existence. Write:

```
Pos(x,f,t) = present under this specified frame
Neg(x,f,t) = absent under this specified frame
Pos(x,f,t) and Neg(x,f,t) cannot both hold.
```

The pot can satisfy Pos in the contact-frame and Neg in the inherence-frame without contradicting this rule. If we erase the frame and say only “positive in some frame” and “negative in some frame,” both resulting predicates hold of that pot. The proposed context-erasing translation therefore cannot preserve a rule forbidding their conjunction. This is an explicit counterexample to a bad translation.

The categorical starting point is a theory with typed frames and the displayed incompatibility rule. Its interpretations must preserve that rule after translating each type and predicate. The selected cognitive interface can be conservatively extended by assigning a selected frame to each of its external-existence tests: every old model has such an expansion without changing its original judgments. The source still has to warrant which context a test selects. We cannot alter a claim's context merely to obtain a desired verdict.

This interface does not make absence anywhere equivalent to sheer nonexistence, nor one failed judgment equivalent to Madhusūdana's full definition of falsity. That definition also requires the relevant presentation condition and all-times negation; the fuller quantifier structure is not encoded merely by the small incompatibility rule. Madhusūdana even offers a conditional alternative analysis of absence. The reconstruction keeps that branch distinct from the one modeled here.

Navya-Nyāya's technical distinctions are valuable precisely because they expose these dependencies. Annambhaṭṭa's own Dīpikā distinguishes counterpositives, their delimiters and the relations under which absence is asserted. Using this precision in Vedānta does not require assuming that every author accepts one whole Nyāya ontology. The philosophical account determines the meanings; the mathematics makes their permitted combinations and translations inspectable. [Source](cite://annambhatta/tarka-samgraha-dipika/category-navya-annambhatta)


## From compatible perspectives to a topos

We now have enough mathematics to ask the question that motivated this investigation: what kind of space could hold these philosophical perspectives? There are several answers, because there are several things we might mean by holding them together. We can organize different descriptions, select descriptions that agree, require partial descriptions to determine a whole, or compare complete logical theories. These are different constructions. We will calculate a small example all the way through before deciding which construction serves the philosophical project.

The next topics extend beyond Cheng's book. Adjunctions can be studied in Riehl; the topos definitions and logical constructions can be studied in Baez and Caramello. The finite examples and philosophical applications here are our own. [Source](cite://emily-riehl/category-theory-in-context/category-riehl) [Source](cite://john-baez/topos-theory-in-a-nutshell/category-baez) [Source](cite://olivia-caramello/topos-theoretic-preliminaries/category-caramello)

## A diagram of contexts

Imagine two detailed descriptions, a and m, which share a smaller vocabulary k. Draw the inclusions of vocabulary:

```
      a       m
       ↖     ↗
          k
```

Call this category I. Besides the two displayed arrows k→a and k→m, it contains three identities. It has no arrow between a and m. Its objects are contexts of description, not individual selves, mental states or historical schools. That choice is important: we are constructing a place for accounts of those things.

Suppose a description in vocabulary a can be reduced to vocabulary k simply by omitting detail. The data travel in the opposite direction to the vocabulary inclusions. Here is a complete example:

| Context | Its possible records | Reduction to k |
|---|---|---|
| k | 0, 1 | identity |
| a | a₀, a₁, a₂ | a₀↦0; a₁↦1; a₂↦1 |
| m | m₀, m₁ | m₀↦0; m₁↦1 |

For intuition, 0 records that a binding identification remains and 1 records that it has been corrected. The a-description additionally distinguishes quiet recognition a₁ from devotional expression a₂. The m-description has only one fulfilled record in this example. No historical tradition is being said to have exactly these records. We are testing a proposed choice of comparison vocabulary.

A **presheaf** on I is a functor F:Iᵒᵖ→Set. The symbol Iᵒᵖ means reverse the arrows of I; Set is the category of sets and functions. Thus F supplies a set of records for every context and a restriction function for every vocabulary inclusion. Restriction along an identity does nothing. Restricting in two stages gives the same result as the composite restriction. Our diagram has no nontrivial two-stage inclusion, so these laws are particularly easy to check.

Think of a presheaf as one coherent system of context-indexed data. It is not yet a system that reconstructs every large context from smaller ones. That additional demand comes later.

**Exercise.** Could the a-to-k restriction send a₁ to both 0 and 1?

**Answer.** Not as the function just defined. We would need a different mathematical representation, perhaps a set of possible answers or a relation. Ambiguity can be represented, but it must be represented in the data rather than smuggled into the word “function.”

## Agreement is a pullback, not a verbal resemblance

We can now ask for pairs of records that agree on the shared vocabulary. Apply the pullback construction already learned:

```
P = F(a) × over F(k) F(m)
  = {(a₀,m₀), (a₁,m₁), (a₂,m₁)}.
```

Why those three? The first pair restricts to 0 on both sides; the other two restrict to 1 on both sides. The remaining three possible pairs disagree. The projection maps send each compatible pair to its a-record and m-record. For any set X carrying two compatible record functions, the unique function X→P sends x to its pair of records. This is the universal property, verified by an explicit formula.

Notice the difference between compatibility and identity. The pullback retains a₁ and a₂ as different possibilities. It does not equate their two forms of realized expression. Nor does agreeing on one question settle agreement on a richer question.

Add a second common question, about a disputed kind of agency. Replace the common set by pairs (recognition status, agency answer). Suppose every a-record answers no and every m-record answers yes, with both answers understood in exactly the same sense. There are now no compatible pairs. Both presheaf records still exist, and the category organizing them still exists. What fails is the demand that these two selected accounts agree on that question.

There are two intellectually different responses. We might discover that “agency” meant intrinsically binding alteration on one side and nonbinding formative power on the other. Then the common question was badly translated, and the interface should be corrected using the texts. Or the meanings might really agree and the answers conflict. Then a synthesis must revise a claim or leave the accounts unglued. Merely assigning them to different contexts does not establish their compatibility.

This gives a precise reading of the proposal that local disagreement need not prevent a global framework. A framework can contain conflicting accounts as distinct objects. A single account satisfying both statements in the same respect is a stronger demand. In the ordinary logic used here, a proposition and its negation cannot both be accepted in one consistent model. A topos does not automatically remove that requirement.

## What counts as enough local information?

First picture an ordinary gluing problem. There are three positions {1,2,3}. A left report assigns a color to positions {1,2}, and a right report assigns a color to {2,3}. They agree on their overlap if they assign the same color to 2. When they agree, there is exactly one coloring of {1,2,3} restricting to both reports: take the left color at 1, the common color at 2, and the right color at 3. If they disagree at 2, no such coloring exists. If we had observed only {1,2}, the color at 3 would remain undetermined. Coverage and agreement do different jobs.

The presheaf of colorings assigns to every region the set of its colorings, with restriction by forgetting positions outside a smaller region. It is a sheaf for covers by regions whose union is the whole region under discussion. The two-report calculation illustrates the existence and uniqueness of gluing. For more reports, their restrictions must agree on every overlap, so the chosen value at a position is independent of which report supplies it.

Our philosophical context diagram is more abstract. There need not be a set of spatial positions behind it. We must specify which context arrows are covers rather than borrowing that judgment from an unmentioned underlying space.

Suppose someone says that knowing recognition status is enough to determine a complete realized life. In our diagram, this amounts to treating the small context k as a cover of a, and also of m. “Cover” has a precise role: it specifies which local data are allowed to count as sufficient for reconstructing data in a larger context.

This is a strong decision. It is not a consequence of drawing k→a. An inclusion says which vocabulary is shared; a cover says that the chosen local information suffices. In this finite category we can impose the following topology: at a, both the sieve containing k→a alone and the maximal sieve cover; similarly at m; at k, only its maximal sieve covers. A sieve is a collection of arrows into an object closed under further precomposition. If f:d→c belongs to the sieve and g:e→d is any arrow, then f∘g must also belong. Later we will use these same sieves to compute truth values.

A **Grothendieck topology** assigns covering sieves subject to three rules. The maximal sieve covers. Pulling a covering sieve back along any arrow gives a cover of its domain. Finally, if a sieve becomes covering after pullback along every arrow of a cover, it already covers. Pullback here means f*R={g | f∘g belongs to R}; it does not require the index category to have pullback objects. These rules generalize whole-region coverage, restriction of a cover, and coverage established locally.

Check our choice. Pulling {k→a} back along k→a gives {id_k}, which covers k. The m calculation is the same. Pullback along identities changes nothing. For the third rule, a sieve on a that covers after pullback to k must contain k→a, so it is either the declared singleton cover or the maximal cover. The same argument works at m; at k there is only the identity test. Thus all three rules hold. This verification is why we can use the topology as actual mathematical data rather than a metaphor.

A **sheaf** is a presheaf in which every compatible family over a cover has exactly one gluing. Existence says the local pieces really arise from some larger record. Uniqueness says they determine which larger record it is. Both clauses matter.

For our singleton cover k→a, the condition says that restriction F(a)→F(k) is a bijection. Every k-record must lift, and it must lift uniquely. Our restriction is surjective, but not injective: 1 has the two lifts a₁ and a₂. The m restriction is already a bijection. Thus F is not a sheaf for this topology.

**Exercise.** Suppose we remove a₂. Is the resulting data system a sheaf? What if we remove a₀ instead?

**Answer.** Removing a₂ leaves one lift of each common value and produces a sheaf. Removing a₀ loses the lift of 0 and still leaves two lifts of 1, so it violates both existence and uniqueness. Sheafhood is not simply a demand for fewer records.

The philosophical diagnosis is now exact. Recognition status does not determine the distinction between quiet and devotional expression in the chosen data. Declaring it sufficient forces the reconstruction to lose that distinction. We could retain the presheaf, choose less demanding covers, or enrich the common vocabulary. Which choice is faithful is a philosophical question about what the accounts claim determines what.

## Sheafification and adjunction: a controlled completion

There is a canonical way to turn this F into a sheaf for our chosen topology. Let LF have the two-element set {0,1} at every context, with identity restrictions. Define η:F→LF by the identity at k and by the two original restrictions at a and m. These components commute with restrictions, so η is a natural transformation. At a it merges a₁ and a₂.

Why call this construction canonical rather than one arbitrary simplification? Take any sheaf G and any natural transformation α:F→G. Its component α_k chooses an element of G(k) for each common record. Because G is a sheaf, the restriction G(a)→G(k) is invertible. Therefore the a-component of a factorization LF→G is forced:

```
β_k = α_k
β_a = restriction_G(a→k)⁻¹ ∘ α_k
β_m = restriction_G(m→k)⁻¹ ∘ α_k.
```

Naturality of α shows that β∘η=α. There is no other β, because its component at k and the two unique lifts have already determined it. Every map from F into a sheaf factors uniquely through LF. This universal property is what our sheafification accomplishes.

The construction works for every presheaf X on this I: put X(k) at each context, with identity restrictions, to obtain LX. On a natural transformation α:X→Y, put α_k at every context to obtain Lα. Identity and composition laws hold componentwise, so L is a functor. The same unique-lift proof applies to every X. Precomposing with a map of presheaves or postcomposing with a map of sheaves commutes with this factorization, because its components are determined by restriction and the same unique lifts.

We have reached an **adjunction**. Write PSh(I) for the category of presheaves and natural transformations, Sh(I,J) for the sheaves for the chosen topology J, and i for the inclusion of sheaves among presheaves. Then

```
Hom_Sh(LF, G) ≅ Hom_PSh(F, iG),
```

naturally in F and G. On the left are maps from the completed object into G. On the right are maps from the original object into the same G viewed as a presheaf. The symbol ≅ here denotes a bijection of sets of maps, not an equality of the presheaves F and LF. The passage in both directions is compatible with further maps; this is what “naturally” contributes.

In general, L is **left adjoint** to R when Hom_D(Lx,y) is naturally in bijection with Hom_C(x,Ry). Oppositely directed functors alone do not establish this condition. Nor does an adjunction usually say that the functors undo each other. Here sheafification genuinely loses an a-distinction, even though its relationship to inclusion is exceptionally well controlled. [Source](cite://emily-riehl/category-theory-in-context/category-riehl)

There is a simpler order-theoretic example. A closure operation Cl on subsets sends A to the least closed set containing it. If B is already closed, then Cl(A)⊆B exactly when A⊆B. Reading each inclusion as the unique arrow in a poset category gives the same adjunction pattern. This will explain the fullness construction below: completion need not identify the determinations from which it begins.

## First understand a topos in sets

A category does not become a topos merely because it is a diagram, a network, or a place where several perspectives meet. An **elementary topos** has finite limits, exponentials, and a subobject classifier. These names specify operations and universal properties. Let us make each intelligible in Set before moving to presheaves. [Source](cite://john-baez/topos-theory-in-a-nutshell/category-baez)

Finite limits combine finitely many requirements. The terminal set is a singleton: every set has exactly one function into it. Products record simultaneous choices. An equalizer of f,g:X→Y is the subset of inputs x satisfying f(x)=g(x). The inclusion of that subset is universal among maps on which f and g agree. Terminal objects, binary products and equalizers suffice to construct all finite limits, including the compatibility pullback calculated above. “Limit” here does not mean an infinite approach to a numerical value.

An **exponential** Yˣ is an object of functions from X to Y. In Set its elements are precisely those functions. It comes with evaluation ev:Yˣ×X→Y, sending (function, input) to output. Its universal property says that a function h:Z×X→Y corresponds uniquely to a function ĥ:Z→Yˣ. For each z, ĥ(z) is the function x↦h(z,x). This is called currying. The parameter z selects an entire function rather than one output.

For example, if X has two inputs and Y has three outputs, Yˣ has nine elements. If Z records practitioners and X records two testing conditions, a function Z×X→Y assigns an outcome to each practitioner-condition pair. Its curried form assigns to each practitioner their whole two-condition response profile. This is bookkeeping, not a theory that a conscious subject is exhausted by such a finite profile.

A **subobject** generalizes a subset: it is represented by a monomorphism into the object under discussion, with isomorphic inclusions identified. In Set, every subset A⊆X has a characteristic function χ_A:X→{false,true}. Membership in A is recovered by pulling back the inclusion {true}→{false,true} along χ_A:

```
A ───────────→ {true}
│                 │
▼                 ▼
X ───χ_A─────→ {false,true}.
```

This square is a pullback because A consists exactly of the inputs sent to true. The two-element set, equipped with its chosen true point, is Set's **subobject classifier**, written Ω. It classifies predicates on every set by maps into one fixed object. A topos requires an analogous universal classifier, but its Ω need not have just two ordinary elements.

## Now perform the same operations context by context

For our small category I, the category PSh(I) of all presheaves is a topos. One presheaf F is an object of that topos, just as one set is an object of Set. Do not confuse I, the index category, with PSh(I), the world of all coherent data systems on it.

Terminal presheaves and products are easy to calculate. Put a singleton at every context for the terminal presheaf. For a product F×G, put F(c)×G(c) at each context c and restrict each coordinate by its own restriction map. Equalizers are also computed context by context; naturality ensures that restricting an equalizing element still equalizes. This accounts for the finite-limit requirement.

Exponentials require more care. An internal function at a context has to work compatibly over that context and its restrictions. It cannot simply be any function between the two sets visible at that one context.

Use our earlier F. Define G by G(a)={u,v} and singleton sets at k and m, with the only possible restriction functions. A local map from G to F over a specifies a function G(a)→F(a) and a function G(k)→F(k), commuting with restriction. Both u and v restrict to the same element of G(k). Their images must therefore lie in the same recognition fiber of F(a).

If the chosen k-image is 0, both images must be a₀: one possibility. If it is 1, each image can independently be a₁ or a₂: four possibilities. Thus the exponential Fᴳ has five elements at a, not nine. At k it has two; restriction sends the single first possibility to 0 and the other four to 1. This calculation makes the word “compatible” do real work. An arbitrary pair of images with different recognition statuses fails the required square.

The general formula is (Fᴳ)(c)=Nat(y(c)×G,F), where y(c) is the representable presheaf learned through Yoneda. In our example y(a) is singleton at a and k and empty at m, so only the a-and-k part of the natural map contributes. The formula explains why the calculation needed those two contexts and not the unrelated m-context.

## Contextual truth, calculated rather than asserted

Choose the subpresheaf A⊆F with A(k)={1}, A(a)={a₁}, and A(m)={m₁}. It is closed under restriction, as required: a₁ and m₁ both restrict to 1. At a, the record a₂ is not in A(a), but its restriction is in A(k). To describe this predicate we need to record where it holds after restriction.

At a there are exactly three sieves:

| Sieve | Meaning for a record at a |
|---|---|
| empty | the predicate holds at neither a nor k |
| {k→a} | it holds after restriction to k, but not at a |
| {id_a, k→a} | it holds at a, and therefore also at k |

The fourth apparent option, {id_a} alone, is not a sieve. Precomposing id_a with k→a would require k→a to be included. Truth of a subpresheaf predicate is stable under restriction.

The classifier map sends a₀ to the empty sieve, a₂ to {k→a}, and a₁ to the maximal sieve. At k there are two sieves; at m there are three. Restriction pulls a sieve back along the context arrow. These sets and restriction maps form Ω. The general formula assigns to x∈F(c) the sieve of arrows f:d→c for which F(f)(x) lies in A(d). Pulling back the maximal-sieve truth map recovers A. We have now seen how the classifier actually operates. [Source](cite://olivia-caramello/topos-theoretic-preliminaries/category-caramello)

There is no contradiction in a₂'s intermediate value. “The detailed record is a quiet realization” can fail at the detailed level while its reduced record satisfies the broader condition “recognition is retained.” The predicate's meaning across contexts is given by the selected subpresheaf. We should not translate this into “the very same proposition, in the very same respect, is both true and false.”

The topos need not obey classical excluded middle internally. A sieve's **pseudocomplement** is the largest sieve disjoint from it; this is the negation operation here. In this example the intermediate sieve at a has empty pseudocomplement: every nonempty sieve on a meets it at k→a. Its union with that pseudocomplement remains intermediate rather than maximal. So the internal disjunction P or not-P need not be true at a. This is a precise algebraic phenomenon, not a general claim that reason fails near ultimate reality. Set itself is a topos with ordinary Boolean truth values.

**Exercise.** Why not let A(a) contain a₀ while keeping A(k)={1}?

**Answer.** Because a₀ restricts to 0, which is not in A(k). That proposed family is not a subpresheaf, and it cannot be classified by the construction just described. Contextual truth still has laws.

## From a theory to its mathematical universe

Our finite presheaf topos organizes chosen records. A **classifying topos** does something more specific: it is associated with a logical theory and represents that theory's models across suitable mathematical universes. To reach this construction, we need a language, axioms and an appropriate logic. Sanskrit precision helps us specify them; it does not replace the specification.

For example, introduce sorts Person, Episode and Condition, a function owner:Episode→Person, and a predicate Occurs(e,s). The formula “there is an episode owned by c at s” is

```
HasContent(c,s) ⇔ ∃e (owner(e)=c and Occurs(e,s)).
```

The displayed definition distinguishes the person, an episode and a condition. It does not identify the person with the episode or say that consciousness itself is produced whenever HasContent becomes true. Further predicates and arguments must decide that.

A **syntactic category** turns formulas-in-context into objects and provably functional relations into arrows. The formula HasContent(c,s), with its displayed variables and sorts, is such a definable object. A formula describing ownership gives a definable map only when the theory proves its required existence and uniqueness. Composition combines definable relations and quantifies over the intermediate variables. Thus category structure can be built from an actual language and its inferences, rather than from an artist's choice of diagram.

Here **geometric logic** permits finite conjunctions, arbitrary set-indexed disjunctions and existential quantification in formulas. Its axioms are implications between such formulas, with the displayed free variables universally understood. Equality and the given predicates supply the atomic formulas. Falsehood is the empty disjunction, so an incompatibility axiom can say that a conjunction implies falsehood. This does not make every nested universal or negated statement geometric. A coherent theory uses only finite disjunctions, giving a useful smaller fragment.

For a geometric theory T, its **classifying topos** B_T has a universal T-model. Giving a T-model in another Grothendieck topos E corresponds, naturally, to giving a geometric morphism E→B_T: the inverse-image part takes the universal model to the specified model in E. This is a universal property about models in varying environments, not just a large set containing all ordinary models.

A geometric morphism f:E→B_T consists of an inverse-image functor f⁎:B_T→E preserving finite limits, with a right adjoint, conventionally written `f_*`. Thus `f⁎ ⊣ f_*`. The inverse-image functor points opposite the geometric morphism, as inverse images of subsets point opposite an ordinary function. Its finite-limit and left-adjoint structure preserve the operations needed for geometric reasoning. We will not treat an arbitrary functor as such a morphism.

Equivalence of classifying topoi is a strong mathematical comparison, often called Morita equivalence of the geometric theories. It makes their model descriptions correspond across these environments, with the relevant structural compatibility. It is much stronger than finding one common finite model or one shared consequence. Neither the existence of B_T nor its equivalence to another classifying topos supplies a missing historical interpretation of the predicates. [Source](cite://olivia-caramello/topos-theoretic-preliminaries/category-caramello)

The distinction matters for Navya-Nyāya. Some source assertions quantify over every time, use negation, or specify absence with tightly constrained delimiters. One cannot drop those operations merely because a conveniently chosen geometric fragment handles positive conjunctions and existential witnesses easily. We can retain a fuller first-order theory and name the fragment being compared, or justify an appropriate reformulation. In either case the omitted content must remain visible.

What, then, is the space of this proposed integration? At present it includes several legitimate constructions: a category of specified theories and interpretations; categories of their models; shared fragments with restriction maps; and presheaf or sheaf categories built on a justified context category. None has yet been shown to be the unique complete mathematical image of the traditions' ultimate reality. Their present value is more concrete: each makes a different integration demand testable, and each exposes different ways in which a tempting synthesis can lose something essential.


## Integration without ranking the traditions

The desired integration can now be stated more strongly than “all these accounts fit in a diagram.” Suppose T₁, T₂ and T₃ are selected source-grounded theories: each names an explicitly specified fragment, not an entire historical tradition. Seek a common theory U together with interpretations of these selected theories in U. On models, these become reduction functors:

```
                 Mod(U)
               ↙   ↓   ↘
        Mod(T₁) Mod(T₂) Mod(T₃).
```

This diagram does not rank T₁ above T₂ or require T₃ to be a historically later correction of both. The arrows ask how one structured model presents itself under different abstractions. Different target vocabularies may disclose different relations. The universal theory U, if established, would be a shared explanatory proposal; its position in the picture does not make one historical school its privileged interpreter.

There are several possible strengths of this claim. One U-model with the required reductions establishes a simultaneous realization of the selected theories. Proving every model of a selected Tᵢ has a suitable expansion establishes a stronger coverage result. Showing how compatible families of local models recover a U-model, uniquely up to the relevant isomorphism, adds a descent or reconstruction result. Proving equivalences of suitable model categories, with their logical structure respected, is stronger again. These demands are not interchangeable.

Nor must a successful abstraction be an equivalence. A description of a devotional disposition and a description of the occurrence of a cognition can omit different information about one life. Neither need recover the whole life by itself. The important question is whether the omitted distinctions remain available elsewhere in the common structure and whether each description's actual claims remain true under its interpretation.

This is a constructive way to pursue the thesis of different emphases on one reality. It puts the burden on explicit interpretations, shared referents and preserved inferences. If two theories assert opposite conclusions using genuinely identical meanings and conditions, their unchanged union has no consistent model. A contextual solution must therefore demonstrate the difference of meanings or conditions from the texts; adding a school-name index solely to evade the conflict would not explain it.

Vijñānabhikṣu is an important precedent for coordinating different disciplines, but his selected argument also corrects a lower teaching's claim to finality. That historical feature need not determine the architecture of the proposed new Vedānta. We can adopt his demand for substantive integration while testing a symmetric family of abstractions. The research must say when it follows his particular argument and when it proposes a different relation among the traditions. [Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/vijnanamrta-bhasya/category-bhiksu-self)

## A shared model with more than a common endpoint

The strongest construction reached in the present study joins two substantial cognitive mechanisms. One interface, K_A, retains the selected Śaṅkara account of changing mental presentation, invariant awareness, two directions of misattribution, ordinary truth, dream and doubt. Another, K_R, retains the selected Trika account of receivers, admitted aspects, operative conditions, displayed content, intrinsic self-manifestation and recognition. The proposed theory U has both interfaces.

The comparison has this shape at the level of models:

```
Mod(T_Śaṅkara) → Mod(K_A) ← Mod(U)
                              │
                              ↓
                          Mod(K_R) ← Mod(T_Trika).
```

An object of the corresponding compatible-model construction consists of three models and specified isomorphisms between their interface reductions. Using isomorphisms rather than literal equality allows harmless differences of names or carrier sets. This version is called a **pseudopullback**. An arrow between two such compatible triples consists of model maps that commute with the specified interface identifications. These conditions make compatibility stable under the chosen maps; a collection of similar-looking models would not suffice.

Here is the core of the actual common witness. There are two local persons i and j, one proposed constitutive self-reference u, and eight conditions. For i, q is temporary objectless quiet; v is a contentful condition of restricted awareness; r retains valid recognition; d retains that recognition in devotional expression. The remaining conditions distinguish initial binding identification, dream, doubt and ordinary veridical cognition. The mathematical names are condition labels, not stages that every historical author is claimed to enumerate.

There is one dated episode for each person-condition pair except (i,q). Thus there are fifteen episodes. Their owner and date functions are exactly the two coordinates. Current first-person participation is given by:

```
Access(c,e,s) ⇔ owner(e)=c and date(e)=s.
HasContent(c,s) ⇔ there is e with Access(c,e,s).
SelfShining(u,s) holds in every condition s.
Recognized(i,r) and Recognized(i,d) hold.
Recognized(i,q) and Recognized(i,v) do not hold.
```

Here Recognized names the shared core of valid recognition as oneself. On the Trika side, the interpretation represents that core through the richer full-recognition predicate. Its absence at v does not say that the restricted awareness of vijñānākala is blank or simply erroneous. The model distinguishes that awareness from the fuller disclosure of agency.

These clauses make several philosophical decisions inspectable. Intrinsic self-manifestation persists without a local episode at q. Quiet does not itself supply valid recognition. Restricted awareness v is not silently identified with quiet. Current participation in an episode remains local even though ultimate self-reference is common. Knowing about another person's episode can be represented by a further episode owned by the knower; it does not require transferring ownership of the original episode.

The next module supplies a presentation mechanism. Every local person has a receiving configuration. A receiver admits certain aspects, and a display occurs when an admitted aspect also has an operative condition:

```
Displayed(h,z,s) ⇔ Admits(h,z) and Operative(h,z,s).
Occurs(e,s) implies
  Displayed(receiver(owner(e)), contentRole(e), s).
```

Complete intrinsic inclusion is distinguished from the restricted admission of a local receiver. The same presentation role can occur before and after correction while the attribution of independent existence is removed. Another layer retains distinct intentional objects and medium-dependent features, so a dream chariot is not made an externally present pot merely because both count as presentations. The common content-role abstraction is deliberately coarser than that object layer.

One can now describe what each source comparison preserves. On the Advaita side, the map retains the medium/feature relation and both mistaken transfers: attributing changing mental activity intrinsically to awareness, and attributing awareness intrinsically to the instrument. It also retains distinct ordinary truth, dream and doubt judgments. On the Trika side, the map retains receiver-specific admission, operative display, local registration, intrinsic inclusion and the correction of independent-existence attribution. The Trika companion also has a same-owner memory pair and its integrating subject; that memory structure is additional to the common interface, not something erased and then falsely reported as preserved by it. [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection) [Source](cite://abhinavagupta/tantraloka/category-trika-receiver) [Source](cite://utpaladeva/isvara-pratyabhijna-karika/category-trika-reflexivity)

The decisive point is that both comparisons use the **same** fifteen-episode U-model. Two different enrichments, each compatible with only one source, would not establish simultaneous compatibility. In the checked construction the designated interface carriers and functions correspond, and every selected predicate has the same extension after reduction. The finite witness and the interpretation proofs were reviewed separately: the witness demonstrates possibility, while the proofs establish that the declared interface axioms survive the translations in every model of the relevant target theories.

The displayed clauses teach the central mechanism; they are not a replacement for every axiom in either philosopher's complete account. In particular, the comparison leaves the strongest opposition between intrinsic nonagency and intrinsic formative freedom outside the shared interface. The fullness-closure module below is connected by a separate, conditional refinement of this same model; that connection requires additional premises and does not follow from episode ownership alone. The positive achievement is a simultaneous model of the selected reflection and recognition mechanisms, with meaningful nonbinding devotion and restricted experience. That is a stronger beginning for nonhierarchical integration than one undifferentiated endpoint, while leaving the remaining philosophical tests explicit.


## Fullness as a mathematical question

Abhinavagupta does not begin by declaring that every thing contains every other thing. He asks what must be included in a complete account of a particular manifestation. Earth has a qualitative constitution; its account therefore leads through subtle qualities, sensory articulation and determination, intellect and the undivided root of nature. Nature as experienced requires a conscious experiencer. A contracted experiencer requires an account both of consciousness and of the power through which contraction occurs. That power cannot be exhausted by the contracted result if it is to explain the possibility of contraction and expansion. The inquiry consequently reaches free, complete consciousness. These are the premises of Abhinavagupta's argument, not claims established by modern physics.

This route is asymmetric. Being a śiṃśapā tree requires being a tree, while being a tree does not require being a śiṃśapā. Likewise, showing that a manifestation requires its constituents does not yet show that every constituent contains every manifestation. Abhinavagupta supplies a separate operation: complete apprehension of an earlier principle retains the completed field of the later one. His example moves from earth to water without abandoning the earth-field already apprehended, then continues through the articulation. The claim is stronger than remembering an arbitrary image: complete apprehension corresponds to a fullness intrinsically present in consciousness. These two arguments—constitutive prerequisite and retained fullness—must be represented separately before they can jointly support an all-in-all conclusion.

[Source](cite://abhinavagupta/paratrimsika-vivarana/category-trika-fullness)


The same discipline can make the fullness proposal more exact. Suppose three determinations are called a, b and c. One source-supported relation says that a determination requires another for its constitution. A second says that complete apprehension retains a further determination. Draw these as different labelled arrows. The distinction matters: dependence on a prerequisite is not already a theorem that the prerequisite contains its dependent.

For a small example, let the prerequisite arrows be c→b and b→a, and the retention arrows be a→b and b→c. Define Cl(A) to be the set of determinations reachable from the starting set A by any finite sequence of these arrows, including an empty sequence. Then:

```
A ⊆ Cl(A)
A ⊆ B implies Cl(A) ⊆ Cl(B)
Cl(Cl(A)) = Cl(A)
```

These are extensivity, monotonicity and idempotence. Together they define a closure operator. In this graph Cl({a}) = Cl({b}) = Cl({c}) = {a,b,c}. The starting determinations remain distinct; their completed constitutive accounts coincide.

To use this as an account of what each determination contains, one further rule is indispensable: if its complete content includes y, it inherits the prerequisite and retention content of y. Merely including adjacent items does not entail including everything reachable. For example, a could initially include only a and b, while b includes all three. Without the inheritance rule, nothing forces a to include c. The reviewed fullness argument explicitly supplies the stronger rule. Adjacent inclusions alone do not establish inclusion of the whole field.

A free path category on the labelled arrows retains the different explanations. The path a→b→a is not the identity at a merely because it returns there. This is the same distinction encountered in the opening exercise about practice paths. Collapsing every reachable route into an unlabelled relation loses explanatory information.

There is also an adjunction. Order subsets by inclusion, and let Closed be the subsets unchanged by Cl. For a closed B,

```
Cl(A) ⊆ B  exactly when  A ⊆ B.
```

Thus closure is left adjoint to the inclusion of Closed into all subsets. This is a general mathematical result for the specified closure operator. The philosophical work lies in defending the arrows and inheritance rule. Abhinavagupta's constitutive-inclusion argument supplies the relevant distinction between prerequisites and retention under complete apprehension. The simple three-item graph illustrates the mechanism; it is not a verified enumeration of all tattvas. [Source](cite://abhinavagupta/paratrimsika-vivarana/category-trika-fullness)

Constitutive fullness also differs from a finite mind's current access. A determination can require the whole for a complete account without the person experiencing it knowing every fact. The latter conclusion would need an additional access rule. This is how the proposed integration can retain restricted experience without making its ultimate reality a fragment cut out of completeness.

## Connecting fullness to a particular experience

A fullness graph and a model of experience can each be consistent while saying nothing about one another. To connect them, we must specify which determination a presentation concerns and how that presentation belongs to an actual episode. We must also keep two questions separate: what a complete account of the presented determination includes, and what its finite experiencer presently apprehends.

Abhinavagupta supplies a reason for the first connection. In the *Parātriṃśikāvivaraṇa*, an apprehension of earth counts as complete only if it discloses earth's entire constitution. The argument then follows its indispensable constituents and their own grounds. This conditional does not say that anyone who sees a patch of ground has already realized that complete account. The earth–water discussion supplies a small, source-based test: earth requires water in this elemental explanation; the complete apprehension of water retains the completed earth-field. These are the author's two differently warranted premises, not conclusions about modern chemistry. [Source](cite://abhinavagupta/paratrimsika-vivarana/category-trika-fullness)

The receiver account prevents an invalid further step. Jayaratha argues that a reflected mountain does not make the mirror heavy. A reflection's support, its presented aspects and the means by which it is known have different roles. Full constitutive inclusion therefore cannot mean that every included property becomes locally instantiated in the receiving surface or mind. [Source](cite://abhinavagupta/tantraloka/category-trika-receiver)

Keep the earlier fifteen-episode model unchanged. Add a separate determination type T containing W and Earth. These are the selected water and earth principles; T is not a complete tattva taxonomy. Its two labelled arrows are:

```
Need(Earth,W)       Retain(W,Earth).
```

Self-inclusion and the two inheritance rules give each determination the full selected profile {W,Earth}. The two elements remain distinct. The preceding closure lesson explains why these two differently warranted arrows are needed and why a larger graph also needs a checked account of its paths and coverage.

Now add `Head(e,t)`: the proposed refinement assigns t as episode e's locally explicit operative determination. This includes an actual local-articulation claim; it is not merely an analyst's note about a hidden constituent. It also does not mean that the episode completely apprehends t. Let `content(e)` remain the episode's old coarse presentation role. Two incidence relations connect the new and old types:

```
Head(e,t) implies RoleHead(t,content(e)).
RoleHead(t,f) implies RoleContent(f,t).
RoleContent(f,t) and In(t,x) imply RoleContent(f,x).
```

Here RoleContent records the complete constitutional account associated with a role's selected determination. It does not say that the role is a physical lump of that element. In particular, neither the persons nor their person-role forms have been renamed as tattvas. Multiple determinations can be associated with the same coarse presentation role.

Define the episode's constitutive profile by both parts of the connection:

```
Profile(e,t) holds exactly when there is x with
  Head(e,x), In(x,t), and RoleContent(content(e),t).
```

The proof now crosses the two modules. Suppose an episode has head W. Closure gives In(W,Earth). The first two incidence rules give RoleContent(content(e),W); the third gives RoleContent(content(e),Earth). These facts yield Profile(e,Earth). The profile belongs to this episode's actual content role. Its intrinsic and reflexive inclusion use that role's existing relation to the intrinsic subject. When the episode occurs, the existing registration rules place the presentation in its actual owner's receiver. None of these steps changes its owner or transfers another person's experience to it.

To see why the bridge matters, remove only the first incidence rule. Keep the old reflection model, the complete two-element inclusion relation and a selected head. We can now leave RoleHead and RoleContent empty, making Profile empty too. Those remaining premises do not prove the joined profile. Merely putting the two models side by side would leave precisely this gap.

A concrete expansion uses two different existing episodes. As an illustrative proposal, assign a water head to one otherwise unspecified episode and an earth head to the episode in which a pot is presented but its existence is doubted. This second assignment means an earth articulation within that presentation, not an equation of the intentional pot with the earth principle. Neither assignment is established by the old model or identified as a particular event in the source. They are explicit premises for testing whether this refinement can coexist with the already modeled experiences.

In that expanded witness, each of the two episodes has both selected constituents in its complete profile. Yet local explicit articulation follows only its assigned head and current access:

```
Explicit(c,t,s) holds exactly when there is e with
  Access(c,e,s) and Head(e,t).
```

There are four episode–constituent profile pairs, but only two selected local-articulation triples. The water-headed episode can therefore have Earth in its complete profile without its owner explicitly apprehending Earth at that moment. The original fifteen episodes and sixty-three displays remain unchanged. Temporary quiet gains no new episode; the dream chariot gains no external existence. Full constitution has not become finite omniscience or indiscriminate property transfer.

Categorically, retain pairs `(determination, role)` with RoleContent incidence. An arrow within one role is a labelled prerequisite/retention path between its determinations. Closure ensures that intermediate determinations stay in that role's profile; identities and composition are empty paths and concatenation. Projection to the role forgets the distinction between its determinations, while projection to the path category retains their distinct heads and reasons for inclusion. In the witness, the shared presentation-role fibre—the objects with that role—has two objects and both labelled directions. A two-edge cycle is not an identity, so equal complete profiles do not make those objects isomorphic in this free path category.

This is an **expansion** of the same model, not a replacement: forgetting the added data returns exactly the old model and its two reviewed school-interface comparisons. More generally, the construction provides an expansion of every set-based model of the old selected theory, using the two marked episodes and their actual content values. An old-language assertion cannot become newly forced if every old countermodel survives this expansion. This is model-conservativity, a preservation result. It does not establish unique refinements, equivalence of the two theories, or validity of the proposed episode assignments as historical interpretations.

The advance is consequently specific: fullness and reflection now admit a connected, nonempty common model with a proved constitutive-profile consequence. The connection is conditional on its declared refinement premises. It still requires source-justified assignments for a complete tattva account, a separate theory of actually attaining complete apprehension, and an argument about intrinsic nonagency versus formative freedom. The existing Śaṅkara interface does not silently acquire every new Trika premise merely because its original cognitive distinctions survive.


## What the proposed integrated Vedānta asserts

The constructive proposal has five commitments. Intrinsic self-manifestation is not produced by a cognitive episode. Finite lives have real, conditioned structures. Their ultimate Self is not exhausted by the conditions distinguishing those lives. Manifestation belongs to the reality's own power rather than an independently supplied second source. Recognition can remove binding appropriation while leaving the possibility of meaningful activity and love.

This proposal says more than the common disclosure fragment proves. In particular, the claim of one constitutive Self across first-person lives requires an argument about individuation and recognition. It cannot be obtained merely by setting a function equal to a constant. Likewise, intrinsic formative power needs an account of how expression is compatible with the absence of the limiting alteration rejected by inactive-self arguments.

Bhikṣu supplies a precedent for preserving an achieved discrimination while correcting its claim to finality. Aurobindo supplies a related demand: a fuller realization must retain the earlier freedom while explaining its relation to manifestation. He also insists that affirming all things as Saccidānanda does not yet explain why this determinate world appears. A law of manifestation is needed. The present proposal inherits these problems as well as their positive insights. [Source](cite://vijnanabhiksu/vijnanamrta-bhasya/category-bhiksu-self) [Source](cite://aurobindo/life-divine/category-aurobindo)

Three possible outcomes of comparison should remain visible. A claim can survive a faithful interpretation. An apparent conflict can disappear when a textually justified distinction of meaning or context is restored. Or the integrated theory can revise a source's claim. The last is a philosophical choice to defend, not a failure merely because it is a choice. What would fail is to revise the claim while reporting that everything was preserved unchanged.

Aurobindo's argument begins with more than self-presence. There is determinate manifestation, and no independent power outside the infinite is available to account for it. Its possibility must therefore belong to the infinite's own power. Being unlimited cannot mean being unable to manifest; it means that a particular manifestation neither exhausts nor binds the being that manifests. Silent freedom remains a real and essential realization. The disputed further claim is that dynamic realization discloses the same reality's own power rather than a merely provisional activity attributed to it.

This makes the premise that a rival can reject visible. Self-presence alone does not entail universal formative agency. One must also defend the reality of manifestation, the absence of an independent outside, and the equal revelatory force of the dynamic realization. Aurobindo then asks a further question: even if all is Saccidānanda, why is manifestation articulated in this determinate way? A law of manifestation is still needed. A categorical integration that preserves only a common ultimate object would leave that explanatory task untouched.

[Source](cite://aurobindo/life-divine/category-aurobindo)

Śaṅkara's agency objection makes one remaining task especially precise. At *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* 2.3.40 an opponent proposes that liberation could occur while the capacity for agency remains, provided its effects cease—like fire without fuel. Śaṅkara rejects this as securing permanent freedom while that capacity remains linked with possible occasions of suffering. His solution assigns agency through superimposed conditions and recognizes the intrinsically free self. An integrated account of divine freedom must therefore explain why its proposed formative activity is intrinsically nonbinding, rather than merely inactive for a time. [Source](cite://shankara/brahma-sutra-bhasya/category-shankara-agency)

Trika and Aurobindo offer a positive account to investigate here: expression need not exhaust or alter consciousness's essential nature. Whether this answers Śaṅkara depends on what kind of agency and dependence is retained, not on the word “power” alone. Śaṅkara himself teaches through infinite power in *Upadeśasāhasrī* II1.18–19 and subsequently rescinds the explanatory division in II1.41–43. Both moments belong to his account. An integration must explain their relation before promoting the provisional power teaching into a final doctrine of intrinsic activity. [Source](cite://utpaladeva/isvara-pratyabhijna-karika/category-trika-reflexivity) [Source](cite://aurobindo/life-divine/category-aurobindo) [Source](cite://shankara/upadesa-sahasri/category-self-reflection)


## The next constructions

The next work is organized around specific arguments, not an indefinite instruction to read more books.

1. **Reference and individual selfhood.** Carry the derived Rāmānuja–Nimbārka naming rule into the proposed integrative theory, preserving its qualifying grounds and each account’s liberated individuality. The selected reference derivation is now established; a map into the larger common theory and mutual recovery of the schools’ explanatory vocabulary are further obligations.
2. **Agency and participation.** Map Bhāskara's real conditioned action, Madhva's dependent agency, Baladeva's nonbinding action and Trika's intrinsic freedom with their different conditions intact. Use ownership, self-recognition and reserved divine governance as tests that can reject a misleading translation.
3. **Ignorance and correction.** Build separate accounts of locus, object, individuation, corrective cognition and residual conditions for the later Advaitins. Compare the strongest positive-ignorance explanation with Saccidānandendra's actual denial in the same register. Matching beginning and end states alone is insufficient.
4. **Fullness and manifestation.** Extend the reviewed two-determination join with source-justified episode refinements and a verified tattva coverage map. Then test Vallabha's distinct manifestation profiles, grace and routes of fulfillment. The conditional join preserves the old reflection model; full taxonomy and actual complete apprehension remain further obligations.
5. **Practice and fulfilled life.** Relate temporary quiet, discriminative knowledge, irreversible correction, continued embodiment and devotion. Preserve Madhusūdana's developmental sequence, Ramaṇa's account of abidance and the distinct postknowledge accounts of Ramakrishna and Vivekananda.

For each construction the deliverable is a connected primary argument, a typed theory, an explicit translation or counterexample, and an independent review asking whether an important source mechanism has been omitted. The [reading and construction plan by thinker](https://vedanta.eeshan.xyz/#/article/vedanta-category-research-plan) records the examined loci, existing formal start and next test separately for the individual authors and traditions. These are plans for arguments that remain to be established, alongside results already obtained.

A useful test of understanding is to revisit one passage and say exactly what its mathematical reconstruction retains. For the absence argument, retain the counterpositive, locus, relation, delimiter and order. For reflection, retain the medium, presentation, self-manifestation and misattribution. For fullness, identify the prerequisite, retention and inheritance rules. When those answers are explicit, the mathematics has begun to explain the comparison rather than merely decorate it.
