# Comparative encyclopedia migration contract

## Current state

The current primitive graph maps thinkers and schools onto a fixed set of
analytic axes. It preserves useful evidence and distinctions, but a tuple or
geometric display can overstate commensurability and detach a claim from the
text, author, historical context, and rival it addresses.

## Freeze rule

Do not add new primitive axes, values, visual geometry, or broad school-level
assignments while the replacement abstraction is being designed. Existing
primitive records remain a legacy analytical aid and must retain their source
links during migration.

## Replacement target

The replacement is claim-centered rather than tuple-centered. Each comparison
must be able to answer:

1. What exact claim is at issue?
2. Who makes it, in which work and locus?
3. What is the claim's register and scope?
4. What is the compared claim, and where is it made?
5. Is the relation agreement, direct disagreement, scope/register difference,
   distinct questions, or unresolved?
6. What evidence supports the verdict and what remains uncertain?

The model may later offer visual exploration, but no visual device may imply
that two traditions have identical questions, that all doctrines fit a shared
coordinate system, or that a source-backed disagreement is a mere symbolic
variant.

## Data preservation

Before retiring any primitive record, preserve its thinker/work/locus links,
confidence, explanatory note, and any comparison it informed. A migration map
must identify whether each legacy record becomes a claim, a source note, an
editorial hypothesis, or an explicitly retired abstraction.

## User-supplied abstraction checkpoint

The replacement design needs the owner's proposed abstraction, including one
example entry, one comparison, intended reader task, and the relation between
philosophical claims and visual form. Until then, only source/claim cleanup and
legacy-data preservation are authorized.
