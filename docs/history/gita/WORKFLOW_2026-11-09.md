# Gītā translation workflow (lead spec, 2026-11-09, rev. 3)

The owner's requirement: for each word, know what it means and what it signifies; render it by
an English word that carries both, else by a new English word, else keep the Sanskrit; write the
line keeping the Sanskrit's rhythm and grammar (Sri Aurobindo); add nothing; where two readings
cannot be held in one line, give a second line. The model makes these judgements IN CONTEXT; code
gathers the evidence and enforces every rule that can be checked mechanically.

## Audit of the lexicon-first design (rev. 2) — what failed and why

Evidence: ch12, three full runs (≈650 calls, ≈25 min each) against the first one-call-per-verse
test (20 calls, 4 min; recorded in private working notes). The one-call test
gave better English ("burns not with grief", "bright-pure", "seated apart", "unbound") and failed
only in two mechanically checkable ways: additions from commentary and inconsistency across verses.

| # | Failure seen | Root cause (class) | Unified fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Odd words: "opposer", "bristling of hair", "stiff-necked pride", "mentally disciplined person" | Words decided OUT OF CONTEXT, by a code score over marks | Word chosen in its sentence by the model under the owner's order; code checks the order's conditions |

## Design

Stage 1 — PARSE (unchanged; `drive.py construe`): per verse, word split, analysis, case, relation and
head, compound type, members and vigraha, referents, grammatical forks; agentic, reads the 16
commentaries; reviewed for grammar only.

Stage 2 — TRANSLATE, per sentence unit, = ENGLISH_CRAFT.md §2 executed (`pipeline.py translate`):
- Step 1, MODEL CALL "meaning": Sanskrit sources only (no Sanskrit–English dictionary) — per word
  the Dhātupāṭha artha, Amarakośa, Śabdakalpadruma/Vācaspatya, every commentator's gloss at this
  verse, the Gītā's own usage, the parse and the owner's term rows. Returns per word LITERAL
  (what the word itself says: root and suffix in the sense used, active root image, grammatical form,
  compound relation), HERE (what commentators say it refers to or implies — evidence only, never in
  the line), readings (forks), a ≤6-word search phrase, and remembered English candidates.
- Step 2, CODE (`english_bench.py`, the English workbench): English found by that meaning — semantic
  search by the step-1 core meaning (≤6 words; never the full description, whose grammar words pollute
  the search) over 872k definitions (OEWN, Webster 1913, Wiktionary; qwen3-embedding, brute-force cosine,
  ~30 ms), keyword search of the seven dictionaries, the remembered words, English words sharing the
  root (PIE, last); ≤10 candidates per word, each with the sense that matched and its modern
  first sense (for the connotation check), current frequency (wordfreq Zipf), stress pattern
  (CMUdict, Wiktionary IPA, compound fallback), origin (native / Latin / French … from the etymology table), and for phrases
  their frequency in English books (Google Books Ngram, cached).
- Steps 3–6 and the line pass, MODEL CALL "translate": the prompt carries ENGLISH_CRAFT §2 steps 3–6,
  its five principles and standard §7.1–§7.16 VERBATIM (read from the files at run time, never
  paraphrased). Returns the line(s) and per word: English, kind (a exact / b uncommon real / c
  construction / d formation / e Sanskrit kept), found_by, carried/lost, why (vs the best rejected).
- CODE CHECKS (unit-tested): TRACE (every content word of a rendering comes from the word's
  candidates, its literal meaning, a standing or seed decision, or a fixed rendering); coverage; no content word without a Sanskrit source (repetition for
  English ellipsis allowed); a word's English bounded by its morphemes; term rows (a lemma's rows are
  its senses: admitted by one row is right; forbidden only when no row admits; owner rows from
  term_policy.json bind; where none exists, the plan's §4 seeds bind unless challenged); compound members
  rendered; every content word a real English word; kind e is the Sanskrit word; a formation names two
  models, one per line; no '?' without a Sanskrit interrogative (§7.8); one dash (L7); no 'of'-chain
  of three (L3); no hybrid compound or bare 'whatsoever' (§7.16); FIXED renderings used.
- Code also runs LanguageTool (local) on every line: grammar findings are code errors.
- REVIEW CALL gets MEASURED facts per line (clause endings, 'of' runs, sibilants, consonant
  collisions, dashes, register-tagged words, syllables, grammar findings); severities S1/S2/S3 by
  ENGLISH_CRAFT §4H and §7; S1/S2 and code errors go back for revision
  (at most two rounds, each re-checked and re-reviewed); S3 is recorded, not forced.

Stage 3 — CONSISTENCY, per chapter (`pipeline.py consistency`): code groups every rendering by
lemma; a lemma rendered two ways (by English stem), and two lemmas rendered by one English word
(§7.6: near-synonyms stay distinct), form groups; each group (a member decided together with its
compound) gets one call that sees every place with the word's literal meaning there and prefers a
rendering already used if it meets the standard; it returns the English per lemma and sense with the
places of each, applied as fixed renderings to whole words and to compound members; code reruns only the units whose rendering changed (reusing their step-1 meaning) and reports
compliance: decided placements that now use the decided English. Owner decisions then go into `term_policy.json` and win everywhere.

Owner sheet (`pipeline.py sheet`): kept-Sanskrit words, new words, words whose signification is
lost with the reason, consistency decisions — the rows the owner rules on.

Cost: per unit 1 meaning + 1 translate + 1 review + ≤2 (revision + re-review); plus lemmas in conflict.
Dev chapter: 12 (its lines appear as examples in ENGLISH_CRAFT §3, so it is not a clean test); held-out: 15.
References (evaluation only, never in prompts): internal/gita_pipeline/gold/ch12.md, ch15.md; `compare.py`.
Runtime: the pipeline runs in prakriya/tmp/english/benchenv (wordfreq, pronouncing, ollama, language-tool-python).
